# Campaign Bible — The Salt Below

**STATUS: concept approved.** Selected from `pipeline/CONCEPT_PITCHES.md`
(picked by the DM's delegate, not the pipeline itself, per GROUND_RULES.md
Rule 9 — this is the one and only time a human-equivalent judgment call

the pipeline's to make autonomously). Why this pitch over the other two:
it has a natural escalating-depth structure (surface city → shallow dives →
deep ruins → the old kingdom itself) that maps cleanly onto how
curse-of-strahd already proved out a bounded-but-deep location structure at
250+ locations in this same repo — the most direct precedent for hitting
100+ locations without the setting feeling padded. It's also thematically
unclaimed by the other four campaigns (no aquatic/nautical entry yet).

## Premise
Port Calder is a boomtown built on the ribs of a kingdom that drowned three
centuries ago. Divers and salvage-mages haul up relics, coin, and the
occasional still-breathing thing from the reef-maze below the harbor, and the
city runs on that trade — brash, prosperous, riotously multicultural, equal
parts fishing town and gold rush. But the deeper salvage crews go, the more
they find that the old kingdom didn't drown by accident, and that whatever
pulled it under is still down there, still hungry, and has started sending
things up to see who's been digging.

## Tone
Swashbuckling boomtown adventure — tavern brawls, diving contracts, guild
rivalries, salt spray and street food — that turns into cosmic-horror dread
the deeper the party goes. Light and loud above the waterline, cold and alien
below it. Keep early-game sessions (surface, shallow dives) leaning toward
the adventurous/mercantile register; let dread creep in gradually as depth
increases, never flipping to full grimdark even at the bottom — the horror is
alien and patient, not gratuitous.

## Factions
- **The Salvage Concordat** — the merchant guild that licenses and taxes all
  diving; wants the boom to keep booming and actively covers up bad finds.
  Political antagonist more than a villain — protects its own profits, not
  malicious for its own sake.
- **The Drowned Choir** — a dockside cult of the old kingdom's remaining
  faithful, convinced the sunken city can be raised again — and half right.
  Best used as a faction whose goals sometimes align with the party's and
  sometimes don't; not simple to dismiss as "the bad guys."
- **Harbor Watch** — the city's law, chronically outmatched by what's really
  down there, trying to hold the boomtown together with too few resources.
  A faction the party can genuinely ally with, not just deal with.
- **The Freedivers** — an informal network of independent salvagers (the
  PCs' likely "home" faction) who actually respect what's below and trade in
  warnings as much as loot. Default entry point for new PCs into the setting.
- **It That Kept the Kingdom** — not a political faction but a presence with
  agents, wants, and a plan for the surface. Should almost never appear
  directly early on; presence is felt through what it sends up, not itself.

## Central mystery
Why did the old kingdom really drown, what deal keeps "It" from simply rising
now, and how much longer does Port Calder have before someone's greed finally
breaks that deal?

## Level arc
1 → 10. Roughly: levels 1-3 surface Port Calder + shallow reef work
(establish the city, factions, and stakes); 4-6 mid-depth salvage sites and
first real contact with the Drowned Choir and the kingdom's remnants; 7-10
the deep ruins and the old kingdom itself, culminating in a decision about
"It" (destroy the containing deal, renew it, or something the party
invents).

## Geography — sized for 100+ locations
- **Port Calder** (surface hub, like Phandalin/Village of Barovia in the
  other campaigns): harbor district, guild halls, market quarter, the
  Concordat's licensing house, dive-outfitter row, several taverns/inns,
  residential wards, Harbor Watch garrison. Aim for 15-20 named locations
  here alone.
- **The Shallows** (levels 1-4 dive sites): reef formations, wrecked ships,
  shallow kingdom outskirts — sized for exploration and early salvage
  contracts. Aim for 20-30 locations.
- **The Drowned Districts** (levels 4-7): the old kingdom's mid-depth
  neighborhoods, half-collapsed, Drowned Choir territory. Aim for 25-35
  locations.
- **The Deep Kingdom** (levels 7-10): the old capital itself and the
  containment site of "It." Aim for 25-35 locations, including the
  climactic dungeon.
- Total target: 100-120+ locations across all four regions, comparable to
  descent-into-avernus's density.

## Naming conventions
Surface Port Calder: salt-of-the-earth, working-harbor names (guild names,
practical street names — "Dive-Outfitter Row," "the Licensing House"). The
old drowned kingdom: more formal/archaic naming to contrast — a fallen
aristocracy's naming conventions (titled districts, old-tongue place names).
Keep the contrast consistent — it reinforces the tonal shift as parties go
deeper.
