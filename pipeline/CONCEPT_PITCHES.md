# Concept Pitches — Fifth Campaign

Three distinct concepts for DM approval. Pick one and replace the placeholder
in `pipeline/CAMPAIGN_BIBLE.md` with the chosen concept's details (or ask for
a redo — see that file's instructions). Each is scaled to eventually reach

## Pitch 1 — The Long Autumn

**Working title:** The Long Autumn

**Premise:** Nine years ago, the seasons stopped turning in the vale of
Ambergast. It has been harvest-time ever since — mild days, blood-orange
sunsets, orchards forever heavy with fruit that never rots. The locals built
a whole culture around the endless autumn: it's harvest festival every week,
markets overflow, and nobody starves. But the vale is slowly filling in from
the edges — a spreading, silvering frost-line that turns anyone who lingers
in it into unmoving, faintly-smiling statues wreathed in dead leaves — and
the deeper into the countryside you go, the more the "harvest" starts to
look like something being gathered, not grown. The player characters arrive
(or were born into) a world that looks cozy on a postcard and is quietly
running out of time.

**Tone:** Pastoral-cozy on the surface, fey-bargain horror underneath. Warm
colors, harvest markets, and unsettlingly polite villagers who've stopped
asking questions, cut with genuine dread the further out you travel. Balance
target: light and welcoming near the town center, darker and stranger toward
the frost-line and the deep orchards.

**Factions:**
- **The Harvest Table** — the town council/priesthood that keeps the festival
  running; genuinely believes maintaining the ritual calendar is the only
  thing holding back the frost, and will suppress anyone who says otherwise.
- **The Gleaners** — a folk-hero vigilante band who sneak past the frost-line
  to rescue people about to be "harvested"; short-handed, burning out fast.
  underestimated, and desperate to unify the ex-Wardens.
- **The Court of the First Frost** — the fey power responsible, which isn't
  malicious so much as keeping a bargain it made with the vale's founders
  centuries ago, exactly and mercilessly to the letter.
- **The Silvering** — the statues themselves, not dead but dreaming; some
  cultists believe they can be woken and worship the process as ascension.
- **The Wayfarer's Guild** — outside merchants and treasure-hunters drawn by
  rumors of a vale where nothing decays, mostly oblivious to the danger,
  a useful source of outside pressure and misinformation.

**Central mystery:** What was the original bargain, who struck it, and what
happens when the frost finally reaches the town center — is it possible to
end the autumn without breaking the promise that's kept the vale fed and
safe for nine years?

**Level arc:** 1 → 11.

## Pitch 2 — The Salt Below

**Working title:** The Salt Below

**Premise:** Port Calder is a boomtown built on the ribs of a kingdom that
drowned three centuries ago. Divers and salvage-mages haul up relics, coin,
and the occasional still-breathing thing from the reef-maze below the
harbor, and the city runs on that trade — brash, prosperous, riotously
multicultural, equal parts fishing town and gold rush. But the deeper salvage
crews go, the more they find that the old kingdom didn't drown by accident,
and that whatever pulled it under is still down there, still hungry, and has
started sending things up to see who's been digging.

**Tone:** Swashbuckling boomtown adventure — tavern brawls, diving contracts,
guild rivalries, salt spray and street food — that turns into cosmic-horror
dread the deeper the party goes. Light and loud above the waterline, cold and
alien below it.

**Factions:**
- **The Salvage Concordat** — the merchant guild that licenses and taxes all
  diving; wants the boom to keep booming and actively covers up bad finds.
- **The Drowned Choir** — a dockside cult of the old kingdom's remaining
  faithful, convinced the sunken city can be raised again — and half right.
- **Harbor Watch** — the city's law, chronically outmatched by what's really
  down there, trying to hold the boomtown together with too few resources.
- **The Freedivers** — an informal network of independent salvagers (the
  PCs' likely "home" faction) who actually respect what's below and trade
  in warnings as much as loot.
- **It That Kept the Kingdom** — not a faction in the political sense, but a
  presence with agents, wants, and a plan for the surface.

**Central mystery:** Why did the old kingdom really drown, what deal keeps
"It" from simply rising now, and how much longer does Port Calder have
before someone's greed finally breaks that deal?

**Level arc:** 1 → 10.

## Pitch 3 — Wanderer's Compact

**Working title:** Wanderer's Compact

**Premise:** A century ago, the sky broke open and a scatter of floating
islands — fragments of some older, vaster world — drifted into place above
the ruins of a fallen empire. Airship clans settled the fragments, stringing
rope-bridges and trade routes between them, and built a rowdy, hopeful
frontier culture in the open sky. But the fragments are still drifting, some
slowly falling, and the old empire's automatons — dormant, half-awake, and
still "at war" — are stirring on the fragments that carry their ruins.
Wonder and open-sky freedom above; grinding, mechanical horror on the
fragments nobody's claimed yet.

**Tone:** High-adventure and open frontier optimism — airship crews,
sky-port bazaars, rival clans racing for new fragments — balanced against
the cold dread of waking something that was built to never stop fighting a
war that ended a thousand years ago.

**Factions:**
- **The Compact** — the loose alliance of airship clans that governs shared
  trade routes and claims; more a fragile truce than a government.
- **The Longfall Cartel** — salvagers who strip automaton ruins for parts and
  sell them, profiting directly from waking the thing everyone should fear.
- **The Wardens of the Hush** — a small order trying to keep dormant ruins
  dormant, chronically underfunded, dismissed as doomsayers.
- **The Iron Choir** — the awakening automaton remnant itself, not evil so
  much as still executing orders from a war and a command structure that no
  longer exist.
- **Skyborn independents** — unaligned clan families and settlers, the
  vast majority of the population, whose survival is what's actually at
  stake in every faction's maneuvering.

**Central mystery:** What the automatons' original war was actually about,
whether the fragments are falling on their own or being pulled, and what
happens to the whole Compact if the Iron Choir finishes waking up.

**Level arc:** 1 → 12.
