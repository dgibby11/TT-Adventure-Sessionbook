[[session_4|Session 4]] — how it fired

Minutes after the dragon withdrew, Vrenn dropped to his knees, holding his head and shaking it, for fifteen or twenty seconds. Then he came to.

"Morvek knows. He knows I'm here."

"Whatever you need to — if you need to give me to this dragon, that's fine. I don't have a fate either way. I have no good ending right now, so you do what you need to do."

DM Only — what the party did not see

That was [[morvek|Morvek]] casting sending. Twenty-five words, delivered straight into his head. The table was never told, and the words were never read out — and he did not use his right of reply.

Why He Is Not Here Yet — Vrenn's Own Reasoning

He worked it out in front of them, and it is the most useful thing anybody said all session:

  • Morvek probably has no tuning fork. "I don't think there's one left in the basement." He expects him to find another soon, and does not think he has the arcane capacity to plane shift today.
  • He cannot be scried across planes. Morvek knows he is on the Material Plane and nothing more precise. He cannot come to this spot unless he can scry, and he cannot scry unless he is already here.
  • "Two to three days is probably the best guess. Two if he really wants to come and get me. Three to four if he has other priorities — because he knows he can track me anywhere. I'm never safe."

DM Only — the thing nobody has said out loud

The party's own theft is what is buying them time, and none of them has said so out loud yet.

On the open question below, "does anything change now the components are gone?": it is now much sharper — the missing [[steel_tuning_fork|steel tuning fork]] is the specific thing Vrenn thinks is delaying him. The party did not steal it for that reason and do not appear to have noticed what it bought them.

[[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] told them plainly, on the ash plain and again at the foot of the basement stairs, why he could not leave with them.

What he said, before any of it happened

"He has my — it's basically the Abyssal equivalent to a soul coin. He has another name for it."

"I cannot go with you. I hope you understand that. I would love to. I would love to break free from this predicament, but I cannot go with you. If I were to leave the plane he would know immediately, and he would follow me, and he would kill all of you."

Then two [[basement_oni|oni]] dropped their faces, and he took a side. He cast into the fight, he stripped a lich's basement of everything that could be carried, and he charged two of the four bowls himself. By the time the circle was ready there was nothing left for him on that plane except a workbench he could no longer credibly return to, and about one round on the clock.

He came with them. He did not choose it. [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] had already asked him to.

Open Questions — updated after [[session_4|Session 4]]

  • Does [[morvek|Morvek]] know where he is right now? ANSWERED, and the answer is half-and-half. He knows the plane. He does not know the place. Vrenn was right about the Token and wrong to think it was the whole of the problem.
  • How long is "follow me"? Two to four days, and one is already gone. Gated on a tuning fork he does not have.
  • What happens when he arrives on a plane he cannot search? This is the new shape of the question. He has to get here, then scry, then travel — and Vrenn is moving south-west the whole time.
  • Does anything change now the components are gone? Still open.
  • What happens to the person who is standing next to him? Still open, and the party has now answered it for themselves: they were offered the chance to hand him to a green dragon and get out clean, and refused.
  • They do not know its name. They do now — "grave token", from Vrenn's own mouth, along with where it came from. See his entity.
  • Can the thing be taken off Morvek? Still nobody's asked properly. Vrenn does not know whether destroying it returns his soul. He does, however, have a plan: pit the dragon against the lich and collect the token off the corpse. He said so out loud, and he does not think it will work.
  • NEW: does the curse matter to any of this? He is now visibly deformed by a fomorian's Evil Eye, speed halved. Nothing connects that to Morvek — but a man being hunted who can no longer run is a different problem than he was yesterday.

DM Only — DECIDED IN PLAY (2026-09-24). It is SLOW, and the table got there on its own.

The three shapes below were written before Session 4 with slow recommended. Slow is what happened, and nothing had to be ruled to get it: the Token fired immediately and unmistakably, so the promise was cashed on schedule — and then Vrenn's own reasoning supplied the delay, and the delay is the party's theft. No tuning fork, no cross-planar scrying, two to four days.

Three things that are better about the played version than the written one:

  • The sending happened and was never narrated (DM, confirmed 2026-09-25). Morvek sent his twenty-five words; the table saw only a man on his knees holding his head for twenty seconds. That is exactly what the prep asked for — "the exact phrasing does not matter, because the players never hear it" — and the conversation afterwards carried the content. Do not let the record drift into "the Token pulsed at him"; it was a spell, and a lich chose to speak.
  • Vrenn did not reply, and nobody has told the party that he could have. Sending permits an immediate twenty-five-word answer. He declined it out of fear. That beat is still unspent and it is the most revealing single thing about him.
  • The clock came out of the victim's mouth as arithmetic, not out of the villain's as a threat. Nobody has to honour a number the DM did not promise.
  • The party's own actions are the brake, and the design wanted exactly that. They have not worked it out yet, which is a scene waiting to happen the first time somebody looks at the fork in Silas's pack and joins it up.

The hard constraint is unchanged: Morvek does not appear in person. He is an absence, an authority and a deadline, and he has now been all three for two full sessions.

The three shapes, kept for the record

The object is a Grave Token and it is a beacon, not an anchor (DM, 2026-08-26/29). Nothing physically stopped him crossing. It tells Morvek where its soul is, always. Full detail on [[morvek|Morvek's entity]] and in planning/vrenn_the_captive.json.

The locked design said "he will leave the site, he will not leave the plane." The table overruled it, on the fiction rather than by accident, and the transcript wins. That is not a problem to fix — it is the campaign cashing its best promise a phase early, and it did so because the players earned it. But it does mean a decision that was comfortably in the future is now due.

Three shapes, and they are meaningfully different campaigns:

  1. Slow — recommended. Morvek knows, and cannot easily act on it. Crossing planes is constrained by exactly the rule Vrenn just explained to the party, and the party has personally taken the fork and stripped one end of the lane. He has to arrange it. Weeks, hanging over the whole of Phase 2. Preserves the hard "Morvek never appears in person" constraint, makes the party's own theft the thing buying them time, and gives Vrenn room to become a character rather than a countdown.
  2. Fast. Something arrives within a session or two — not Morvek, who is far past a level-10 party, but something sent. Keeps the pressure honest and cashes the promise immediately.
  3. Vrenn's read is wrong. Entirely legitimate: he is repeating what Morvek told him, and Morvek is the one who benefits from him believing it. It is the only reading that lets Vrenn ever be safe — and it retires the best consequence the campaign has produced. Use only if the table needs a companion rather than a fuse.

Whichever way it goes, the party has acquired a person. A Wizard 13 with plane shift, Arcana +15, and a satchel of very expensive components is not a follower a level-10 party can carry without a stated policy on what he does in a fight. Session 3 gave the honest answer already: he acts once, decisively, out of despair rather than tactics, and then does something practical and non-combat. Hold him at that dial.

The thread underneath the thread

He sold the Token himself, and not for himself. His sister was going to die at the hands of something in the Undermountain; somebody was willing to trade for that, and what he had to trade was his soul. She is alive (DM, 2026-08-29), and whether Vrenn knows that is deliberately undecided. He does not tell this story and should not be made to — if it surfaces it surfaces later, in fragments, somewhere safe. He is now travelling with the party, which means "somewhere safe" is finally a place that can exist.

Retroactively it also means bought and sold multiple times stops being metaphor. The Token changed hands and he went with it, every time. He was never a person being traded. He was a receipt.
