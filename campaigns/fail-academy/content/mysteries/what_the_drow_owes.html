[[the_captive_drow|The drow]] at the workbench heard four people arrive on the landing stones behind him, turned around, and said this before he had properly looked at them:

The slip

"Oh — I wasn't — it's not time. I told you I needed another day."

He mistook them for someone else. Someone he expected. Someone he owes work to, on a schedule, and has already asked for an extension from.

Answered in [[session_3|Session 3]]

  • His name is Vrenn. He gave it thirty seconds into the next scene, unprompted.
  • Who he was expecting: [[morvek|Morvek]] — a lich, who owns him, and who was due at dawn to collect a scroll.
  • What he makes: scrolls and enchanted goods, to a list, on a schedule. He has sent roughly six packages, to more than one address, writing down words he is given. One of them went to [[torvald_thatch|Thatch]]. He has never heard of the man, or of the Academy, and does not consider himself responsible for what happens after a crate leaves.
  • Why he never used the scroll: Morvek holds his soul in an object on his belt — "basically the Abyssal equivalent to a soul coin, but he has another name for it." Leaving would not have freed him. It would have told Morvek exactly where he was.
  • Why the scroll failed, and it was not [[silas|Silas]]: Vrenn scribed it with his own magic quill, and only he could ever have cast from it. He had only just learned that himself.

Still Open

  • How long has he been there? Never asked, never answered. Long enough that his robes were once as good as Silas's.
  • How did a lich come to own a drow's soul? He has not said, and nobody has pushed.
  • What is actually in those crates? He made the contents. He is now sitting next to the party. Nobody has asked him.
  • And the big one: he said that leaving the plane would get everybody standing near him killed. Then he left the plane. See [[what_follows_vrenn|What Follows Vrenn]].

DM Only

His name is Vrenn and the person he owes is Morvek, or an intermediary of his — the periodic check-in pattern that mirrors torvald_the_insider.json exactly, and deliberately: this is how the whole supply chain runs, at both ends.

This is the best hook Session 2 produced and it was entirely unscripted. The party heard it clearly, it implies a supervisor and a delivery cadence without naming either, and it is small enough that it does not feel like an answer — which is precisely the register the design brief asks for. If they ever get a real conversation with him, this is the door.

Why he never used the scroll — ANSWERED (DM, 2026-08-26/29), superseding the deliberately-dual reading. It was never his to use. Morvek holds his soul in a Grave Token on his belt — an Abyssal soul-binding, not an infernal soul coin; nothing here touches the Nine Hells — and the Token tells Morvek where its soul is wherever it goes. He is not too broken to act. He is leashed. That is a better answer than either original (nowhere better to go / stopped being able to act on his own behalf), and both are retired.

And it re-aims the Session 2 handoff. He did not give away his one chance at freedom — he was never going to use it. What he gave four strangers was Morvek's property, the day before Morvek came to collect it. "They might kill me, they might not, I don't know" stops being resignation and becomes an accurate risk assessment by a man who had already decided. Braver, not smaller.

Session 3 spent most of this mystery deliberately and it was the right call — the party earned a real conversation and got one, and everything they learned funnels into a bigger question rather than closing the thread. The licensed slip is still unspent: "there's more of us now than there used to be." Traffic increasing. Still the cleanest way to land the clue that has now failed to arrive twice.

What has NOT come out of him and should stay in: the Grave Token by name, the sister, the Undermountain, Thanatos, Orcus, Harthoon, and how he came to sell his soul. He does not tell that story and should not be made to.
