[[the_captive_drow|The drow]] at the workbench heard four people arrive on the landing stones behind him, turned around, and said this before he had properly looked at them:

The slip

"Oh — I wasn't — it's not time. I told you I needed another day."

He mistook them for someone else. Someone he expected. Someone he owes work to, on a schedule, and has already asked for an extension from.

Open Questions

  • Who was he expecting? Somebody comes to that platform on a regular cadence, and it is not the hooved things — he was not afraid of whoever it was until he realised the party were strangers.
  • What is he making, and for whom?
  • How long has he been there? He gave no answer and there was no time to ask.
  • He had a way out — a plane shift scroll — and had never used it. Why not?
  • He gave that way out to four strangers rather than take it himself, said "if I leave now, I'm dead anyway," and stayed. What does that mean?

He never gave his name. Nobody asked.

DM Only

His name is Vrenn and the person he owes is Vrok, or an intermediary of his — the periodic check-in pattern that mirrors torvald_the_insider.json exactly, and deliberately: this is how the whole supply chain runs, at both ends.

This is the best hook Session 2 produced and it was entirely unscripted. The party heard it clearly, it implies a supervisor and a delivery cadence without naming either, and it is small enough that it does not feel like an answer — which is precisely the register the design brief asks for. If they ever get a real conversation with him, this is the door.

Why he never used the scroll — ANSWERED (DM, 2026-08-26/29), superseding the deliberately-dual reading. It was never his to use. Vrok holds his soul in a Grave Token on his belt — an Abyssal soul-binding, not an infernal soul coin; nothing here touches the Nine Hells — and the Token tells Vrok where its soul is wherever it goes. He is not too broken to act. He is leashed. That is a better answer than either original (nowhere better to go / stopped being able to act on his own behalf), and both are retired.

And it re-aims the Session 2 handoff. He did not give away his one chance at freedom — he was never going to use it. What he gave four strangers was Vrok's property, the day before Vrok came to collect it. "They might kill me, they might not, I don't know" stops being resignation and becomes an accurate risk assessment by a man who had already decided. Braver, not smaller.

Everything else about him is in planning/vrenn_the_captive.json and is unspent. Only two things are allowed to slip out of him: Vrok's name, and the remark that there are more of them now than there used to be.
