Read Aloud / Performance — owed to Session 3

[[ellery_voss|Voss]] catches the four of you in a side corridor while the rest of the graduating cohort is still filing toward the seating — robes half-adjusted, no time for a real conversation. She presses a folded slip of paper into whichever of you is nearest. "Before I forget, and before today gets its hooks into all of us. Names and cities — people I trust, if you're ever somewhere I'm not and need to be somewhere safe. Memorize it, don't lose it, don't need it. Most operatives never do." A beat, and something almost like a smile. "Go. You're about to be late for your own graduation."

The list is written and live. Six entries, one of them struck out. Each is a real entity — [[nyella_sarreth|Nyella Sarreth]] (Silverymoon), [[anselm_ferreck|Anselm Ferreck]] (Neverwinter), [[ledger|Ledger]] (Mirabar), [[huri_sibreldo|Huri Sibreldo]] (Triel, crossed off), [[lysandra_belaine|Lysandra Belaine]] (Waterdeep), and [[emeric_vantt|Emeric Vantt]] (Caer Westphal, the Moonshaes). The physical prop should be full names, cities, and one terse locating note each — slightly more than the "names and cities" Voss says aloud, which is fine and worth the trade for a prop the players will actually pore over. Keep Huri's strike-through legible: one clean line through the name with the city still readable underneath. It is the most interesting thing on the page and the players will find it inside a minute.

This is a plant, not a puzzle — it should read as routine onboarding, not foreshadowing. Two things Voss does not do here, both deliberate: she gives them no countersign (which is exactly the obstacle [[anselm_ferreck|Ferreck]] presents in Neverwinter), and she does not mention that [[emeric_vantt|Vantt]] has not written in two years, though she will say so plainly if asked.

Before the Ceremony — Tito's Final Thesis

Unscripted, and now canon. The Bards' final thesis is a performance, not a written examination — and by department tradition the entire Bards Department attends every fifth-year thesis, every year group and faculty both. A day or two before Commencement, [[tito|Tito]] delivered his: a piece of storytelling rather than a musical number — a tale of two star-crossed lovers, both parts played by him alone, turning side to side with a different mask for each. He performed it wearing [[standing_ovation|The Standing Ovation]], the cloak [[viola_gossamer|Viola Gossamer]] made for him.

It went extremely well — comfortably one of the best in his year, if not the best. He brought the house down, and for the rest of the week the Bards' rows think of him as the one who did that. [[silas|Silas]] was in the audience and reports laughing and crying, though by his own account not necessarily at the intended moments. So, by tradition, was [[viola_gossamer|Viola Gossamer]] — who watched her own cloak carry the best performance of the year, and whom Tito never noticed was there.

Commencement

Well scheduled in advance, and long attended by all four as underclassmen, so nothing about the shape of it is new. Graduates have their own block of seating with no order imposed within it; the four of them sat together. Faculty line the ends of the rows, with an overflow standing at the back. Friends and family fill the rest.

Dowe's Opening — as played

[[headmistress_dowe|Headmistress Dowe]] opens the ceremony herself, brief and characteristically dry. The speech is the same speech she gives every year — that this Academy exists to make you formidable, and whether it also makes you wise is still up to you — and the graduating class has all heard it before at somebody else's Commencement. She does not stray far from it. She then introduces the guest speaker.

The party has had very little contact with Dowe over five years. She keeps to her office on the top floor of [[main_complex|the Main Academy Complex]], almost all of it administrative; when she is out on campus she is brusque and dry and not especially chatty. For this session she is deliberately a background figure — present, presiding, not a scene partner.

The Guest Speaker — as played

Councilor Bertram Hollis of Silverymoon's Merchant Council: a stout, faintly porky human, nicely dressed, delighted to have been asked and entirely unaware how little that delight is shared. Nobody has much respect for him and most of the graduates knew that before he stood up. He gives a bland, generic, thoroughly forgettable speech, runs over his time, and ends on "each and every one of you has the power to make a real difference in this world" before trailing off. The applause is a courtesy — arguably gratitude for his leaving the stage. It is hot, it is stuffy, and everyone including the four of them is ready for it to be over.

The Conferral — Which Ring Each of Them Took

All four had already registered their pattern with the Registrar in the days before — privately, with the list in front of them and as long as they wanted to consider it. Commencement is where the choice gets announced, not where it gets made.

Dowe reads the graduates' names in seating order. Each crosses the stage to her, is handed their ring from an ornate mahogany case with a glass lid, and collects a diploma from a clerk standing beside her on the way off. The bands are struck identical — gold, the Academy crest to the face, the year inside — so nothing about a graduate's choice shows on their hand. It shows because Dowe says it out loud, to several hundred people.

The Terms — stated at the table

It was explained to the cohort during the rehearsal process and it is worth restating: the ring attunes the moment you put it on, it attunes only to you, and it stays bound. There is no ring-sharing. There is no killing a graduate for their ring. It cannot be sold — off your hand it is a piece of metal. If you ever want a different one, you go back and earn it.

  • [[guntrah|Guntrah]] — Ring of Swimming. Called up from the Barbarians Department, with his construct walking beside him. He lowered himself to take it from her. His reasoning, given plainly when she asked: he can't attune to most of the sixteen, he can already fly and already dash, so — why not move faster underwater. Dowe smirked, announced it to the crowd, and told him they don't often get asked for that one, and that it was a very wise choice.
  • [[silas|Silas]] — Ring of Regeneration. Called up as "Silas, the wizard, from the School of Evocation" — after a slip of the tongue that briefly enrolled him in the Bards. Dowe read the pattern out and handed it over. "Great selection." Silas, accepting it: "I definitely could use this." Dowe: "I hope you don't have to, but it's nice to know that you have it."
  • [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] — Ring of the Ram. Marched up head high with his Aura of Courage running and took it without ceremony. Announced as "Tavian Stormnet, Paladin of the Oath of Glory."
  • [[tito|Tito]] — Ring of Free Action. Threw his cloak open to bare his chest on the way up, cast prestidigitation for a shower of sparks, raised his hands to the crowd and claimed the moment out loud — he has thrown all the best parties, and this is his farewell. The cheer that started was cut off by Dowe: "please, enough with the theatrics, let's just come over here." He gave her a faint bow. She read his pattern to the crowd, called it one of her own favorites, and asked him to collect his diploma with no further theatrics.

All four put their rings on immediately and all four attuned on the spot.

Nothing sinister about the ritual itself — a genuine, mundane school tradition, not a [[ninth_thesis|Ninth Thesis]] or Thornwick mechanism. That held at the table and no player probed it.

Where's Voss?

Walking back to their seats, three of the four clocked it: [[ellery_voss|Professor Voss]] was not there. Not late, not standing at the back with the faculty overflow — absent. Between them they have attended a good number of these ceremonies and she has never once missed one, or any other Academy event.

[[tito|Tito]] did not notice. He was, by his own account, having a moment about himself, which was fair enough.

[[guntrah|Guntrah]] nudged the other two and said it out loud. The read landed immediately as wrong rather than as a puzzle — she's been busy lately, I wonder if she's okay, seems odd she didn't check in and see if she needed us for anything. They agreed to go and find her after the ceremony, and to still make the party.

DM Only — What Was Actually Happening

Voss used the ceremony's own cover — everyone accounted for, [[academy_provisions|Provisions]] reliably unmanned — to try to get into the back room herself and find out what has been sitting on the delayed requisition she has been chasing since [[session_1|Session 1]]. She did not expect to find [[osric_morne|Osric Morne]] there; he stayed back deliberately (see the_prov_investigation.json's osric_beat — paperwork has been going missing on him and he no longer trusts the normal channels either). They had a real argument, entirely off-screen. Osric refused to let her past the counter on principle, the same principle that applies to anyone; Voss pushed harder than her composure usually allows before catching herself. She left angry, mostly with herself.

The party still has no idea any of this happened beyond her own account of it, below.

Farewells

The ceremony closed with brief congratulations from Dowe, a musical number from underclassmen of the Bards, and a general release to mingle. All four went to find people. This was the longest stretch of the session and almost all of it was unscripted; what follows is now canon.

Guntrah and the Barbarians

[[guntrah|Guntrah]] crossed the stage with his construct walking at his heel — deliberately, as a small piece of showing-off — and the Barbarians in attendance noticed. Not raging, but visibly annoyed, several of them, at the sense that they had been tricked; he does not look like a barbarian and never has. He did not care. He had graduated, and he had done it his own way.

Afterward he went over to the Barbarians' block to say goodbye properly: it had been an interesting time, and a pleasure to study with them. A student a year below him stepped forward and said he thought it was pretty cool — most of them can't do half of what Guntrah can do, and the fact that he pulled it off for so long is impressive on its own terms. Guntrah told them straight that passing on a different set of skills was a genuine challenge and that he appreciated them pushing him, because it made him grow in a different direction. "Perhaps this is not goodbye. But if it is — thanks."

They sent him off with the traditional greeting: the clasped-hand pull with a headbutt. Guntrah, running on enchanted gear rather than muscle, threw the man around a bit in the process. It read as real to everyone present. "Goddamn it," said the man, shaking his head.

Holdar — the beat that finally landed

Guntrah looked out into the crowd for [[prof_bram_holdar|Professor Holdar]] and found him standing off to the side, two-thirds of the way back on the right. He held the look — not long, no words, just enough to be seen as who he actually is — and moved on.

Holdar turned his head away. He would not meet Guntrah's gaze. What it looked like, from the stage, was shame.

By the time Guntrah went looking for him again after the ceremony, Holdar had already left. He was not anywhere to be found.

DM Only — what Holdar was actually feeling

Guntrah read it as shame and Guntrah is half right. Holdar turned away partly out of embarrassment — five years of being wrong, in public, with the proof walking past him — and partly out of disgust. Both at once, and he could not have separated them himself in the moment.

The disgust is the important half, because it means this is not a man quietly coming around. Holdar built the Barbarians Department on one conviction: theory is the enemy of instinct, hesitation is a failure of commitment, and strength is the only argument that survives contact. Guntrah did not beat that on its own terms — he routed around it for five years with enchanted gear and a straight face, and then walked the stage with a construct at his heel. To Holdar that is not ingenuity; it is the exact thing he warns students against, rewarded in front of everyone he teaches.

He is also, underneath it, ashamed of himself — he intercepted the boy's acceptance and has never once doubted it was the right call, and the doubt arriving now, at a ceremony, in front of witnesses, is unbearable. He left rather than be spoken to.

Deliberately unresolved: whether the disgust is aimed at Guntrah, at himself, or at what the Academy just applauded. Don't pin it — Holdar hasn't. It should stay live and unpleasant, because the obvious version of this scene (gruff mentor privately proud) is the one the table will expect and the one that is not true.

He does not know Guntrah saw him look away. Guntrah did not push it. Whatever happens next between them is unspent.

Guntrah's Parents — Silas and Breena

[[guntrah|Guntrah]] spotted his adoptive gnomish parents in the crowd on his perception check and gave them a nod. Afterward he went and found them properly, sitting in the chairs like children with their feet nowhere near the floor, barely able to speak for how proud they were. "Pops? Mom?" — "I did it."

He told his father the joke that has been sitting there all year: he has a friend with the same name as him, a half-orc, isn't that strange — that's a gnomish name, why would he have a gnomish name? "Maybe when this is over we'll go find them. There's not too many orcs here."

Then the real conversation. He is not coming back to the shop, at least not yet. He and his friends have opportunities through the university and he wants more of the world before he goes home. His father took it well — "just promise me you'll be safe; your mother told me this was a probability" — with the sadness visible under it. Guntrah asked whether they'd be all right without him, whether the shop was all right, and got "we've got dozens of years left" — a phrasing that stopped him for a moment before his father clarified he meant the two of them together, not himself. Guntrah promised to come back before he is an old man, and to find a way to stay in touch.

Which prompted the gift. His father reached in and produced a speaking stone — one he'd meant to send a year ago and lost in the mess of his own workshop, found again just in time before they left. "I wanted to make sure you always have a way to reach us." They will try to reach out at least once a day.

Guntrah picked them both up, hugged them, and told them he'd be in touch.

Guntrah introduces his father to Professor Tavel

Talk turned to how Guntrah actually got through, and to Ollie — the little steel construct, ball-shaped with a couple of stabby arms and legs. Three and a half years to build. Guntrah's father recognised the design instantly as almost exactly the one he'd shown Guntrah years ago when they were practicing; Guntrah told him he'd finally made it, and that he'd had help from an artificer instructor.

His father's response, which is the whole of the man in one line: "Everybody needs a little bit of help from time to time. You should never turn away anybody that offers to help you."

[[prof_tavel|Professor Tavel]] was still there, in conversation with a couple of lower-year students. Guntrah took his father over and introduced them. His father was visibly starstruck — Tavel, it turns out, is genuinely famous, a dwarven artificer known for a run of inventions some forty or fifty years ago, which Guntrah had no idea about and which surprised Tavel a little in turn. Tavel: "I recognized this talent a mile away. Look at Ollie here, he's brilliant." Guntrah's father, chiming in: it's actually part of my design — he just finished it exactly like we planned. Tavel: "Your son's a very talented artificer. He's going to go places."

Guntrah caught his father withdrawing a little from the conversation as it went on — the sadness of a man realising his son isn't coming back to the workshop, and deciding it's the right thing anyway. "Thanks again for taking him under your wing."

Guntrah shook Tavel's hand with the barbarian pull, bent nearly double to do it, and thanked him for everything.

Silas and his parents — and what he told Tavian

[[silas|Silas]]'s parents were there: an orc father and a human mother, both enormously proud. He hugged them, and told them Rosgaunt would be safe without him.

Then he called [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] over, and this is the beat of the session. He introduced him to his parents as the reason he made it through — and got genuinely tearful doing it.

He told them what he had never told them at the time: that he only got to come here because of the lottery, that a lot of the other students gave him grief he didn't deserve for it, that he nearly went home more than once in that first year and wanted many times to just call them and ask to be picked up. He didn't tell them then because he didn't want to burden them. What kept him there was Tavian — who encouraged him to stay, who kept telling him he could do it, and who told him that if he left he'd always wonder what might have been. Silas said he'd felt like an impostor, and that Tavian made him feel like he belonged here. He hugged him, crying.

Tavian's reply: "Yes, Silas. You're part of the crew."

Silas's father reached out to shake Tavian's hand, changed his mind halfway, and pulled him into a hug instead. He is a man of few words and did not use many. Silas's mother, in tears herself: "we're very grateful. Thank you for your help in all of this."

Tito and his parents — Baldur's Gate

[[tito|Tito]] went to find his parents, chiefly in expectation of a graduation gift, which he says is traditional in his family.

What got established: both parents are satyrs, he is an only child, and the family is wealthy — comfortably so, but not flashy and not ostentatious; his own framing was "Tony Soprano rich" rather than anything grander. Home is Baldur's Gate, where his father runs a merchant house in import-export. Tito went away to school at eighteen and has been here five years, on track the whole way. He attended a private school before that. He has heard things about what his father actually does — possibly illicit, possibly illegal — but only ever as hearsay, and his father has deliberately sheltered him from it until he came of age. There is an unspoken but entirely clear expectation that he will come home and take over the business, and it is being offered rather than imposed.

His parents had arrived a day early and seen his thesis performance. His mother's first question was whether he owns any shirts anymore. They had a party prepared for him at home; he told them he couldn't make it because he is throwing his own, and that he has things to take care of first and will let them know when he's coming back. His father stood there and said very little. His mother asked if he was all right for food and money, and revealed the graduation gift was waiting back at the house — sneakily, given he wasn't coming.

Then, as his mother turned to go, his father winked and slipped him a sack of gold, deliberately without his mother knowing. Tito has not counted it and does not know what's in it. "Get out of here."

Tavian's village, and Orvyn

[[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] has no family and expected nobody. To his surprise, about six people from the fishing village that took him in had made the trip and were waiting for him — including the man who took him in. They were the loudest cheer in the building when he crossed the stage. They do not get out of town much, and when they do it is for supplies, so an excuse to dress up and be happy about something was welcome. Good shoes, no holes in the overalls, one set of good fish waders.

Tavian went over, worked the crowd of graduating classmates first with a great deal of easy charm, then spent the time with his village. The affection is unmistakable and entirely unceremonious — you gonna come fish with us again? You got any spells to get some more fish? Wonder if that fancy ring would catch some.

Afterward he sought out [[commander_orvyn|Commander Orvyn]], who was waiting for him. A good firm handshake, and: "I think you're bound for very good things. I think you're going to achieve a lot of glory. As long as you stay focused and hold to your oath, I think you're going to do great." Then, deliberately: "And don't forget — I owe you another story."

Tavian, still holding the handshake, thanked him for everything he'd taught him about the Oath of Glory, and said he'd take it to heart, protect his crew, and do glorious things. One more firm handshake, and Orvyn turned and walked away quietly and stoically, exactly as he always does.

DM Only — Orvyn's hook is still open

[[session_1|Session 1]]'s standing promise — "come find me after graduation, and I'll tell you another story; this one's about a young tiefling boy who first set foot on this campus some fifty years ago" — was explicitly restated at Commencement and still has not been paid off. The party is now off-plane and cannot collect on it. That is fine; it keeps.

Voss's Office

Once the crowd had thinned — still late afternoon, plenty of day left, the party well ahead of the evening's festivities — the four of them regrouped and went to find Voss, on [[silas|Silas]]'s push. Her office had been their first thought anyway; she had left them a note there once before.

Her door was shut. They knocked. She opened it, harried: "yes, yes, what can I do for you?"

Voss — as played

[[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]]: "You missed graduation." — "I did, yes." — "Are you okay?" — "No, I'm not. Come in quick."

Door shut behind them. She has been trying to get to the bottom of these incidents and she has just been to the provisions office. She has spoken to [[osric_morne|Osric Morne]] ten times in the last ten days and it has done no good — he won't show her the paperwork, won't show her any audits, won't open his books. He is not being cooperative, and not because he is a bad man: he swears there is no paperwork on these items because they never came through his office at all, and that she is barking up the wrong tree. She does not want to break school rules. She cannot afford to lose her position. And she has run out of ways to do this properly.

"I really hate to ask you this — and you must know this isn't something I would condone ordinarily. But is there any chance you might be able to look in the provisions office on my behalf?"

It obviously cannot have come from her. They are graduates now, so what is the worst that happens — they already have their rings, they cannot be expelled. But people hold grudges. Someone floated "graduate assistants" as the cover story and it stuck.

She does not know what is in there. There might be nothing. There might be no paper trail at all. There might be another one of the artifacts, or a logbook of everything coming in — she does not know how he runs his office. "Just see what you can see. And if it's nothing, then it's nothing, and I'm barking up the wrong tree."

They accepted on the spot. [[tito|Tito]]: "You can count on us, Professor."

Voss laid out the building for them: the main shop entrance, a back room or warehouse behind it, and Osric's living quarters, which open onto both. There is a transport door at the back for drop-offs, and as far as she knows he holds the only key. He works constantly and effectively lives in the building; there is almost no avoiding him.

The plan came together fast. Voss would go in the front and hold his attention herself. She offered two or three minutes; the party asked for five or six; she said she would manage six and improvise a fresh distraction if she needed to. The party would follow about thirty seconds behind, walk past, and go around the back.

The lockpicks

Getting through the back door was the obvious problem. [[guntrah|Guntrah]] can build any nonmagical tool he likes, but it takes an hour. Voss went to her desk, opened a drawer, and produced a rolled leather kit of metal lockpicking tools. "I confiscated this three weeks ago."

"Again — this doesn't come back to me. And just dispose of it when you're done, please. I don't want to see it anymore."

The Provisions Break-In

Voss walked down and went in the front. From the alley the party could hear her faintly — an animated discussion, not quite shouting, making it very clear she was unhappy with how she was being treated.

The back door is a standard door with a standard lock and nothing special about it. The alley behind is quiet: the back of another building, a student passing perhaps once every five minutes.

[[tito|Tito]] picked it, with [[guntrah|Guntrah]] holding the kit to give him advantage. It took three attempts and got harder each time — the third was the last one the mechanism would survive, and he made it by a hair on a Bardic Inspiration die he gave himself. Click.

All four went in — Tito on stealth, Tavian holding his breath the whole way (Air Genasi don't need to; he maintained it helped him move quietly), Guntrah noisy in armor but carried by the group. They pulled the door most of the way shut behind them rather than latching it, which killed almost all the light.

[[tito|Tito]] has no darkvision. The other three do. For him the back room was a dark room at night with a little moonlight in it — five to ten feet of vision and no sight of the walls.

The back room

A very tightly run operation. Shelves all around, crates stacked everywhere, every single crate labelled by hand in an expert script with exactly what is inside it. In one corner, further off, a processing table where inbound shipments are opened, emptied and logged. It reads as a mailroom — for goods rather than mail.

No logbooks visible. No archives of any kind from where they're standing. Just stock.

They searched in the dark, at disadvantage. Guntrah rolled well and found a number of things a less scrupulous person would have pocketed, but not what they were looking for. Tito found nothing. And then [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] — who was helping by picking things up more or less at random and holding them out to be identified, on an Intelligence of 4 — held up the right one.

The Package

What was written on it

A plain shipping container, closed, nothing visible inside. It is addressed to the groundskeeper — [[torvald_thatch|Torvald Thatch]]. Beneath the address, written by hand and also to Torvald Thatch: an instruction not to let Professor Voss see this. By name.

And beneath that, in a different script: several lines in Celestial.

[[guntrah|Guntrah]] recognised the second block as Celestial on an investigation check. [[silas|Silas]] — the party's only Celestial speaker — was across the room and reading it as fast as he could walk. He got one word off it before Guntrah, who had already announced the find and started tearing the package open, got it open.

Silas called out to stop. It was already too late.

DM Only — the trigger changed at the table

The spoken-trigger-word design is scrapped (DM ruling, 2026-08-23). The locked plan had the glyph keyed to a proper noun inside the Celestial text, fired by whoever read it aloud. At the table the DM ran it as fired by the package being opened, and that is now what happened. session_plan.json's "Session 2 Celestial trigger word" open item is closed — no word is owed, and none was ever spoken.

The rest of the design held: 7th-level glyph of warding storing plane shift, 20 ft radius, DC 20 Charisma save, one round to follow. The elegant part of the original — that the trap only catches someone literate enough to be a threat — is weakened by this change, since anyone can open a box. Worth deciding whether that clue is still available to the party by another route before it matters, because as run, the package would have taken Osric or Torvald just as readily. See the Leak Scan block below.

What the party actually retained: a package for the groundskeeper, a handwritten instruction to hide it from Voss, and Celestial text nobody read. They do not know it was a glyph, do not know it was keyed, and do not know why nothing had happened to it before they touched it.

The Portal

As played

The moment the container comes open, the package is gone and so are two of you. A rift has opened directly behind where you were standing, and you can feel it pulling.

[[silas|Silas]] and [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] were taken. [[guntrah|Guntrah]] and [[tito|Tito]] held their ground — feet planted, or simply enough will to resist the pull.

Guntrah investigated it in the seconds he had. He has studied portals: teleportation circles and rings, dimension door-type portals of limited range and duration — and this is neither. It carries far more power than those, and it is a doorway to another plane. He could not tell where it went, only that it left this one. He also knew these things are short-lived. Ten seconds, at the outside.

"Shit. We gotta go." Guntrah ran at it. [[tito|Tito]] followed a step behind him.

Nobody stayed behind in the Annex. Tito asked, before jumping, whether they could bring the letter with them in case it carried instructions — it had already vanished with the package when the rift opened.

DM Only — how the saves actually fell

Per the DM (2026-08-23): the numbers don't matter, only the resulting action. Silas and Tavian failed and were taken; Guntrah and Tito resisted and then chose to follow inside the one-round window.

The Other Side

Arrival — as played

Going through is not like blacking out and it is barely visual — closer to a psychedelic experience, your brain everywhere at once, like far too many mushrooms. You shut your eyes to make it stop. It feels like a long time. It is a few seconds.

You come to lying on the ground. Dark red sky. Heavy dark cloud. Very distant screaming — something large, more than one. Rumbling underfoot. The landscape is desolate.

Close to your right: two ritual statues. A figure hunched over a workbench, not unlike ones you have built things at yourselves. A large runic circle worked into the stone at the centre of the area, with several other landing spots like the one you're on arranged around it. The whole site sits like an island in the earth — the ground simply stops, and a moat runs all the way around it, lit orange from below. Beyond that, smoke, and past the smoke, mountains you can only just make out.

The party arrived in two audible pops — Silas and Tavian first, Guntrah and Tito a beat later. The figure at the bench heard them.

DM Only — What This Place Is (unchanged, and still unknown to the party)

A fabrication/ritual outpost on Thanatos, the 333rd layer of the Abyss where Orcus resides (see campaign_arc.json's premise) — one of the sites where the corrupting items seeded around campus this year are actually made. The dais is also an altar. The site belongs to Vrask, a lesser lich, who runs this operation on the Abyssal side. Vrask answers to Harthoon one tier up; that hierarchy is DM-only background and Harthoon has no presence here.

None of this reached the table. The party does not know the plane's name, does not know it is the Abyss, has never heard "Vrask", and never examined the altar, the tools, or the workbench. See "What Did Not Land", below.

Hard constraint, still in force: Vrask does not appear in person. Any lich is well past a level 10 party. He is felt as an absence and an authority, never a body in the room.

The Drow at the Workbench

He set down whatever he was holding and turned to look at them: a drow, ashen-skinned, slim and lean the way elves are, but visibly haggard — not weak so much as used up. Work gloves. Mid-job on something.

First contact — as played

"Oh — I wasn't — it's not time. I told you I needed another day."

Then, as it registers that they are not who he expected: "You're not— you need to leave. You need to leave immediately. You've got to go. This is not good."

The party tried. Who are you? Where are we? Say hello, friend. [[tito|Tito]] stepped between him and the others and tried the one gambit they had: "Thatch sent us."

"I don't know who that is. You need to leave. In fact—"

And that was when the hooves started across the bridge.

The drow said "it's too late, it's too late," crouched as low as he could get, and went under his workbench — not hidden, plainly visible, simply blocking out the world. He stayed there for the entire fight.

DM Only — the drow is Vrenn, and he never said so

He never gave his name and the party never asked. Per the DM (2026-08-23) he is still Vrenn and the name is expected to land in Session 3. Until then, all player-facing prose says "the drow." Do not let "Vrenn" appear anywhere the players can read.

His full history is in vrenn_the_captive.json and is entirely intact and unspent — how long he's been here, the chain of owners, the deliberately incomplete craft, his service at the altar. Nothing whatsoever leaked. His characterisation played almost exactly to the brief: wary rather than grateful, no help offered unprompted, no useful answer to a direct question, terrified of what was coming.

One thing that did land, and is a gift: "it's not time — I told you I needed another day." He mistook them for someone he owes work to on a schedule. That is a live, unexplained thread the party heard clearly, and it points at the existence of a supervisor and a delivery cadence without naming either. If they ever get a conversation with him, that is the door.

Torvald's name entered the scene from a player's mouth, not the DM's — Tito volunteered it — and the drow's honest "I don't know who that is" gave nothing back. The guardrail held, and it held better than a refusal to say the name would have: the party now has a data point that the groundskeeper is not known at this end of the chain.

The Fight

Two very large four-legged hoofed creatures came across a permanent stone bridge over the moat — no mechanism, just stone. [[tito|Tito]], who has more practice listening to hooves than anyone else present, picked out something else in the sound: a crackling arcane energy travelling with them. Roughly forty feet out and closing. Five seconds, no cover worth the name, nowhere to go.

What they got done in that window: Tito ducked behind [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] and inspired him. [[guntrah|Guntrah]] flew thirty feet straight up, so focused on the ground that he had no idea what was in the air with him. Tavian called "crew, get ready!" and cast bless on himself, Silas and Tito. [[silas|Silas]] readied a spell.

The wall

The first creature charged. Silas's readied action fired and a wall of stone came up in its face — sixty feet of it in a U, twenty on the left flank, twenty across the front, twenty on the right, doubled to twenty feet high on two sides with the panels left over. Six inches thick, AC 15, 180 hit points a panel.

The creature ran into it and started hitting it, because it did not know what else to do. It is not an intelligent thing.

The wall decided the fight. Both creatures spent most of the encounter beating on stone while the party shot over and through it. Tito's chromatic orb, cast at 5th level, hit for 43 cold and bounced for 35 more before the party worked out that these things resist cold; Guntrah put crossbow bolts and Branding Smite into them from the air; the second creature gave up on the wall and threw a lance of lightning at Guntrah for 34. Tito switched to acid, throwing orbs through the stone at the cost of damaging it, and blind-fired at disadvantage twice more for 27 and 39. Tavian held a guiding bolt the entire fight for the moment one of them broke through, and landed it when the wall finally came down. Silas, with no line of sight through his own wall, readied mind sliver for the same moment and landed it on a natural 1.

They won. Guntrah dropped the first with a second crossbow attack he had forgotten he was entitled to. The second broke through, reached Tito and hit him three times — claw, hooves, and a ten-foot tail that wrapped around his shield even through a shield spell — and Tito killed it on his next turn with phantasmal killer. There was nothing on either corpse worth taking: natural weapons, metallic hide, horns, tail. Salvageable components at most.

Most of the wall is still standing, with a hole in it. Silas has not dropped concentration.

DM Only — the armanites

Two armanites, CR 7 each. The DM deliberately declined to name them when asked ("I don't know if I want to tell you that"), so the party has no name for them; Tito improvised "blight" as a label mid-fight and that is the only word they have. The read-aloud description was given straight from the stat block — the dense hooves, the curling talons, the serrated tail that can flense flesh, the innate lightning, and the detail that they are almost never seen in packs as small as two. That last line is doing real work and the party heard it.

The fight ran much easier than blocked out — the plan called it "genuinely hard" for four level-10 PCs and it was close to trivial, because a 60-foot wall of stone against two creatures with no ranged options but one rechargeable lightning attack is a hard counter. The DM said so out loud at the table: "it wasn't really meant to be a challenge." Note for the future: Silas's wall of stone is a fight-ender against melee-only enemies and needs to be planned around rather than walked into.

The Scroll, and How It Ended

Mid-fight, [[silas|Silas]] and [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] both noticed the drow reaching up from under his bench, groping around the workbench above him, and pulling something down: a scroll, which he unrolled and looked to be on the point of reading. He never read it.

When the second creature went down, he rolled it back up, came out fast, and crossed to Tavian — the one who had spoken to him.

The handoff — as played

He puts it in your hands. "You need to get out of here immediately. There's going to be fifteen more of them here in about a minute. You have to go now."

"They might kill me, they might not, I don't know. But if I leave now, I'm dead anyway. So just — just go. It's the best thing."

"Where do we go?" — "Read the scroll."

Tavian handed it to [[silas|Silas]]: "Silas — what does this say?"

It is a plane shift scroll: the same spell that brought them here. Silas is a wizard, which put a real dilemma in front of him — he could keep it and copy it into his spellbook, having it available for good once he is high enough level to cast it, or he could try to cast it cold, right now, and get everyone out. Copying takes fourteen hours. They did not have fourteen hours. They had about a minute.

He tried to cast it.

How Session 2 ends

Casting a 7th-level spell off a scroll, ten levels of wizard and a +9 modifier behind it. Natural 1.

The spell goes out of the scroll and takes nothing with it. The scroll is spent. There are fifteen more of them coming, and you are still here.

The session stops there. The party is stranded on the far plane, the drow is still under his bench, two corpses are on the platform, most of a stone wall is still standing, and something is coming across the bridge.

DM Only — Carried Forward: The Escape (still entirely live, LOCKED 2026-08-19)

None of this was used. It is prep for Session 3, not a record.

Plane shift needs a forked metal rod attuned to the destination plane, and it lands you "in or near" a destination described in general terms. That component is the whole answer to how the party gets home and why home is not where they end up.

The rod is here, at the dais. It has to be: this site ships finished goods to the Material Plane and cannot do that without one. It sits in or beside the central dais as part of the working anchor, and it is set to a delivery lane rather than to a place anybody chooses. There is more than one lane. The Academy's consignment has already gone out — it was the package in the Annex — so the rod is currently set to the other one, which terminates in Neverwinter Wood.

That is why they land where they land, and it is not arbitrary: somebody else is being supplied out there too. That is a real clue, available without any roll if they think about it, and it quietly seeds Thundertree as one of the Session 3 directions. [[session_1|Session 1]]'s unresolved figure at the treeline is a separate thread and should stay separate.

The rod is portable and they can take it. It is physical proof and a genuine future asset, but it is not an escape hatch: plane shift is 7th level and the party's highest slot is 5th. They will carry the means to go home and be unable to use it for a long time. That ache is the point.

The drow knows the lanes operationally and not at all conceptually — he can say there is "the other one" and that it has been used more lately, because he has watched it happen. He cannot say where it goes.

Status of each exit after 2026-08-20:

  • The central dais — untouched and fully live. Never approached, never examined. Forcing it under duress is an Arcana check, or trivial with the drow's help. Exposed, out on the open platform.
  • The drow's scroll — SPENT. He gave it to them unasked (they never had to find it behind the loose stone, and never searched his alcove), and Silas rolled a natural 1 casting from it. The spell is gone from the scroll. This exit is closed permanently. Note the cost to him: the only thing that was ever going to get him out, given away and then wasted in front of him.
  • The causeway south — untouched. Still leads deeper into the site rather than to safety.
  • The alcove chambers — untouched, including the one the drow is kept in. Still defensible dead-ends, still the best place for him to actually talk.

Session 3 opens under a clock: fifteen armanites, about a minute out, stated aloud at the table. That is not survivable and is not meant to be. The dais is now effectively the only exit, which is a tighter and better scene than the original two-option choice — but it means the arrival in Neverwinter Wood is now a Session 3 event, and everything below shifts one session later.
