The Clock

At the table the drow said "fifteen more of them here in about a minute," and that is what the party heard. It is an estimate, not a fact (DM, 2026-08-26): he has watched these things for a year and knows their numbers and patrol patterns, and fifteen-in-a-minute is where he puts the highest probability. He was speculating.

DM Only — what the hedge buys you
  • It converts a countdown into a decision window. You are not obliged to drop fifteen armanites on the platform on a stopwatch.
  • A forward patrol of three or four arriving ahead of the main body is the most useful shape: it pressures them to move rather than fortify behind Silas's wall, and it makes the wall's ten-minute expiry felt rather than explained.
  • Whatever arrives, the answer is a chase, not a stand. Two armanites played close to trivial because a 60-ft wall of stone hard-counters melee-only enemies. Do not run the follow-up as a scaled-up version of the same fight.
  • If they ask him how sure he is, he tells them the truth: "I've watched them a year. Fifteen's what I'd bet. I've been wrong."

DM Only — WHY THEY HAVE TO COME BACK (DM, 2026-09-02). Load-bearing; read before running.

The hole this closes: a scroll can be cast anywhere, and Vrenn sweeps his kit into a bag when they flee. So as the session was written there was no reason to return to the platform at all, and Route A's entire climax — retake the site, hold it for an hour — had nothing holding it up. This fixes it.

The ruling: planar travel only works where the boundary is thin. The ritual site sits on one such place, which is why the site is there at all. The House basement works for a different reason — amplification glyphs worked into the floor, boosting travelling magic on the back of the building's arcane infrastructure. Those are the only two viable casting spots the party knows of. Anywhere else the casting fails and the scroll is consumed.

Vrenn knows this and says so, in exactly his register — secondhand, flat, no theory. He is repeating something Morvek told him, not explaining planar physics. He volunteers it unprompted the moment anyone starts planning to cast elsewhere; see his follow-up line above.

The question a sharp player WILL ask, and the answer: "the package sent us here from a supply cupboard — that wasn't a thin place." Correct. Getting into the Abyss is easy; getting out is not. The constraint is on leaving, not arriving, which is both thematically right and exactly why a shipping operation would build here. It also means nothing about the Academy end needs special properties, which keeps Session 2 consistent as played.

Scope it to this layer. The thin-place requirement is about how deep Thanatos is, not a universal law of planar travel. That matters because A7 promises a future payoff — a Silas who eventually casts plane shift himself gets to choose his destination — and that payoff should not quietly acquire a second condition.

What this buys, beyond plugging the hole:

  • The two routes become symmetrical and the choice becomes clean: two thin places, one scroll, one circle. Vrenn's line names both, so the party has what it needs to choose.
  • It explains why the site is guarded at all. A thin spot is strategically valuable, which is why Morvek cares, why there are armanites, and why the north gate has something behind it.
  • It sharpens the ache on the fork. They will carry the rod off-plane and still need a thin place to use it. Deepens A7 rather than breaking it.
  • Be ready for the combined play: take the scroll to the House basement and cast it there. Both conditions are satisfied and it is a legitimate, clever solution. Do not block it.
  • Vrenn packing his kit now cuts the right way — the portable part is portable, the fixed part is not. Let that land when somebody works it out.

What it changes about the decision: the question is no longer "do we go back" — they must. It is where do we survive until dawn, and can we take the platform back when we do. Less agency on one axis, far more coherence on every other.

The Decision

The question is not "how do we escape." The only exit takes until morning to build, so the question is "where do we survive the next twelve hours" — and both routes are answers to it.

  • Route A — go to ground nearby and come back at dawn. Vrenn transcribes a fresh plane shift scroll in about an hour. Morvek arrives at dawn for it.
  • Route B — the house east. There is a way out inside it. It is not undefended, and Vrenn will not pretend it is a good idea.

The Heat — applies to both routes

The [[ritual_platform|platform]] is safe. Everything off it is not. Step past the moat and the air is an assault — the kind of heat that makes every hour a decision about water.

Vrenn, before anyone leaves the platform

"Fill everything you've got before you go. There's a cistern behind the bench — it's clean, it's the only clean thing here. There's nothing out there. Not water, not shade, not anything." (beat) "I've seen people come back from an hour out there. I've seen people not."

DM Only — the rule, and the ruling (DM, 2026-09-02)

There is no "the Abyss causes exhaustion" rule in 5e — the edition dropped 3.5's planar traits and its Abyss material is narrative. What applies is the DMG's Extreme Heat: at 100°F or above, a creature without access to drinkable water makes a Constitution save at the end of each hour or gains one level of exhaustion. Fire resistance or immunity is an automatic success. Medium or heavy armour gives disadvantage, which catches [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] in plate and [[guntrah|Guntrah]] in scale mail.

DM ruling: DC 7 (RAW is DC 5, which this party literally cannot fail). Waterskins hold five hours of fresh water each. While a character has water they do not roll at all.

Per-hour failure chances, computed against the actual sheets:

 in Tavian's auraoutside it
Guntrah (CON +9, disadv.)0%0% — cannot fail, ever
Silas (CON +3)0%15%
Tito (CON +1)10%25%
Tavian (CON +1, plate, disadv.)19% — always in his own aura, and still the worst odds at the table

Two corrections to the working assumptions. Silas cannot fail at all inside the aura — at +6 against DC 7 a natural 1 totals exactly 7, which meets it. He is only at risk if he strays. And Tito's odds more than double outside the aura, from 10% to 25%, so "Tito will probably be OK" is true only while he stays close.

Aura of Protection is only 10 feet, and this session keeps splitting them up — the gaps at the House, somebody scouting, Guntrah flying thirty feet up the way he did in Session 2. That is where the dice actually appear. Do not announce it; just track who is standing where.

Exposure by route: Route A is about four hours (roughly two out, two back — Leomund's tiny hut explicitly keeps the air inside "comfortable and dry regardless of the weather outside", so the eight-hour night is free). Route B is about two, plus whatever they spend inside the House, which is stone and should count as sheltered.

Why this is already self-balancing, and why the "multiple levels before a hard fight" worry mostly evaporates: a waterskin covers five hours and Route A exposes them for four. Tavian carries one and has the worst odds; Tito carries one. Silas and Guntrah carry none on their sheets and are precisely the two who cannot fail inside the aura. So a party that simply drinks takes no exhaustion at all, and the dice only come out when they run dry, split up, or forget. Against that, unwatered Tavian has a 17% chance of two or more levels across Route A — which is the outcome to avoid, and the waterskin is what avoids it.

If you want it to bite without being cruel, charge exertion rather than raising the DC: an hour of digging a shaft, or a fighting retreat across open ground, drinks a skin in half the time. That makes the dig-in bypass cost water as well as time and noise, which is exactly the right price for it.

Outs the party may find, all legitimate: fire resistance is an automatic success, so [[silas|Silas]]'s protection from energy covers one person, and Guntrah's artificer infusions include Resistant Armor, which would make him permanently immune on a long-rest swap. Let all of it work.

Exhaustion — 2014 track, confirmed campaign-wide (DM, 2026-09-02)

2014 rules for the whole campaign, so this is the 2014 exhaustion table: 1 disadvantage on ability checks · 2 speed halved · 3 disadvantage on attack rolls and saving throws · 4 hit point maximum halved · 5 speed 0 · 6 death. A long rest removes one level, provided the creature has also had food and drink. (Vrenn's sheet was built on the 2024 PHB by mistake and the DM is rebuilding it on 2014.)

The interaction to watch, and it is the dangerous one: casting plane shift from the scroll at dawn is an ability check. Exhaustion level 1 means Silas rolls the escape at disadvantage. That is the single worst outcome available in this session, and it is worth knowing before it happens rather than discovering it at the table.

The good news is that Route A self-heals, and this is the reassuring bit. The structure is: exposure out (about two hours) → a long rest in the tiny hut, which removes one level → exposure back (about two hours). So anything picked up on the way out is cleared before the return, and a character can realistically only arrive at the dawn casting with one level, and only after being unlucky twice. They have rations for the food-and-drink requirement — [[tito|Tito]] carries nine and [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] has his own.

Route B has no long rest in it, so exhaustion there does not clear. It is also only about two exposed hours, and one waterskin covers five.

Lore flag, low stakes: canon usually writes Thanatos as a cold, deathly layer rather than a hot one. Session 2 established red sky, a lava moat, smoke and heat at the table, and table canon wins — but a lore-literate player may raise it.

DM Only — where Vrenn gets water and food

The cistern is the answer, and it does double duty. There is a covered cistern on the platform, behind the workbench, kept clean. It is why the platform is habitable at all — which turns the "the platform is immune to the heat" ruling from a convenience into a reason. The site was built to be worked in, because somebody has to work in it.

Food arrives with the consignments. He is not fed so much as maintained: rations come in on the same cadence the goods go out, which is the delivery schedule already established by his own slip in Session 2 — "it's not time, I told you I needed another day." Morvek supplies him the way you keep a lamp topped up, and Vrenn has never once been asked whether it is enough.

What this buys you at the table: Vrenn's warning becomes actionable rather than atmospheric. "Fill everything you have" is something the party can act on in ten seconds, and a party that ignores it has made a choice rather than been ambushed by a rule. It also gives Route B a real cost — they leave the only water on the plane behind for a two-hour round trip.

Vrenn has one waterskin of his own (DM, 2026-09-02). If he goes with them, that is a fourth skin in the group and he will offer it before he is asked. He also needs less than they do: drow trance is four hours rather than eight.

Deliberately not decided: what happens to the cistern if the site is ever abandoned, and whether anyone has ever poisoned it. Both are available later; neither is needed now.

ROUTE A — Go to Ground, Come Back at Dawn

DM Only — the shape of the branch, and why it is not a non-event

[[silas|Silas]] has Leomund's Tiny Hut, and it is a ritual (character sheet, 2026-08-30) — no slot, no preparation needed, available every night whatever he prepped. He does not have Rope Trick. So do not design this branch around whether they can survive the night. They can. Design it around the four things the hut cannot do:

  1. Ten minutes to cast. A ritual is not an escape button. They cannot raise it with something crossing the bridge at them; they have to get clear first.
  2. The dome is opaque and coloured from the outside. It is a twenty-foot hemisphere of solid colour standing in a landscape with nothing else in it. Placement is the entire puzzle.
  3. It is immobile, and it ends if Silas leaves it. Once it is up, that is where they are, all night, and nobody scouts out and back.
  4. Eight hours, exactly. A long rest is eight hours. With roughly twelve hours until dawn, that leaves about two hours of exposure at each end — and the hut cannot cover both.

So the branch is three scenes: the break (getting off the platform), the night (where the dome goes, and what walks past it), and the return (taking the platform back and holding it for an hour). Nothing gets through the hut. Everything happens on either side of it.

Expect Alarm too — also a ritual, also 8 hours. Free early warning, and fine; let it work.

A1. The Break — getting off the platform

The site is an island. One permanent stone bridge crosses the lava moat, and that is where the hooves are coming from. The moat is roughly fifteen feet across and lit from below.

DM Only — running it

The bridge is contested by definition, so the interesting question is whether they cross the moat somewhere else. Fifteen feet over open lava is a real jump and they can mostly make it: [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] has STR 16 and a running long jump of sixteen feet, [[tito|Tito]] has Mirthful Leaps (+1d8), [[guntrah|Guntrah]] flies, and Silas has Misty Step, Fly and Feather Fall. Silas himself is the problem — STR 7, AC 11, and the one person who absolutely must not fall in. Let the party solve that; it is a good three-minute problem and it makes Tavian and Guntrah useful in a scene that would otherwise belong to the casters.

Vrenn crosses without fuss. He has done it a thousand times and knows a place where the lip is closer. If the party never asks him, he does not volunteer it until somebody is about to do something stupid.

The wall runs out during this scene — ten minutes total, and most of it is already spent. It was cast on round one of a roughly six-round fight, so call it nine minutes left when Session 3 opens. Nothing later in Route A may depend on it still standing: by the time they come back at dawn it is twelve hours gone.

One real decision hiding in that, though. Wall of stone's own text: "If you maintain your concentration on this spell for its whole duration, the wall becomes permanent and can't be dispelled." So [[silas|Silas]] can spend the next nine minutes concentrating and leave a permanent sixty-foot U of stone standing on the platform — which is a serious gift to A3's hour-long defence, and it is exactly the sort of thing this party is smart enough to work out. The cost is that he can cast nothing else that needs concentration while they are breaking out, and he keeps burning the 5th-level slot he would rather have back. Do not prompt it. If they take it, A3 gets much easier and they earned it; if they drop it, the wall is gone and A3 is the fight as written.

If they try to fight the forward patrol instead: let them, briefly, and let it go badly enough to be informative. Three or four armanites in the open with no wall is a genuinely dangerous fight for a party with no slots and no healing. The scene's lesson is that the platform is not holdable tonight.

A2. The Night

The ground beyond the moat — read-aloud material

Off the outcrop, the land is a low rolling plain of ash and slag, shin-deep in places, grey-black and soft underfoot. Vents breathe smoke out of it at intervals, which is where all that haze comes from — it hangs at chest height and thins as it rises, so anything standing on the ground can see about forty feet, and anything above it can see for miles.

The plain is not empty. Long low spines of cooled slag run across it toward the mountains, ten to twenty feet high, hard underfoot and dark. Scattered between them: the spoil of old workings nobody has touched in a long time — collapsed shafts, the stubs of retaining walls, a sink deep enough to stand in and not be seen.

And the ash takes footprints, and holds them.

DM Only — terrain is a PROPOSAL, swap freely

Everything above is invented to fit what the recording already established (desolate, smoke, rumbling underfoot, distant screaming, mountains barely visible, one stone bridge, a causeway south). It is built to do three specific jobs, so if you replace it, keep the jobs:

  • Cover exists in specific places, not everywhere — so choosing where to camp is a real decision instead of a formality.
  • Behind a slag ridge or down in the sink, the dome is not visible from the platform. Out on the open ash, it is visible from a long way off. That is the whole puzzle.
  • The ash holds a trail. Four people and a construct walking out leave a line pointing straight at wherever they stopped. The answer is available and unstated: walk the slag ridges, which are hard and take no print. Let them find it; do not offer it.

The structure east is visible from the ridge tops — that is how Route B gets offered to the eye rather than only in dialogue. Do not detail it; it is not designed.

Roughly twelve hours until Morvek comes. The hut covers eight of them.

DM Only — the night, in three beats

Beat 1 — they find the bodies. While the party is still out in the open, or just as the dome goes up, noise carries from the platform: the main body arriving and finding two of their own dead on the working floor. Play it entirely as sound. It tells the party, accurately, that going back is now a problem.

On the corpses (DM ruling, 2026-08-30). They stay where they fall and they rot where they lie, and bodies scattered across the plain in various states of decay are completely unremarkable here — this place chews through its own constantly. Two useful consequences: out on the ash the party can use carcasses as cover and as a reason a search passes them by, and nothing about a dead armanite is inherently an alarm. On the platform it reads differently, because two guards dead on the floor of a working shipping site is not weather, it is an event. Note also that the armanites themselves are established as not intelligent — they may well not draw any conclusion at all. It is whoever supervises them who notices, and that is a lead the party can pull on if they ever ask Vrenn who checks up on these things.

Beat 2 — the search. Something quarters the ash field for a couple of hours. This is where placement pays out:

  • Well placed (behind a ridge, in the sink, trail covered) — they hear it pass, twice, and nothing happens. Reward that with a clean night and say so plainly; do not manufacture a scare they earned the right to skip.
  • Badly placed (open ash, or a trail leading to the door) — it finds the dome. It cannot get in. So it sits down outside and waits. That is far worse than a fight and costs no hit points: eight hours of something breathing on the other side of a wall they cannot see through, and it is still there in the morning. The long rest still happens. The exit is now the problem.

Beat 3 — Vrenn talks, if he came. This is the alcove scene the original design always wanted and never got: a defensible dead-end with nothing to do for hours. It is the one place he opens up, and it should be the only quiet in the session. Keep to his budget — the fork, the schedule, Morvek's name, "there's more of us now than there used to be." Nothing about himself, nothing about the sister, nothing about the Grave Token beyond the one flat sentence. If they push kindly and well, give them one thing that is not on the list, and make it small.

If Vrenn stayed behind, the party spends the night not knowing whether he is alive, and the return in A3 carries that. Both versions are good; do not steer.

They get the long rest. Full HP, full slots, Tito's four Luck points back, and — the one that decides the ending — Guntrah's five uses of Flash of Genius. That is the reward for solving placement, and it is what makes A6 winnable. Make them believe they might not get it; then give it to them.

A3. The Return — taking the platform back

They need to be at the workbench with a full hour of dark left. Coming back at first light is coming back too late.

DM Only — this is Route A's actual climax, not the casting

The platform is occupied. Two of their own turned up dead here; leave a standing watch on it — two or three armanites, not fifteen, posted rather than patrolling. The party has to take it back quietly, because noise at this hour brings the rest of them, and then hold it for an hour while Vrenn writes.

That is the encounter: a defence with a clock and a win condition that is not "kill everything." It is the right fight for this party and the right use of what they have — and note that this time you know wall of stone and wall of force are coming, so plan around them rather than being blindsided the way Session 2 was. Wall of force in particular cannot be broken through at all, so give the encounter a reason they cannot simply seal themselves in and wait: Vrenn has to be at the bench, the bench is where the bench is, and something has to be able to reach it or there is no scene.

B4a. Detect Magic and Dispel Magic at the House

DM Only — have this ready; they will cast both

The single most useful RAW fact: detect magic does not pass through a foot of stone. Casting it at the outside wall gets nothing. It is 30 ft, and it is blocked by 1 ft of stone, 1 inch of common metal, a thin sheet of lead, or 3 ft of wood or dirt. So the spell is an inside tool, room by room, and the party cannot X-ray the building from the ash.

What pings once they are inside, by school:

  • The two guards — Transmutation. This is the big one. Change Shape is magical, so detect magic makes them, and it should be allowed to work — a party that spends a spell to be careful has earned the warning.
  • The front door glyph — Abjuration.
  • The shriek-ward on the basement stair — Abjuration.
  • The north gate — Abjuration, and unmistakably the strongest thing in the building. See the warning below.
  • The ritual circle — Conjuration, strong. And with it the amplification glyphs worked into the floor around it, which is why the basement can be cast from at all. A good detect magic read should show the circle and the glyphs as one connected working rather than two things in a room.
  • Above the basement — use this. Faint Transmutation and Enchantment on primed goods waiting to ship, in the same style as the corrupted items planted around the Academy. That is the load-bearing Session 2 clue nobody looked at, arriving through a spell the party will cast unprompted. Also up there: Morvek's own quarters — heavy Necromancy, and the correct read is do not open that door.

Dispel magic — three rulings to make now:

  1. The glyph and the shriek-ward are 3rd level, so they dispel automatically. Deliberate: both are meant to be solvable, and this is the reward for scouting rather than a dice-roll tax.
  2. The north gate ward is 7th level, and dispelling it is a catastrophe — it opens the gate. This is a fair trap rather than a cruel one, because the aura reads as the largest thing in the building and the bars are visible. A party that reflexively dispels everything it detects will have a very bad moment, and they will have earned it honestly.
  3. The circle is NOT dispellable. Rule it a permanent planar anchor rather than a spell effect. Otherwise one clumsy dispel magic destroys the party's only way out, which is a session-ending accident nobody enjoys.

And the one they will try that does not work: dispel magic does not strip Change Shape. It ends spells, and Change Shape is an innate ability, not a spell. So the shape of it is: detect magic finds them, and nothing the party has un-disguises them. Knowing is the whole prize.

Note on alarm rather than the Alert feat: [[silas|Silas]] has alarm as a ritual, 8 hours, a 20-ft cube. It warns when something enters the warded area — it does not pierce a disguise, so it will not reveal the guards. What it will do is deny them a clean jump: if the party wards the stair or their own position, the guards crossing it trips the warning and the ambush becomes an ordinary fight. That is a fair reward for a free ritual.

Check numbers: your table already ruled +9 for Silas on the scroll ability check, so use +9 for dispel magic too and stay consistent. DC is 10 + the spell's level, so DC 17 for the gate.

B5. The Guard

DM Only — TWO ONI (DM, 2026-09-01). Replaces the quarantined glabrezu block.

Two oni, CR 7 each, disguised as captives and working in the basement alongside everything else. Change Shape is RAW — an oni can take Small or Medium humanoid form at will, so this needs no homebrew at all, and it is exactly what the ability is for.

Encounter maths: 3,900 XP each, 7,800 doubled to 11,700 adjusted for a pair. Four level-10s sit at Hard 7,600 and Deadly 11,200, so this is just past Deadly — genuinely harder than the glabrezu was, and the right side of "tough but beatable." Add the B7 waves on a timer rather than more bodies at the start.

The best thing about this choice, and the reason to lean on it: two more captives in the basement is a question the party will ask, and it reinforces everything they already believe about this place after meeting Vrenn. They may well try to free them. The party's decency is the ambush trigger, which is far better than a door opening.

Tells, for Insight rather than a flat DC. Give a good roll something concrete rather than "you sense something is wrong":

  • They are working, and the work is not producing anything. Nobody has moved a finished piece all the time the party has been watching.
  • They are not hurt. Everyone else down here is.
  • Their manacles fit badly — loose at the wrist, and not worn into the skin the way Vrenn's are.
  • They are calm. Vrenn flinches; these two do not, and they are between the party and the stairs without ever seeming to move there.
  • Heavy footfall for their size. An oni in Medium form still walks like something Large.

Tactics worth knowing before the table: AC 16, ~110 HP, two attacks, darkness and invisibility innately, and Regeneration 10 — which stops on any turn they have taken acid or fire damage. The party has both in quantity ([[tito|Tito]] switched to acid against the armanites in Session 2 and will remember; [[silas|Silas]] has fireball and wall of fire), so the counter exists and they have already shown they hunt for it. Pull the actual block; treat these numbers as the shape rather than the record.

If they are spotted early by detect magic, do not punish it. Let them open the fight on their terms once — they will still be facing a just-past-Deadly encounter with the stairs at their back.

Superseded: the planar-bound glabrezu, its bargain, and the binding-sigil-in-the-floor hook are retired (DM has no glabrezu miniature). One thing worth carrying across if you want it: the glabrezu was going to be the second independent source who had never heard of Thatch. The oni can serve that just as well if the party thinks to ask.

B6. The North Gate

Barred and warded beneath the skull crest. Nothing behind it makes a sound — until the room gets loud, and then something very large adjusts its weight.

DM Only — the loaded gun

Behind the gate is a beast too dangerous even for this realm (DM, 2026-08-31). It is an in-case-of-emergency measure, it belongs to the landlord rather than to Morvek, and it exists to apply pressure — not to be fought.

The rule that makes it work: it cannot leave the basement. So releasing it does not add a monster to the room, it makes the only exit unreachable, because the circle is in here with it. Threat and escape occupy the same floor. That also explains why the thing is caged beside the teleporter rather than at the front door: it is not defending the building, it is defending the asset — which quietly tells the party what the circle is worth.

Make it one of the things they already heard. Session 2's arrival gave them "loud, very distant screaming monsters", plural and unexplained, and it was never paid off. This is one of those, caught and kept. Free continuity, and the party has a sense of the scale of it before they ever look at the door.

Who can open it: the guards cannot — the ward is Morvek's and is not theirs to touch. But they know exactly what is in there, and an oni that has dropped its disguise will happily threaten to open the gate once the pretence is over. A lie the party has no way to check, from something that has already lied to them once about a much more basic thing. If the pressure ever needs to be real, Morvek or the landlord's steward can open it from outside.

The better gift: the party can open it themselves. A group losing badly might crack that gate deliberately for the chaos and run for the circle. It is a genuinely terrible option, available at all times, and that is the most interesting kind.

Do not stat it. The moment it has a block somebody will want to fight it. If it comes out, the scene is a flight, not a fight — the same rule as the thing that crosses the plain. If you want a block in your back pocket, Nightwalker (CR 20, MToF) is the pick: undead, so it belongs in a lich's basement, and canonically a thing that other undead are afraid of.

Sensory detail: the wear marks are on the inside of the bars, and the stone around the frame is polished smooth where it has pressed.

B7. Running the Climax

DM Only

The braziers are the clock. Lighting and feeding all four takes long enough to matter — tune the number of rounds to how the fight is going, but decide the shape before the table: it is a defence with an objective, and the win condition is not "kill everything".

Escalation comes from OUTSIDE, down the stairs — not from the ground floor. The House is empty above (B3), so there are no leftover mooks to fall back on. What arrives is what the party's own noise pulled off the plain: a vargouille flock, an armanite patrol, whatever was already wandering out there. That is a much tighter design than mopping up stragglers — their noise discipline across the whole approach (the glyph, the ward, knock, an hour of digging in the open) decides what shows up, and none of it needs deciding in advance.

If they came in through the hole, things come down the hole. Same principle, better geometry — they chose where it was.

Mind the totals. Two oni are already 11,700 adjusted, past Deadly on their own, so arrivals should be small and late rather than large and early. Four ghasts landing at once on top of the pair prices out around 20,000 adjusted, which is not a fight, it is an obituary.

Escalate on the timer, not on damage. If they are winning too easily the answer is more arrivals, not tougher ones. The horror of this place is quantity.

Watch the west grates for a cheap "something is already inside with us" beat between waves.

B8. The Circle, and Where It Lands

DM Only

Ruled planar, and the reason it is guarded: a teleportation circle is same-plane by RAW, so this one is the landlord's — or Morvek's — private line off the layer. Do not argue the rules at the table; it is the Abyss and it is theirs.

They can use it. They cannot aim it. Same limitation as Vrenn's scroll, for the same reason: it is keyed to where this operation ships. Both routes therefore land in Neverwinter Wood, and you prep one arrival — see A8 for the "Neverwinter is a region as well as a city" reading, which works identically here.

Scrapped: the consequence budget for Route B is spent on the basement (and on what they may have let out of a gate), which is a better cost than a different patch of forest.

B9. Traps

DM Only — two, and neither does damage

A lich with a standing staff and mindless guards does not trap his own corridors — he would lose servants to them. Traps belong where strangers arrive and on the one door worth protecting.

  1. The glyph of warding on the front door (B2). An alarm, not a blast. It does not hurt anybody; it tells the house.
  2. A shriek-ward on the basement stair. This is the branch's difficulty dial, and with the House empty above it does two things rather than one: it wakes the basement, so the oni are in true form and waiting and there is no surprise on the party's terms; and it starts the clock, because that noise carries across a plain full of things that come towards noise. Find and dispel it and the basement is a private problem with a clean first round.

Free tension needing no design: Tavian in plate on stone stairs. That is a Stealth check, not a trap.

B10. Vrenn on This Route

DM Only — and the quiet moral difference nobody will notice at first

He walks them out, points out the weak spots, and shows them where the basement stair is. Then he goes back, because he intends to be at his workbench when Morvek arrives, and that is simply what he does. Let that departure be undramatic; he does not make a speech about it.

Here is the thing worth noticing: Route B costs him nothing. He has all night to make Morvek's scroll as normal, so he meets dawn with the work done. Route A saves the party and wrecks his morning; Route B is riskier for them and leaves him unharmed. He will state it once, flatly, without pleading, and then let them choose:

If they are weighing the two

"If you go to the house, I still have tomorrow's work to give him. If you take the scroll, I don't."

He does not repeat it and he does not argue. If they take the scroll anyway he hands it over exactly as written in A5.
