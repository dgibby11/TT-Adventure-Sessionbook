Session 3 — Before Dawn
Status

Party
[[silas|Silas]] · [[guntrah|Guntrah]] · [[tito|Tito]] · [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] — and, from the first minute to the last, [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]]
Opens
Mid-scene on the [[ritual_platform|ritual platform]], exactly where [[session_2|Session 2]] stopped
Ends
In the basement of [[the_house|the house]], holding hands inside a live planar circle, with something a round away from coming through a grate. Where they landed is Session 4.
Runs
Roughly four or five hours of in-world time, from the platform to the far side of the circle. Nobody rested. Nobody healed until it was nearly too late.

Premise: no time had passed. No short rest, no long rest, nothing healed since the morning of Commencement. Two of the hooved things dead on the stone, most of a wall of stone still standing with a panel knocked out of it, a spent plane shift scroll, and a drow who had just watched four strangers burn the only thing he owned that was worth anything.

Before Any of It — Voss's List

Handed over off the clock, as a flashback: [[ellery_voss|Voss]] caught them in a corridor on graduation morning and pressed a folded slip of paper on them. Six names, six cities, one of them struck out. It has been in [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]]'s pocket since Commencement, and it is the only lifeline any of them has to anybody who is not standing on this rock.

Tavian mentioned it twice during the session — "I might need you to read this for me" — and there was never a quiet enough minute. It still has not been read out. Full list in [[session_2|Session 2]].

He Gives His Name

[[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] put a hand on [[guntrah|Guntrah]]'s shoulder and spent 21 points of Lay on Hands on him — the first thing anybody did. And the drow, already moving, said it:

As played

"Friend — my name's Vrenn. Listen, we've got about thirty seconds to get out of here. We can find some place nearby to lay low and hide, or there's a house about an hour's walk. We can go there. Let me grab my stuff."

He ran back to the workbench and started shovelling vellum, inks and components into a satchel. Guntrah went with him and took the Help action to get more of it into the bag — and got a good look at what was on that bench while he did it: valuable gems, powders and components nobody recognised, a lot of it, all of it high quality. Whatever else Vrenn is, he has been working with expensive material for a long time.

Tavian stood facing the bridge the whole time, watching for what was coming. He rolled a 5 and heard hooves he could not place. He was fairly sure they were already here.

Across the Moat

The bridge is the only way on or off the platform and the bridge is where the noise was coming from. So Vrenn went the other way — around behind the two idol-busts, to a stretch where the lava moat narrows from fifteen feet to about twelve, and where the stone is scuffed enough that this is plainly not the first time somebody has jumped it.

  • Before anyone jumped, Vrenn filled his waterskin at the covered cistern under his workbench and told them all to do the same. "Take as much water as you can, but quickly." This turned out to matter within the hour.
  • Vrenn crossed by misty step. [[silas|Silas]] worked out that a STR 7 wizard clears seven feet of a twelve-foot gap and misty stepped after him. [[tito|Tito]] used Mirthful Leaps. Guntrah has Winged Boots. Tavian jumped it.
  • Tito healed himself for 2d8+4 on the far side — the first-level cure wounds he had been sitting on since one of [[hooved_demons|the hooved things]] hit him for 21.

The Ash Plain

Off the outcrop the visibility improves — the lava was most of what was blinding them — but a haze still sits eight to ten feet deep over the ground. What they could see: hoofprints, a great many of them, of the kind the things on the platform would leave; shapes moving perhaps half a mile out; cracks in the ground venting steam and worse; corpses; and no sign of anything built by anybody.

They could hear fighting and screaming in the distance, in more than one direction, all the time. It smells of sulphur. The party settled on their own read without needing a roll: this is one of the layers of the Abyss — fiendish rather than infernal, no devils, no cities, wilderness and desolation in every direction.

And it is hot. The moment they left the platform the air became genuinely hard to work in — stifling, heavy, and much worse inside Tavian's plate.

Behind the Rocks — Vrenn Answers Questions

A minute's run brought them to a low outcrop where they could get down out of sight, and Vrenn finally stopped and explained himself. He apologised for the rush first: if more of them had arrived and he had been standing alone next to two dead guards, they would have assumed he did it and killed him. So he had to leave with them. And if they had stayed, they would have been killed too.

The briefing, as played

"That scroll I gave you was the only one I had. My master has all of these things for me to do — it's a huge list. I'm making another one, but it won't be ready till tomorrow. I've done all the preparations. I can't finalise it until I have the ability again."

Who's your master? — "Morvek. M-O-R-V-E-K. He's a lich. He's the one having me do all this. And he owns me. Don't go looking for him — he's too powerful. He's got me trapped."

Why can't you come? — "He has my—" (he has to reach for the word) "—it's basically the Abyssal equivalent of a soul coin. He has another name for it."

Then the two options, exactly as he saw them:

  1. Wait out here until dawn and hope nothing finds them, and he finishes the scroll in the morning.
  2. The house — an hour east. Morvek is not there tonight. His things are. Morvek is a tenant in it; it belongs to a minor demon who is away and comes back tomorrow to collect. And the basement is full of arcane material, and Morvek comes and goes through that basement, which means there is a way out of it.

Four other things came out of him in that conversation, unprompted or nearly so, and all four are load-bearing:

  • The scroll did not fail because of Silas. "It wasn't his fault. The quill I used was my magic quill — I've only just learned that means only I can cast from it. Tomorrow I'll use a standard enchanted quill and anybody can use it." Silas had spent the whole intervening scene assuming he had blown it.
  • He has sent about six of those packages. Asked who the box was for, he remembered it: addressed to Thatch, the groundskeeper. He writes down the words he is given. He has never met the man, does not know who he is, and is not responsible for what happens after a crate leaves.
  • He has never heard of the Academy. Told they came from FAIL Academy, he had no reaction at all. Told they came from Faerûn, he immediately produced a steel tuning fork from his bag — steel being the material keyed to the Material Plane — and explained it: Morvek gave it to him because Morvek wanted things going to the Material Plane. "He told me Neverwinter, so I made a scroll for that. Then he gave me a picture of this other place, and I made a scroll for that."
  • The house has traps. Skeleton servants wander the ground floor doing enchanted chores and do not attack. Upstairs is Morvek's door — "heavily guarded with some sort of arcane enchantments, and I'm sure there's an actual lock as well. Don't dare try to go in there."

Guntrah asked for Insight on "if we're successful, he won't know." He read as legitimate and trustworthy.

The Decision

[[silas|Silas]] offered Leomund's Tiny Hut as the alternative and it got a fair hearing — opaque, colourable, eight hours, nothing gets in. It died on two objections, neither of them the DM's: a dome that appears on an empty plain is a thing that gets investigated, and Vrenn cannot simply take a long rest to order in the middle of the afternoon surrounded by strangers after a firefight. Tito said what everyone was thinking — the house is an hour away, it sounds safer, and there is a lich's basement full of arcane goods in it. They went east.

The Walk

Marching order: Vrenn leading, Tavian near the front, the two casters in the middle, Guntrah at the back. Vrenn hugged what cover there was. Tavian's plate announced them the whole way and there was nothing anybody could do about it — find steed was floated and abandoned, and Vrenn's skill empowerment was useless because nobody in the party is proficient in Stealth.

Twenty to thirty minutes in, everyone rolled badly at once — 8, 5, 3, an 8 for Ollie, and a natural 1 for Vrenn — and one of [[hooved_demons|the hooved things]] came out of the haze about seventy or eighty feet off Silas's flank, saw the whole group, shouted something in Abyssal, and ran the other way to fetch the rest. Nobody had a reaction that could stop it.

The hide

They ran for a five-foot rock ledge and went flat. Before any of it mattered, Vrenn upcast invisibility at 6th level and made all five of them invisible — which turned a disaster into a group check they passed comfortably (22, natural 20, 20, and Tavian scraping through on enhance ability). His instruction was one line: movement makes sound; stay exactly where you are.

Six or seven of them came back. They quartered the ground where the group had been, looking for tracks, shouting at each other in Abyssal. One came within fifteen or twenty feet of the ledge and did not find them.

And then the search failed

They gave up. They started blaming each other. And then they started killing each other — clawing and stamping until six of them were dead on the ash and one was left standing, bloodied, thirty or forty feet away.

Guntrah broke invisibility to put two crossbow bolts and an Arcane Jolt into the survivor — 13, then 9, then 21 to finish it. Thirty-five damage in two shots, and the last of them went down on top of the other six. Tito's verdict: best not to leave any alive. Nothing on them was worth looting; they are natural weapons and nothing else.

Silas re-hid Guntrah by casting invisibility at 3rd level (himself plus one), the party took hold of a rope and went single file, and they covered the last twenty-five to thirty minutes without further incident.

Water

At the one-hour mark the heat came due. Ruled at the table: a standard waterskin is four pints, five hours' worth of drinking in this atmosphere, and everybody consumes one per hour or takes a level of exhaustion.

  • Tito, Tavian and Silas had containers. Guntrah has no backpack at all — a thing nobody had noticed until they went looking for a waterskin — and Ollie does not carry water. Tavian shared his, which put him down to three hours' worth.
  • Vrenn had filled his own at the cistern and was fine.
  • Standing consequence: Guntrah is buying an adventuring pack at the first opportunity, and wants it built into Ollie — Ollie is now his backpack, and is getting a water spout.

The House

Vrenn called it a house. It is not one.

The approach — as described at the table

An outpost, or a very small fortress — a defensive building, not a home. Two floors, maybe a dozen rooms, cracked and crumbling and not really livable. No watchtower, nobody looking out, no lights. From the angle they came in on, a flat wall with no windows at all.

Vrenn talked as they walked in: he has been here a few times, always with Morvek, always told exactly what to do. He is not allowed in the basement and has never been in it — but he has heard something down there, loud, some kind of creature, and Morvek always arrives and leaves from down there, which is the whole reason he thinks there is a way out.

Cracks in the masonry big enough for the smaller ones to squeeze through were pointed out and never used. They went to the back door.

Getting in

  • Guntrah rolled Arcana 17 on the lock and confirmed it is not magical, just a lock.
  • Nobody in the party has usable Sleight of Hand (+1, +1, +4, −2) — but Guntrah's All-Purpose Tool becomes a set of thieves' tools as an action, and Vrenn is at +6. Ruled at the table: an attuned All-Purpose Tool can be handed to somebody else while keeping its shape. Vrenn rolled 19+6 and opened it, and did it again on the basement door at 13+6.
  • Guntrah went in last and locked the back door behind them.
  • Inside: a back hallway, something barely qualifying as a kitchen, a couple of shut doors, and a door that used to be iron-banded and is now mostly rotted wood. One skeleton was visible further down the hall, carrying something, and it did not look at them once.

Before the stairs, Vrenn gave them the piece of the world nobody had asked for:

Why the platform exists

"Ever since the Spellplague there's very limited planar travel. It's almost impossible to go from one plane to another except for the spots where the veil is thinnest. That's one of those spots. That's why that area was constructed. That's why I was working there. And I'd assume — because Morvek travels from this house — that this is another one."

And, flatly: "I cannot go with you. I would love to. If I were to leave the plane he would know immediately, and he would follow me, and he would kill all of you."

He also told them, without being asked, that if he dies they are stuck: he is the only one who can make them another scroll. Keep me alive. They put him in the middle.

Down the Stairs

Guntrah took the front, Tavian the back, Ollie was left at the top of the stairs under a standing order — if something happens up here, come in, close the door, and run down. Everyone was still invisible.

The basement is one room under the entire building: fifty to sixty feet across, seventy to eighty long, fifteen-foot ceiling, four pillars, torchlight. And in it —

  • A very large inlaid circle in the centre of the floor, fifteen to twenty feet across, worked in a different material rather than carved — and faintly glowing. It looks like the circle on the platform, except that one was dead and this one is not.
  • Four small altars with bowls, out near the pillars at the corners.
  • Two humans in tattered clothes, hunched over workbenches, working — in exactly the pose the party first found Vrenn in.
  • At the back, badly lit: a skull mounted on a wall that turns out not to be a wall.

Vrenn talks them past the first one

Vrenn rolled a natural 1 on Stealth and stumbled at the bottom of the stairs. The nearer worker looked up: "What are you doing here?" Vrenn dropped invisibility on himself, Tavian and Tito (he could not drop it selectively for himself alone) and lied: "Morvek sent me to get more vellum. I found these two out in the wastes and they're going to help me." Deception +0 on an 18, plus Guntrah's Flash of Genius for 23. It worked. "Take what you need."

The thing Guntrah saw

Guntrah had sidestepped left out of the doorway while that was happening and rolled Perception 15 — and caught the second worker, off to the side of the stairs, watching him move while he was invisible.

He kept it to himself and readied an action: if that one makes a violent move toward anybody, the crossbow goes off first. Nobody else in the party knew. He spent a genuine minute weighing it out loud in character terms — are these two more prisoners like Vrenn, who have no stake in any of this, or are they the lich's? Shooting first would be murder if he was wrong.

The Circle

Vrenn whispered them a job: go and look like you're inspecting the circle, and don't attract attention. They did, and between them they took it apart.

  • Guntrah reads Abyssal — which came up for the first time here, and would have been extremely useful an hour earlier. Arcana 24: it is not writing, it is a codex of Abyssal characters worked into the inlay. He has seen teleportation circles at the Academy and this is a version of one — but not a teleportation circle. Something else. And the geometry points at the four bowls: they are the key to switching it on.
  • Silas rolled a natural 20 for 29. He has read a plane shift scroll with his own eyes and the symbols match it. This is planar travel, he is certain of it, it is incomplete and needs an activation trigger, the bowls are that trigger — and the whole apparatus may be an amplifier: not a thin place, but a machine that makes casting work where it otherwise would not.
  • Tavian drew [[witness|Witness]] and asked it for help, using the sword's Intelligence 17 in place of his own 4. He rolled 12 and got nothing, but the blade answered — established at the table as the register in which Witness reliably speaks.

The tuning fork changes hands

Silas worked out what he was missing: he has components enough for the four bowls, but the fork is the spell's direction and Vrenn has it. Invisible, he whispered for it — Stealth 12 plus a Flash of Genius for 17 — and Vrenn, who could not see him and had to take it on trust, hesitated badly and then handed it over.

The handoff

"Did you figure out how this works?"

"I'm pretty sure I did."

He gives him the fork. "I think I've resigned to my fate."

Tavian saw the fork come out of the bag and vanish and drew the correct conclusion. Tito rolled a 3 and missed the whole thing. Vrenn announced they had what they needed and started walking back to the stairs — let's go tell Morvek — and Silas, still invisible, caught his arm to stop him.

The Grate

And with everybody stopped and turned around, they noticed the back of the room properly for the first time. The skull is mounted on a grate. Behind the grate is pitch black. And out of it came a low guttural snarl.

Both workers laughed. It did not sound human.

The turn

"I think you should probably leave now."

"Why?"

"You're not supposed to be here."

"But we're helping Vrenn."

"No, you're not."

Tito said "Silas, get it going." Silas started pulling components. And Guntrah said fuck this and put two bolts into the one who had been watching him — 19, then 16, 35 damage before initiative was even rolled.

It changed shape. They were oni — and the party knew the word, so the DM used it.

The Fight

Both picked up glaives from their benches. Silas took a crit and a second hit in the opening exchange for 34 damage before he had acted once, and every single round after that was a race between four bowls and two very intelligent monsters who knew exactly what was being attempted and had been hired to stop it.

The shape of it:

  • The bowls were the clock. Each one costs an action or a bonus action, and the spell cannot fire until all four are charged and everyone is within five feet and touching.
  • Silas did two on his first turn — misty step out of the glaive's reach, one bowl by hand, one by mage hand — and then spent the rest of the fight being hunted.
  • Vrenn fought. Nobody asked him to. He turned around at the foot of the stairs, made a visible snap decision — well, shit, if I'm going to die I might as well help — and upcast thunderwave into a corner for 28. Tavian nodded at him. Tavian also said, out loud, come with us.
  • Then Vrenn changed jobs. He cast haste on himself and spent two turns emptying every loose component in the room into his satchel — deliberately, so that nobody else could follow them through the circle for a good long while. On the last of those turns he saw what Silas was doing, understood it, and charged the third bowl himself.
  • The oni were built to be nasty: glaive on one, claws with necrotic on the other, flight, at-will bonus-action invisibility, and truesight — the party's invisibility was worth nothing to them the whole fight.
  • Silas went down and stayed down. Claws took him to a lethal blow, Relentless Endurance put him back at 1, and then the glaive found him with a natural 20. Two successful death saves (14, 10) while the fight went on above him.
  • Tavian emptied the Ring of the Ram in one shot — all three charges, one attack roll at +7 with a Bardic d10 behind it for 21, 22 force damage and the oni thrown fifteen feet under a bench. The DM's aside: another few feet and it would have gone through the wall and brought the building down.
  • Tavian killed the first oni with two hits and two upcast Divine Smites, then Inspiring Smite for 20 temporary HP into Tito.
  • Tito killed the second — Unsettling Words to sandbag the save, then a 4th-level blight for 30 necrotic. His chromatic orb earlier in the fight came in noticeably under, which is real intel: cold is a bad choice against these.
  • Guntrah worked with greataxe and Arcane Jolt throughout, brought Ollie down the stairs into the fight, and used shield to bounce the surviving oni's last swings off AC 23.

How It Ends

Every time an oni died, the thing behind the grate hit it harder. When the second one went down the DM rolled a d6 in the open for how many rounds the grate had left.

He rolled a 1

Three bowls charged. One to go. Silas unconscious inside the circle. And one round before whatever that is comes through.

Out of initiative, one action each. Tavian used Lay on Hands to get Silas to 1 hit point. Vrenn ran the last component to the last bowl and cast.

He needed a 5 or better. He rolled an 18.

Everybody grabbed hold of everybody. The circle fired.

And Vrenn went with them — not because he chose it, but because by then he had no choice left at all. Where they came out is the opening of Session 4.

DM Only — What Actually Happened at the Table (reconciled 2026-09-03)

The prose above is written from the transcript. This is the record of divergence from the plan. Nothing has been deleted — unplayed design is preserved in the Carried Forward blocks below.

  • Route B, chosen fast and never revisited. The decision took about ten minutes of table time and turned on two player-generated objections to the hut (a dome on an open plain gets investigated; Vrenn cannot long-rest to order after a firefight). Route A was never entered. It is entirely intact and unspent — see the Carried Forward block.
  • The whole session ran in one night. The dawn deadline never became a real constraint, because the circle worked. "Before Dawn" turned out to be about four or five hours, not twelve.
  • THE BIG ONE — Vrenn left the plane. The locked design was "he will leave the site, he will not leave the plane." At the table the DM ruled the opposite, on the fiction rather than by accident: his bridges were burned the moment he took a side in that basement, and with one round on the clock he had no choice but to go. This is now the single largest live thread in the campaign — see the Session 4 block below, because it is a decision that has to be made before the next session opens, not during it.
  • The Session 2 scroll failure was retconned in-fiction, deliberately. Vrenn scribed it with his own magic quill and only he could have cast from it. Silas's natural 1 still happened mechanically; in-world it was never his fault, and the DM chose to give the player that back. Silas had been carrying it — he said out loud that he thought it was him. Excellent beat; keep the retcon.
  • The heat rule changed. Designed as DC 7 CON per hour with waterskins as an exemption. Played as: one drink per hour or a level of exhaustion, a waterskin holding five hours' worth — no roll at all. This is simpler and better at the table. Use the played version going forward.
  • The road encounter went to the armanites, and then went sideways in the party's favour. The vargouilles and the thing that screams were never used. The armanite pair became a spot-and-fetch: one scout, seven back. They then turned on each other and killed six of their own — a DM improvisation that paid off the "they are not clever" characterisation from Session 2 perfectly and handed the party a free kill.
  • The hour's walk did NOT become a short rest. The road encounter broke it, exactly as the design anticipated. The party went into the basement on Session 2's fumes and it very nearly cost them Silas. The design flag was correct and the valves were not used; note that the two-oni encounter alone still landed just past Deadly and produced one PC at 0 HP with two death saves. Right side of the line, but only just.
  • The entire ground floor design was bypassed. No glyph of warding (they used the back door), no shriek-ward at the stair head (never triggered, never mentioned), no gaps, no party split, no three-skeletons scene — one skeleton was glimpsed in a hall and ignored. Everything B2/B3 was built for is unspent.
  • Both locks were opened by Vrenn, with Guntrah's tool. A nice unplanned answer to a party with no rogue: the drow is the best lockpick in the group at +6, and the All-Purpose Tool supplies the kit. Ruled at the table that an attuned tool can be lent while holding its shape.
  • The basement ran short of its own map. The braziers became four bowls on small altars, the barred gate became a grate with a skull on it, the room came in at 50-60 by 70-80 rather than 75 by 130, and the embalming slab, the blood pools, the west grates, the matching crate and the bone benches were never described. The escalation waves from B7 never arrived — the fight was two oni and nothing else, which was plenty.
  • The two oni landed exactly as designed and the ambush trigger was better than written. The design said the party's decency would be the trigger. In play it was Guntrah's suspicion — he caught one tracking him while invisible, sat on it for the entire circle scene readying an action, and fired first. That is a better version of the same beat and it belonged entirely to a player.
  • The oni were run with truesight and necrotic claws, neither of which is on the printed block. See the Canon block. Fine, but write it down — the party has now seen both and will plan around them.
  • The circle was not the loaded gun; the grate was. The d6 was rolled in the open in front of the players and came up 1. That single roll is what made the ending. Note for future: rolling that in the open was the right call and cost nothing.
  • The north gate beast never got out, and is still in there, and is now awake and angry in a basement with two dead oni in it and a working planar circle.
  • Voss's contact list still has not been handed over, or if it was it was off-recording. See Open Items — this needs a one-line answer from the DM before Session 4.
  • Guntrah's character sheet was corrected mid-session. Seven active infusions, cap is four. See the Canon block.

DM Only — What Did Not Land

  1. The Session 2 make-up clue died a second time. The primed goods waiting to ship, the consignment record showing more than one Material Plane destination, and the empty crate matching the Provisions Annex were all sited on the ground floor and in the basement's south-east corner. None of it was mentioned at the table (DM, 2026-09-03) — the party went in the back door, straight down the stairs and out through the floor, and it hit the cutting-room floor rather than being looked past. This is the load-bearing clue that has now failed to arrive twice, and the answer is not a third room: the DM intends to have [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] cover it in dialogue in a later session, and Vrenn made the contents. Not to be designed now.
  2. Nobody looked at anything. No detect magic, no dispel magic, no Investigation of the benches, the workers' work, or the room. B4a was written for a party that scouts, and this party moves.
  3. The Thatch cross-check half landed. Vrenn confirmed the address and confirmed he has no idea who Thatch is. The oni were never asked — the design's "second independent source" beat did not happen, because the conversation went from take what you need to crossbow bolts.
  4. Morvek's door was never approached. Vrenn warned them off it, they took the warning, and they never went upstairs. The sealed-door design is unspent and can be reused wholesale.
  5. Nobody worked out that the two workers were guards until one of them said so. Guntrah's Insight-by-inference — are these prisoners like Vrenn or are they his? — was the closest anyone came, and he reasoned it out purely from behaviour, without a roll.

DM Only — SESSION 4 OPENS ON THIS. Decide before you run it.

Vrenn is off the plane, and Morvek's Grave Token is a beacon. Vrenn said the consequence out loud, twice, in front of the players, in his own words: "If I were to leave the plane he would know immediately, and he would follow me, and he would kill all of you." He then left the plane. The party made him do it, and one of them invited him.

The question is not whether this fires. It is how fast. Three shapes, and they are meaningfully different campaigns:

  • Slow. Morvek knows where the Token's soul is but crossing planes is expensive and constrained by exactly the thin-place rule Vrenn just explained — and the party has stripped the components out of one end of the lane and taken the fork. He has to arrange it. Weeks, and it hangs over the whole of Phase 2. Recommended: it preserves the "Morvek never appears in person" hard constraint, it makes the party's own theft the thing buying them time, and it gives Vrenn time to become a character rather than a countdown.
  • Fast. Something arrives within a session or two — not Morvek, who is well past a level-10 party, but something sent. Keeps the pressure honest and cashes the promise quickly.
  • Vrenn's read is wrong. He is repeating what Morvek told him, and Morvek is the one who benefits from him believing it. That is a legitimate reading of everything on file, and it is the only one that lets Vrenn ever be safe — but it also retires the best consequence the campaign has generated so far. Use only if the table needs the drow to be a companion rather than a fuse.

Whatever the answer, the party has acquired a person. A Wizard 13 with plane shift, Arcana +15 and a bag of very expensive components is not a follower a level-10 party can carry without the DM deciding what he does in a fight. Session 3 already showed the honest answer: he acts once, decisively, out of despair rather than tactics, and then does something practical and non-combat. Keep him at that dial.

Also carried out of the basement: the [[steel_tuning_fork|steel tuning fork]] is in Silas's hands, and it is the one component plane shift does not consume. They now hold the means to come back — and cannot cast it for a long time, which is exactly the ache A7 was designed for and it survived the route change intact.
