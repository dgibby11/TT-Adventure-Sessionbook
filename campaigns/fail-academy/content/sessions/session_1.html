Orvyn's Gift — Read Aloud / Performance

After a morning sparring session, Commander Orvyn asks Tavian to stay behind a minute. Once the other students have filed out, he draws a fine, dark rag from between two pristine plates of his armor and begins polishing the blade of his own sword.

"It's beautiful, isn't it?" Orvyn says. "It can sing stories of the world, if you know how to listen." He slides it back into its scabbard and leans it against a bench, then crosses to the standing basin and washes his face before returning to sit. He motions for Tavian to join him.

He tells a story of battle — one of the many he fought in his younger years — and of the elf who chased glory at his side through all of them: Aelrindel Dawnbringer, closer to him than any comrade, near enough to a brother. The two of them served as the fists of Tempus together. Then his voice grows somber, and he tells of the wound that took Aelrindel from him — mortal, and faster than Orvyn could reach him. Before he could do anything, Aelrindel struck his own bargain with Tempus on his deathbed: rather than let his story end there, he pledged his soul into his blade, to live on in steel since his body could not go on.

"This longsword," Orvyn says. "After the battle, I buried him — and saw runes on this blade that hadn't been there before. When I picked it up, one of them lit, and I could feel power radiating out of the steel, not so different from Tempus's wind at your back when you're rushing into a fight. The sword longed for glory just as much as I did. That much was certain." He pauses. "It's been over fifteen years since it's seen battle. I know what I need to do. I'm just not certain I can."

Orvyn closes his eyes and murmurs a short prayer, then rises and draws the sword. All five runes blaze to life as he looks at it — and in an instant, every one of them goes dark. He turns the blade around, taking it by the edge, and extends the hilt to Tavian with a nod.

The moment Tavian's hand closes around the grip, the first rune ignites again, blinding bright. Orvyn's shoulders loosen. He lets out a breath he seems to have been holding a long time.

"I have fulfilled my oath to Aelrindel," he says. "You carry no such oath — but it's plain he favors you, and he'll see you to glories I never could." He straightens, some of the weight visibly leaving him. "Come find me after graduation, and I'll tell you another story. This one's about a young tiefling boy who first set foot on this campus some fifty years ago."

And with that, he turns and walks back toward the barracks — rigid, upright, and every bit as authoritative as always.

Viola's Gift — Read Aloud / Performance

[[tito|Tito]]'s rehearsal breaks late — cases snapping shut, the last few students filing out into the corridor. [[viola_gossamer|Viola Gossamer]] is waiting just outside the door, half in the shadow of the frame, wings already in that stuttering, off-rhythm blur that gives her away before she's said a word. She's holding something wrapped in plain paper, too big to be a book, hugged against her chest with both arms like it might make a break for it if she loosens her grip.

She doesn't wait for him to spot her first — waiting leaves room to change her mind. The second he's in earshot: "Tito — hi — good, you're —" She cuts herself off and just shoves the parcel at him with both hands. "Here. This is for you. It's not a big thing. I mean, it is a thing, but it doesn't have to be a thing—" Glitter sifts off her shoulders as she talks faster to outrun her own sentence.

If he starts to unwrap it, she talks straight over the sound of tearing paper. "It's a cloak. Obviously, you'll see that. I made it — well, my family did the actual weaving, that part's just good fabric — but the part that matters, the enchantment, I did that myself. Every bit of it." She's talking with her hands now, feet already half-turned toward the corridor, like the rest of her has decided to leave before her mouth is done.

She can't quite let the hem go unmentioned, either. "Don't — don't look too close at the stitching, on the side with the letters. I rushed the end of it, it's not as even as the rest. It's fine, it works fine, just — don't look at it too close." A beat. Her eyes actually land on his — maybe a second and a half, the longest she manages all conversation.

Then, quieter, almost to herself: "You probably don't even remember why. It's not — it doesn't matter."

And before he can get out more than her name: "Anyway! Congratulations. You'll do something wonderful, I'm sure — everyone says so. I have to go, I told Maestro Lira I'd — bye, Tito—" Wings snap into a full, panicked shimmer, and she's gone around the corner at a near-run.

Crumb's Gift — Read Aloud / Performance

A note reaches you summoning you to [[oswald_crumb|Professor Crumb]]'s tower — a proper letter of introduction stapled to it, as if you were a visiting dignitary and not a student who's been climbing these stairs for years. Crumb doesn't look up when you enter. He's mid-catalog, three ledgers open at once, muttering part-of-speech labels to himself as he sorts a tray of dried components back into their jars. "Sit. Or stand. Whichever." A pause, precisely as long as it takes him to finish the row he's on. "Precisely. Now..."

He produces a pouch of oiled goblin-hide from beneath the desk and sets it down like a man returning a library book. "Your graduation gift." Every interior pocket is labeled in his own copperplate — alphabetically. "I built the sorting system myself, over the winter recess, because the supplier's factory labeling was, and I quote my own note on the invoice, 'a grammatical crime against the noun.' Take care of it." He taps a folded slip of paper tucked into the flap. "I've written down what matters, so neither of us has to rely on memory."

The note, in his flawless script: "A wizard who runs out of components mid-incantation has committed the arcane equivalent of a sentence fragment. Mind your accuracy. — O.C." Without waiting for a reaction, he slides open a discreet drawer, removes a tiny purple velvet pouch, and gently places it into the centermost pocket of the bag. "A Pearl of Power. Requires attunement — footnote it separately if you're keeping records, which you should be." A beat. "Accuracy matters more in this profession than most people credit. An imprecise wizard is a dangerous one. Better you learn that from a label than from an accident."

He is already turning back to his ledgers before finishing the sentence: "In four years, you're the only student whose spellbook I've never had to send back covered in red ink." He doesn't look up, but the pause runs longer than his usual clipped ones. "You've made my job considerably less difficult than it might have been. I don't say that lightly, and I won't be repeating it." Then, briskly: "Now, where was I..." He returns to his sorting and cataloging. The meeting is clearly over.

The Summons

The Summons Note

A short, unexplained note reaches all four of them independently: "My office. Thursday, after last class. All four of you. — E.V." No context.

Given how well they know her, this is already strange — not because she'd have no reason to want the four of them together. They're a known friend group who work well as a team, and she's watched that for years; wanting them in a room together isn't the odd part. It's how she's doing it: a formal, unexplained summons isn't how she operates, even with students she trusts and likes. She talks to them. She doesn't send notes.

The Empty Office

They arrive on time. She isn't there. The door is unlocked, ajar.

The Office — Read Aloud / Performance

Voss's office is exactly as familiar as four years of office hours should make it, and exactly as spare as she is. Shelves of incident logs and exam records run floor to ceiling along one wall, spines labeled by year in her own precise hand — decades of them, going back further than any of the four have thought to ask about. A single narrow window looks out over the FAIL Chamber grounds. Her desk sits at the center, uncluttered except for what's on it now: no personal effects, no clutter, nothing that isn't working. The only thing that looks remotely out of place is what's waiting for them.

Her handwritten notes are sitting in plain view on the desk — dated that same day, written to no one, more a log of her own thinking than a letter:

Voss's Notes — Handout / Read Aloud

Three this year. Three, this school year alone — not spread across some longer span I could talk myself out of.

The catalog item — someone fed something into the midterm requisitions that had no business being there. Attracted the pack, and worse, angered them past anything natural. I've asked Provisions for the acquisition record twice. Twice I've been told it "doesn't exist." A requisition doesn't stop existing.

The component pouch in the Arcane Tower stores. Wizards and Sorcerers share that supply room and spent a week blaming each other for what happened, which told me plainly that neither of them did it. Turned the pouch over myself after. Small mark stamped on the underside — [here she has drawn the mark itself, carefully, in ink: a maker's stamp, no words]. I've seen that mark before and never once thought about where it came from.

An instrument, left anonymous in the practice closet. Ordinary to look at. Enchanted to feed on its own sound — play it past a few minutes and whoever's holding it starts wanting to hurt something. Found because a rehearsal nearly ended in a fistfight over nothing.

The Academy's never been silent — I've read the incident logs back generations, and something goes wrong here every so often, has for centuries. That's not what stops me. What stops me is the shape of it. The old incidents are scattered — years apart, no common thread, easy enough to write off one at a time as bad luck. These three are not that. Three, inside a single year, and every one of them traces back to something that shouldn't have been where it was — placed there, not natural. That's not the campus's old checkered luck repeating. That's a pattern, close together and all the same shape, and patterns have authors.

"A secret, once it is understood for what it is, is a kind of power." I keep coming back to that line. Read it again last night. She wasn't writing about ghosts or thieves. She meant something living here, wearing the ordinary shape of the place, using its silence as cover.

If she was right — and I think she was — then something is doing exactly that. Somewhere on these grounds. I don't know what. I don't know where. I have three incidents, one old thesis, and a very bad feeling that we've been standing on top of something for three hundred and fifty years and calling it a floor.

Need more before I bring this to anyone. Cross-reference incident dates against the exam schedule and faculty travel logs. Start with Provisions — someone signed for that catalog entry.

— E.V.

Voss's Return — The Ninth Thesis

She arrives to find them mid-read and doesn't pretend otherwise. The reason for the summons becomes obvious once they've seen the notes: she's recruiting them, as [[ninth_thesis|Ninth Thesis]] field operatives, to work off-campus once they graduate. She already has operatives. She believes this party can succeed where her existing people haven't.

The Guntrah Spotlight

She singles out [[guntrah|Guntrah]] directly — his Investigator background and his instinct for untangling exactly this kind of mystery make him, in her eyes, the party's biggest asset for this work. Say it to him, not about him.

She names the Ninth Thesis outright — a real recruitment pitch loses credibility if she's cagey about what she's even asking them to join. All four had at least heard of it before: real, exclusive, operating in the background, never confirmed. She opens with "How do I put this bluntly — have you heard of an organization on campus called the Ninth Thesis?" and admits almost immediately that she's bad at recruitment pitches, that it isn't normally her job, and that she's keeping this one further off the books than usual.

What She Actually Tells Them
  • Where the name comes from. The first graduating class numbered nine. Their original theses are displayed in a case in [[old_library|the Old Library]] — where there are only eight. She asks whether any of them ever noticed.
  • What the ninth said. One of the nine, a woman, wrote about something more common than most people realize: everybody has secrets. Hers is the line quoted in the notes on the desk. Voss believes one such secret concerned the founding of this Academy. She does not name her.
  • Her own stake. "I don't know if you know this about me, but I am loosely descended from one of the founders of this academy. There has been a Voss here in some capacity or another for hundreds of years, and some of us have been in charge of leading the Ninth Thesis." She does not say which founder.
  • The work. She handles campus. Operatives work the wider world — some in Neverwinter, some in Baldur's Gate, one just up the road in Silverymoon, several more besides. Their job is to gather information and occasionally act on it, which isn't common. She communicates with them daily and runs the organization herself.
  • The terms. Around 50 gold a week, negotiable, paid weekly in coin rather than Academy credits, in arrears if they're somewhere payment can't reach. Supplies provided on top. A small written contract to follow — "we are very much by the book even though we are not talked about in the open." Discretion expected; no oath, no handshake, no signal.
  • The stakes. The pay is high because the danger is real, and as graduates they won't be shielded from it the way students have been.

Outcome: all four accepted. Graduation was never conditional on it — she was explicit that they graduate either way and that this is an opportunity, not an obligation.

The Bulette

Framing: this bulette is corrupted, not possessed — there's no intelligence riding it, no voice, nothing to negotiate with. It's ordinary veil-bleed: proximity to the destabilizing veil has made it wrong in scale and ferocity compared to what the bestiary would lead anyone to expect, and that's all it is. No clue here — physical, verbal, or otherwise, suppressed or not — should point toward the fissure or the ruins it came from. The party fights it because it's dangerous and close, not because they're investigating its origin.

The Approach — Read Aloud / Performance

There is no trail, not really — just the least dense part of the forest, and about an hour of breaking branches and going around things before the first sign of anything. That sign is sound, not sight: crunching, somewhere ahead, slow and plodding rather than fast. Fifty yards or so, and too dense to see through.

Then the trail of damage — no felled trees, but saplings knocked flat and undergrowth flattened in a line that isn't heading toward campus at all; it runs perpendicular to it. Streaks of blood on the leaves and broken branches. Not much. Enough.

The destruction stops at a hole. Ten feet across, ragged rather than cut, angling down and away, with a smear of the same blood running over the lip.

The Ground — A Tunnel Fight

The party went down the hole. That decision shaped the entire encounter, and it should be understood as the pivotal one: cramped, single-file, no room to move side by side, roughly fifty feet in, in the dark. The bulette had circled back through its own tunnel system and came up underneath them.

What follows is a fight across two vertical shafts rather than a field — the party's entry hole and the new one the bulette punched through, about ten feet apart on the surface. Anyone who wants to change floors has to climb, fly, or be hauled. This is not an open-terrain fight and should not be run as one.

The Wrongness — Read Aloud / Performance

Once it's on the surface and in daylight, it is recognizably the creature from the textbook plates and recognizably not right. The rock-like hide carries a purplish hue, and there are veins visibly streaking across the surface of the skin — which makes no sense, because that skin is stone. When it takes real damage, plates slough away and expose the muscle beneath. Its blood runs red, but a glittering, darker red than blood ought to be.

As it dies, the pulsing purple in the veins dims out with the rest of it.

DM Only — Mechanics

Stat block, CR, and mechanics are tracked in D&D Beyond, not here. Framing only: this was not designed as a genuinely hard fight, and it played much harder than that — see the "What Actually Happened at the Table" note below. Fighting it underground, on its terms, is the reason.

The Aftermath

Examining the corpse turns up several things, none of which resolve anything:

  • The corruption reads as faintly devilish — something evil tainted this creature into behaving the way it did. Explicitly not a possession; nothing was riding it. Something did this to it and left.
  • [[tito|Tito]] connects it to stories from his own fae ancestry — accounts of creatures in the Feywild getting riled into unnatural strength and recklessness by something that triggers it in them. A comparison, not an answer.
  • Still half-swallowed in its mouth is an ordinary forest animal, an elk or similar, caught mid-meal when the party arrived. Nothing significant about it.
  • Bulette scales, pried off and pocketed — powdered, they're a component in earth-based geomancy. [[oswald_crumb|Professor Crumb]] is the obvious buyer if anyone wants coin for them, given the party's prior expedition experience with salvage.

There is also a quieter realization, and it should be allowed to land: as level 10s, this should not have been a difficult fight. It was. Several of them are genuinely beaten up. The takeaway isn't "we lost" — they won — it's this is what it's actually going to be like now. No one standing by to slip a ring on a finger and make it better, no hospital wing. For [[guntrah|Guntrah]] specifically there's a thread of guilt available: he had the means to stay out of the tunnel and to pull the fight into open ground, and instead they went down the hole on the creature's terms.

The Debrief

On the way out of the forest, [[torvald_thatch|Torvald]] is visible about fifty feet ahead — mid-conversation with someone, quietly, who then walks away. He congratulates them, says he'll notify Voss himself. When the party mentions the corruption and the purple blood, he's mildly skeptical — "Are you sure this just wasn't your first encounter with one and you just underestimated it?" — but says he'll pass it along. What he finds genuinely out of the ordinary isn't the creature. It's that Voss sent students to handle it at all. He rolls his eyes at the party's assurances and walks off.

The Debrief — Read Aloud / Performance

The party overtakes Torvald on the way and reports to Voss directly. She puts down her quill: "It's taken care of, then." She is unamused by theatrics and used to them — until the word corrupted comes up. Then she stops. "Could you describe it for me, please?" And she picks the quill back up and starts writing.

The detail she seizes on is not the purple blood. It's habitat: a bulette burrows in rocky, mountainous terrain. It does not den in deep woods. Its presence there at all was unnatural before anything else about it was.

Pressed on whether this connects to the three incidents in her notes, she refuses to make the leap: "They all share a pattern, which is that they are magical objects that were placed on campus anonymously that didn't belong here. A corrupted bulette wandering too close to campus and outside its lair is a totally different pattern than the other three. That's not to say that you're wrong — just that we need to establish more before we can call it a pattern." Asked directly whether the two are unrelated: "I don't know that they're unrelated. But I cannot say that they are related."

She declines an invitation to the party's celebration, deflects a question about which faculty drink, and closes it out: "We will speak tomorrow. Thank you very much for your service." She won't make a habit of sending them out again before graduation — she cannot have students die between now and then; it would be a very bad look for the school.

[Note: [[tito|Tito]] floated a theory in character during this exchange — that the irregularities corroborate "a subplot to overthrow the academy." That's his own guess, not confirmed truth. Let the party keep chasing it without confirming or denying.]

DM Only

In the meantime, off-screen, she reaches out to trusted [[ninth_thesis|Ninth Thesis]] operatives working the region — Silverymoon, Everlund, wherever she has a contact who owes her a favor — asking them to run the [[thornwick_consortium|Thornwick]] name and stamp against whatever trade and merchant records they can access. What comes back over the following weeks is real but thin: the name is genuine and active, attached to legitimate-looking component and specimen orders moving through at least Silverymoon and Everlund, but with no ownership record, no founding date, no name anyone can put to who actually runs it — which sharpens her unease rather than resolving it. None of it points at the Academy, at Crumb, or at anyone on campus; her operatives have no reason to look there.

What does point at Crumb is her own memory, not their research — the nagging "I've seen that mark before" from her original notes eventually resolves itself: she placed it on crates near his tower before one of his expeditions, years ago, and thought nothing of it at the time. She adds "talk to Crumb about his sourcing — if only to rule him out" to her working list. Not an accusation. Professional diligence from someone who reads incident logs for a hobby.

This is her own legwork feeding directly into Phase 2's Ninth Thesis investigation thread (the alumni/Thornwick-stamp cross-reference that eventually surfaces the campaign's antagonist) — see phase2_the_one_who_got_away.json. She withholds all of this from the party for now — not coyness, a genuine call to protect their focus through finals — but see the optional beat below if you want them to glimpse it anyway.
