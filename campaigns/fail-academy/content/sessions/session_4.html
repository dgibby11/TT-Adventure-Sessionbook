VRENN KNOWS NOTHING ABOUT THIS FOREST. Plane shift is inaccurate — he aimed at a name and landed miles off. He is as surprised by the dragon as they are.

1. Arrival

The circle lets go. No transition, no tunnel — one instant of pressure and then the smell changes completely.

Read aloud — the first breath

The heat is gone. That is the first thing, before your eyes work: the air is cold, and wet, and it does not hurt. You have been breathing sulphur and ash for a day and a night, rationing water by the hour, and now there is moisture on your face and the smell of leaf-rot and green growing things and, somewhere close, running water.

You are standing in a clearing perhaps forty feet across, in grass and fern, under a sky you cannot see. The canopy above you is enormous — old growth, closed over, letting down only a grey-green half-light that tells you nothing about the hour. Thick vegetation walls the clearing on every side.

There are trails. Two narrow ones at knee height, pushed through the undergrowth by something small and regular. And one much wider, opposite, where something heavy has come through more than once and the saplings have given up.

Nobody is shooting at you. Nothing is on fire. For the first time since you walked out of a supply cupboard, you are somewhere that is alive.

DM Only — running the arrival

Let the relief land before anything else happens. This party has been on a clock since the middle of Session 2. Give them a full minute of table time where the only thing to do is breathe and drink. The creek matters — they rationed water by the hour on the far side and one of them went without.

They do not know where they are, and nothing in the clearing tells them. No roads, no sound of habitation, no sun to navigate by. The whole session leans on description because they are geographically blind — that is the point, and it is what makes [[tedrovaxilliath|Ted]] valuable rather than just dangerous.

What a good roll gets them: temperate old-growth forest, northern, wet, late in the year. Nature or Survival says the trees are wrong for anywhere near the Academy. Nobody can name it. Do not let anyone identify Neverwinter Wood without being told.

[[silas|Silas]] is at 1 hit point and standing. Somebody should notice.

VRENN CANNOT GUIDE THEM (DM, 2026-09-24)

Plane shift is inaccurate by design — you name a destination in general terms and arrive in or near it. Vrenn aimed at the one above-ground name he owned and the spell put them down somewhere in the general region, not where he had once stood.

So Vrenn is as lost as they are, and as surprised by [[tedrovaxilliath|the dragon]] as they are. He surfaced near this wood once, decades ago, fleeing, briefly, and never knew anything lived in it. He can give the party the name of the place and nothing else — no geography, no landmarks, no warning.

This is deliberate and it protects the session: it keeps Vrenn from becoming a guidebook, and it is precisely what makes Ted valuable rather than merely dangerous. The [[underdark_breach|Breach]] is about five miles off and the [[fomorians|fomorians]] only arrived in the spring, so even a Vrenn who knew this forest well would not know about them.

2. The Sending

Within a minute of arrival — before they have decided anything — [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] stops moving. He is listening to something nobody else can hear.

Morvek's sending — 25 words. Pick one.

A. "Vrenn. You are on the Material Plane and I know it. I do not yet know where. I will. Do not make me hurry."

B. "You have taken something of mine and gone where I cannot yet see. Stay where you are, Vrenn. I will come and collect you both."

C. "Three days. Perhaps four. Stay with them if you like — it makes no difference to me. Do not make this tiresome, Vrenn. Come home."

DM Only — how to play it

Vrenn does not reply. Sending permits an immediate reply of twenty-five words and he does not use it, out of fear. Tell the party that he could have answered and didn't — it is the most revealing thing about him all session.

He relays the gist rather than the words, badly, twice, getting it wrong the first time. The content: Morvek knows he left the plane. Morvek does not yet know exactly where. He is coming, and Vrenn has a couple of days at best.

CHOSEN: A (DM, 2026-09-24) — and the exact phrasing does not matter, because the players never hear it. Vrenn relays the gist, badly, and the wording is for the DM's ears only. It states precisely what has been established — the Token gave him the plane and not the place — it puts the clock on the table without naming a number the DM then has to honour, and "do not make me hurry" is a threat that implies he could.

The decision this hands the players, in hour one: the thing Morvek is tracking is standing next to them. The obvious move is to put distance between themselves and Vrenn. [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] personally asked him to come, so if anybody proposes it, that is a real scene. The DM is content for Vrenn to leave voluntarily — do not build anything downstream that needs him to stay.

Mechanically, why Morvek is not here yet: scrying fails across planes, so the Grave Token is all he has while he is in the Abyss. He must plane shift to the Material Plane first and scry from there — a 7th-level slot and then a 5th. He also has a burgled house, two dead hired guards, an agitated caged beast and a landlord owed an explanation. The DM chooses when he arrives.

3. The Wood Tells Them Whose It Is

The clearing is inside the six-mile radius of a green dragon's lair, and everything in it is subtly wrong in ways that accumulate. Seed all three omens before Ted ever speaks.

Read aloud — the choking fog

There is a fog on the forest floor. It is ankle-deep at the clearing's edge and knee-deep in the trees, and it has a faint green cast that you keep telling yourself is just the light coming through the canopy. It is not just the light.

It smells like three things that should not be together: the sharp chemical bite of chlorine, the sweet heavy rot of something large decaying somewhere out of sight, and pine — far too much pine, thick and sugary, like a scent laid over a smell.

Read aloud — the twisted trails

The trail you chose is narrowing. You are certain it was wider when you started down it. The thorns here grow in long whipping canes, and they are across the path in a way that looks less like growth and more like a hand closing — and when you back out and try the other way, that one is open, and inviting, and leads somewhere you had not intended to go.

You are not being stopped. You are being steered.

Read aloud — the silent spies

There is a squirrel on the branch to your left. It has not moved in a while. It is not foraging, it is not grooming, and it has not broken to cover the way a squirrel does when five people walk under it. It is looking at you.

So is the thrush above it. So are two crows further back, sitting on the same limb, silent. And when you look down, there is a snake laid along a root by your boot, perfectly still, head up, watching.

Nothing in this forest is making any noise at all.

DM Only — what the omens are for

These are the green dragon regional effects, and they do three jobs at once:

  • They replace a map. The party cannot navigate, so the forest itself has to be the information. Every omen says something owns this before anything says what.
  • The spies are how Ted already knows. Do not have him merely smell the Abyss on them — he watched them arrive, through a dozen sets of eyes, from the moment the circle opened. When he finally speaks, he should already know how many of them there are, that one of them is barely standing, and that one of them is a drow. That is far more frightening than a good nose, and it is the omen paying off.
  • The trails are the geography. If the table wants to go somewhere, the thickets decide how easy that is. Use them to keep the session in the clearing and its immediate surroundings without ever saying "you can't."

The creek is already slightly wrong — a green cast, a taste, fewer things living in it than there should be. That is a seed. Upstream it gets much worse, and the worst of it is directly over his lair. See [[teds_lair|the sinkhole]]. Do not point at this.

4. The Rest

They need it badly and they have the means. Give it to them.

DM Only — the hut, and why Ted waits

Expect Leomund's Tiny Hut almost immediately. [[silas|Silas]] has it as a ritual, free, every day, whatever he prepared. Ten minutes to cast, eight hours, nothing gets in.

RULING — Ted can hold a conversation through it. The spell bars creatures and objects, and blocks spells and magical effects from crossing. It says nothing about sound. The dome is not soundproof and ordinary speech works both ways.

That gives you the better scene, and you can have it twice:

  • The dome is opaque from outside, transparent from inside. The party see him perfectly. He cannot see them at all — he is negotiating with a blank dome, addressing people he can only smell and count.
  • Neither side can cast at the other, which is exactly why he talks instead of testing it.
  • Ten-foot radius, immobile, and it collapses if Silas steps out. Five bodies fit under the nine-creature limit — but they are pinned in one spot for eight hours with an adult green dragon standing in the clearing.
  • An opaque dome in a forest clearing is a beacon. Perfectly good reason he finds them in minutes.

Play it by ear, but the strong version is: he talks to them through the dome, then is still there when it drops. The second conversation is different because they have had eight hours to decide what they want from him.

Only if it ever turns hostile: decide in advance whether a breath weapon counts as a "magical effect" barred by the dome. It is a monster ability, not a spell, and RAW is genuinely ambiguous. Irrelevant while he is talking.

If they do NOT cast the hut, he simply walks into the clearing while they are setting a watch, and the conversation happens face to face with Silas at 1 hit point. That is more frightening and perfectly fine.

5. Tedrovaxilliath

Full profile, personality, hoard and reward: [[tedrovaxilliath|his entity]]. The short version for the table is below.

Read aloud — the arrival of something large

The forest goes quiet in a way you can feel in your teeth — and then quieter, because the small noises you had stopped noticing all stop at once.

Something very large settles into the clearing without much sound at all, which is worse than if it had crashed. Green — not the green of leaves but of old copper and deep water, scaled, streaming with the fog that came in with it. The head comes down and around on a neck like a felled trunk, and the eyes are amber, and interested, and entirely unhurried.

One wing does not fold like the other. There is a long pale seam through the membrane, old and badly healed.

When it speaks, the voice is cultured, dry, and far too quiet for the size of it.

"You reek of the Abyss. You have thirty seconds to explain why I should not consider this an invasion."

DM Only — running the negotiation

The core of the scene: he will not believe "we arrived here by accident." That is the truth, and it sounds exactly like a lie — five armed strangers and a drow stepping out of a portal in the middle of his forest, stinking of the Abyss. His suspicion is correct reasoning from good evidence, which is what makes it playable rather than tiresome.

The answer is not [[tito|Tito]]'s Deception +12. It is consistency. He asks questions designed to catch a lie, lets the conversation move on, and circles back to the same ones later to see whether the answers match. A party that simply tells the truth, twice, gets further than a party that performs. Let a good Deception roll work — and then let him check it later and find the seam.

What moves him, in order:

  1. That they want to leave. He wants them out of his wood. Their goals are aligned and it takes him a moment to believe it.
  2. [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] being obviously a prisoner rather than a leader. Ted reads power dynamics instantly and Vrenn does not carry himself like anyone's spearhead.
  3. Being asked about the forest. He is vain about it and it is the one subject where he stops interrogating and starts talking.
  4. Somebody noticing the wing without making it an insult.

What he will and will not answer. Where they are, what year it is, what lies in each direction, which water is safe, what the Wood contains — freely, at length, and accurately. Mortal politics, the Academy, the Ninth Thesis, who rules Neverwinter now — he genuinely does not know and does not care. Do not use him as an exposition dump for the main plot. He is a local expert on one forest.

He will not let them near his lair, and the fomorian job is deliberately in the opposite direction.

The treant story is his to tell and he tells it as a grievance, not a defeat — see his entity. He is sincere and he is wrong, and the corruption is real and his. A player who names that has genuinely got somewhere.

6. The Job

Two [[fomorians|fomorians]] have camped just inside an Underdark breach at the edge of his range and have been eating his game. He tried to deal with them himself; they went deeper into the cave and he could not follow.

Tedrovaxilliath, making the offer

"There is a hole in the north of my range where the deep places come up. Two things came out of it in the spring and have been eating my deer ever since. Not hunting. Eating. There is a difference and you will not understand it."

(a pause, entirely unembarrassed) "I went. They went down a crack I do not fit into and waited for me to become bored. I have not become bored. I have become inconvenienced, which lasts longer."

"You are small, you are armed, and you want to leave my forest. Kill them, and I will tell you where you are, where to go, and how to get there without dying on the way. And I will give you some things I have no use for."

DM Only — the job, the fight, and the reward

THE WALK NORTH CROSSES A RIVER (DM, 2026-09-24, from [[neverwinter_wood_map|the map]]). Five miles, and a real river in the way — not in the original prep and worth thirty seconds of thought before they set off. [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] is in plate with DEX −2 and disadvantage on Stealth; [[silas|Silas]] has STR 7 and an Athletics of −2, and also has shape water as a cantrip and water walk nowhere on his list. It is a crossing, a delay, and a good place for something to be watching them from the far bank — or for nothing at all to happen, which after the last two sessions is its own kind of beat.

The cave: [[underdark_breach|the Breach]]. Full layout there. The short version is a collapsed shaft under a fallen giant of a tree, an antechamber the fomorians have fouled, and a crack at the back that goes down a very long way.

The fight: two fomorians, CR 8 each, ~11,700 XP adjusted — the same just-past-Deadly number as the two oni in Session 3. Do not let the party take it unrested. Evil Eye is genuinely nasty against this group: [[silas|Silas]] at CHA 8 and [[guntrah|Guntrah]] at CHA 12 are the soft targets, and [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]]'s Aura of Protection (+3 to all saves within 10 ft) is the counter if anybody thinks to stand close.

RULED (2026-09-24): [[tito|Tito]]'s satyr Magic Resistance DOES apply to Evil Eye — advantage on the save, even though a curse is not strictly a spell. He has earned it and it is a good moment for the player.

The reward — all five options stay on the table (DM, 2026-09-24) and are chosen live depending on how the negotiation goes. Full list and his reasoning on [[tedrovaxilliath|his entity]]. Make sure the potions land: four in a rotted adventurer's pack, and the party currently has none at all.

And the free part, which is what they actually need: where they are, what year it is, the four directions, and safe passage out toward Neverwinter. He will present it as the generous half of the deal. It costs him nothing.

If they refuse the job he does not attack. He becomes markedly less helpful, tells them the way out of his forest and nothing else, and the thickets stop cooperating. That is a complete and reasonable outcome.

7. Where They Go

DM Only — the four directions, and the expected path

Once Ted has given them their bearings, the fork that has been waiting since Session 2 finally opens. None of the four are detailed — by design. Prep the one they pick.

  • West — Thundertree. A ruined town. No longer connected to the artifact plot (DM, 2026-09-23); the supply-chain link is struck. Still a location the DM likes and is free to reuse for something else. Note: campaigns/lost-mine/ already has a built ruins_of_thundertree and venomfang, a young green dragon squatting there. If an adult green holds this wood, that is free plot — parent, rival, or the reason a younger green had to take a ruin on the edge.
  • Northwest — Mount Hotenow. The volcano whose eruption gutted Neverwinter. Some of Ted's hoard was scoured out of the city by it.
  • South — the Triboar Trail. The original planned route, toward Triboar and Yartar.
  • East — Agatha's Grove. Involves undead.

The DM's expectation, and it is a gut read rather than a rail: deal with the fomorians → take their bearings from Ted → head for Neverwinter, to find [[anselm_ferreck|Anselm Ferreck]] and a way back to the Academy or a way to reach [[ellery_voss|Voss]].

If that holds, Ferreck is the next prep job and his design is already written and entirely unspent. The key beat: Voss never gave them a countersign. His verification is deliberately not a password — it is a question about Voss that only somebody who genuinely knows her could answer, something small, domestic and unresearchable. Pick that detail from established Voss material rather than inventing one; the whole point is to cash in five years of office hours. That is the scene that turns the contact list from a prop into a lifeline.

DM Only — BRANCH: the party sides with the fomorians against Ted

Flagged by the DM 2026-09-24 and worth being ready for, because it is a completely defensible read of the evidence. Ted threatened them inside thirty seconds. He is a green dragon, which is the only fact about him most parties need. He eats people and does not pretend otherwise. He is asking them to do a job he failed at. And if they follow the water upstream they will find hard physical proof that he is poisoning the forest. A party that concludes the dragon is the problem has not made a mistake — they have read the available evidence correctly and reached the wrong answer, which is much better.

The language problem, and its three answers

Fomorians speak Undercommon and Giant. No player character speaks either. Guntrah has Common and Abyssal, Silas has Common, Celestial and Orc, Tito has Common and Elvish, Tavian has Common, Common Sign and Orc. So the branch opens only if somebody solves this, and there are exactly three keys:

  • [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] is a drow. He speaks Undercommon. He is the obvious answer, and it makes him abruptly indispensable at the exact moment he may be planning to leave. Use that.
  • [[tito|Tito]]'s Universal Speech — 1/long rest, up to four creatures understand him for an hour. Purpose-built for this.
  • [[silas|Silas]]'s comprehend languages, a free ritual he has every single day, or tongues from his spellbook.

Three independent routes means this is a live branch, not a theoretical one. Do not gate it behind a single roll.

Where they can talk without being overheard — and it is the only place

The silent spies cover the whole six-mile radius. They do not go underground. The [[underdark_breach|Breach]] sits about five miles out, just inside Ted's range — so he sees them go in, and he cannot see what happens inside. That makes the cave the one genuinely private room in the Wood, and it is worth far more than the fight in it.

He will, however, notice that they went in and came out without any fomorians being dead. He is not stupid, and he is already paranoid.

What the fomorians actually want

Not revenge — they are not proud enough for it. They want the Wood's game and to be left alone, and they would be delighted to see Ted dead so long as somebody else does the dying. They will agree to almost anything and they will absolutely betray the party afterwards, because they are cruel in a patient, planning way and because the hoard is right there. INT 9 means they can be bargained with; CHA 6 means they are bad at hiding what they think of the bargain.

THE MATHS — read this before you let it happen

Four level-10 PCs with no healing potions, plus two CR 8 allies who will turn, against an adult green dragon in its own lair, is a party wipe. Legendary actions, legendary resistances, lair actions, flight, and a breath weapon, on ground he has shaped for six hundred years. Do not run that fight straight at CR 15 and hope.

Three ways to have the branch without killing the campaign:

  1. Flex him DOWN, not up. The standing licence is CR 15–22 "for the best player experience" — that licence runs both directions. A wounded, ageing dragon with a ruined wing who is beaten off rather than slain is a perfectly good outcome and leaves him alive and extremely motivated.
  2. Make it a flight, not a fight — the same rule as the caged thing in Session 3. He withdraws, the party "wins", and they have made a permanent enemy who knows the whole forest and controls every animal in it. That is a much worse prize than they think.
  3. Let the fomorians go first. If the party send their new allies in ahead, the fomorians die, loudly, and the party get to watch exactly what an adult green does to two CR 8 giants before deciding whether they are next.

The better version of this branch, if you want one

Ted is beatable by information rather than damage. The corruption is real and he cannot see it; [[the_standing_treant|the treant]] is alive and has the other half of the story; the fomorians want him gone. A party holding all three of those has genuine leverage over a creature who has never had to negotiate with anybody. A three-cornered settlement — the fomorians leave, Ted moves his lair or accepts the corruption is his, the treant stands down — is a far better session than a dragon fight, and every piece of it is already on the board.

And if they simply kill him: the Wood loses its keeper. The regional effects end, which they will notice and enjoy. Then the fomorians expand, nothing culls the herds, and whatever Ted killed three of over six centuries has nothing standing in its way. That is a real scar and it should be allowed to happen.
