Premise: they came off a plane where they had been rationing water by the hour, with [[silas|Silas]] standing at 1 hit point, no healing potions, nothing healed since the morning of Commencement, and a drow nobody had planned to bring. What they got was cold wet air, running water in earshot, and a forest that belonged to something.

The party came through it almost untouched. The only damage any of the four took all session was 13 psychic to [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]], off an Evil Eye he otherwise shrugged off. Everything else that was spent was spent on [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]], who took a greatclub and a curse, and on Tavian's new spectral horse, which was destroyed.

1. The First Breath

No transition and no tunnel — the heat simply stopped. Cold, wet air that did not hurt to breathe, no sulphur, no ash, and the smell of leaf-rot and growing things. Running water somewhere out of sight.

A grassy clearing forty to fifty feet across, ferns round the edge, ringed by enormous deciduous trees with a little fir mixed in — a canopy sixty to seventy feet up, closed completely over, letting down only filtered light. No way to tell where the sun was. Heavy undergrowth all round the outside, with two small game trails leading off it that nothing the size of a person could use. No road, no path, no sign of anybody.

[[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] came through with them, and had not intended to. He said so: he had made it clear he was staying, and then [[silas|Silas]] went down and the thing behind the grate was a round away, and he cast rather than find out what happened to him when it came through.

Vrenn, asked where he had taken them

"I don't quite know. This doesn't look exactly familiar to me. This is the only place I know in the Material Plane, and I know that's where you wanted to go. The way the spell works, I have to think of someplace when I'm casting it, and that's the place we go."

"The one time I was up here on the surface, I emerged from a cave — when I fled Menzoberranzan. I must be close to here, I would think. I think the spell gets you close to where you want to be. But I don't know where this is exactly. Just some woods somewhere."

Pressed on it: he travelled perhaps a hundred miles underground getting out, about ten years ago, and has no idea in what direction, because nothing in the Underdark is reckoned by cardinal points. Everything down there is relative — how far above or below, and that way or this way.

2. Working Out Where They Are

The party solved this themselves.

[[guntrah|Guntrah]] rolled a 10 on History trying to place Menzoberranzan and got the honest answer: a drow city up north near the Spine of the World, and a hundred-mile radius off it is an enormous amount of ground. The DM put a real regional map on the table and showed them the circle. Five major forests inside it, and several smaller ones.

So Guntrah used his Winged Boots and went up through the canopy.

What he saw, on an 18 at disadvantage

The canopy is thick enough that he had to climb through it as much as fly, pulling branches aside, and got a couple of feet clear of the top.

Small mountains, perhaps fifteen miles off at the limit of vision — four or five in a row, and one of them looks volcanic. No sign of the great range; that would be far too far to see. A great many rivers, breaking the canopy in several places, which struck him as unusual. And, low in the direction of the setting sun, a faint smudge of smoke, or possibly just cloud.

Ruled at the table, after an argument about the curvature of the earth and the Strait of Gibraltar: from about a hundred feet up you see roughly twelve miles.

Bearings as they settled them: mountains to the northwest, the fire west-southwest. Evening — a couple of hours before sunset, the sun going down in the west because the DM decided on the spot that it does.

Then the deduction. Nature checks were useless (a 0, a 9, a 12 — nobody is proficient and the DM said so out loud). But the rivers were the key, and Guntrah told the others about them.

Silas gets there, on a 16

There are fir trees scattered in, so this is not down near Baldur's Gate. Only a few forests have that many rivers. And the only one that is neither northern nor southern, with that mixture of trees — "Probably the Neverwinter Wood. That's your hunch."

He told the party: "I think we might be there."

They named the place themselves, in the first twenty minutes, off a flying reconnaissance and one History check. They still had no idea what they were standing in.

3. The Voice in the Trees

Ten minutes in, [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] cast find steed and got a translucent spectral warhorse in his own air-genasi colours. He named it Roach. Everybody admired the horse.

Passive Perceptions of 10, 12 and 13 noticed nothing at all. Vrenn's 18 noticed something — and the DM handed it to him reluctantly, on the grounds that he had been trying to get the players to do their own roleplaying and they kept handing him the drow.

Vrenn, quietly

"Did you guys hear that?" (pointing northeast) "There's something in the trees over there. It sounded big."

Active Perception all round — 15, 15, 13, 11, and an 18-passive Vrenn who rolled a 7 — turned up nothing to see, but two things to notice:

  • It does not smell like a healthy forest. Not rotten. Funky. Mildewed.
  • A very faint fog low in the taller grass, with a greenish tinge to it. Not coming from any direction. Everywhere. It had been there the whole time and they had simply not looked down.

And then, out of the woods, from somewhere impossible to place — a deep, almost snarling voice.

As played

"Who sent you?"

[[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] used Divine Sense. Nothing — no celestial, fiend or undead within sixty feet.

"No one." — "You smell like the Abyss. Who sent you?"

[[tito|Tito]] went to work. "We belong here" — deliberately meaning Faerûn rather than the forest, which is why it is not a lie, and which is why the DM made him roll straight Deception instead of with advantage once he committed to meaning the forest. He rolled it to 26.

A couple of seconds of silence. Then: "I think you're mistaken. You belong to the West." Which made no sense to anybody. "Your friends do not belong here. You're on my property."

Tito tried Persuasion next — a performative little bow, "we are but passing through, headed to the southwest, to the village outside of the woods, with no harm intended" — and rolled a natural 1. A 13 would have been enough and his modifier alone clears it, which is exactly the argument the table then had (see the rulings block). It did not go over.

But the shape of the deal arrived anyway: "To leave is what you wish?" — yes — "Everything has a price. You must pay my price if you wish to leave my land."

4. The Head Through the Underbrush

Crunching from the northwest. A shadow, and then a head — a very large green dragon head pushed through the thicket. Not a youngling. Somewhere in the adult-to-ancient band and hard to place exactly in the gloom under that canopy. Nobody present is a ranger with dragons as a favoured enemy, so nobody got more than that.

He looked them over, slowly, plainly assessing. Then: "First I need to know if I can trust you." And, about Vrenn, immediately: "That one I cannot trust."

Tito's second lie, and it is the one that mattered

"He is our prisoner." — rolled to 30.

Vrenn took it without a flicker. He has spent his life around things far more powerful than a green dragon and it showed; then he looked at Tito with an expression that said, plainly: if I'm your prisoner, you have to speak for me. I can't answer the question myself.

Then the dragon looked at [[silas|Silas]] — standing there on a handful of hit points — and closed the evening down.

As played

"First of all, you need some rest. I'll be here when you wake up."

And he withdrew his head into the foliage and went back to being unseen.

5. Morvek Knows

The moment the dragon was gone, [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] went down on his knees, holding his head and shaking it, for fifteen or twenty seconds. Then he came to.

DM Only — what that was. The party have not been told

That was a sending, and the party were never told so. Morvek cast it; Vrenn received twenty-five words; the table saw only a man on the ground holding his head. The words were deliberately not read out — and Vrenn did not use his right of reply, out of fear. Neither of those has been said to the players. What they got instead was everything below, which the DM judged would make it evident, and largely does.

On "I don't know how that works exactly", below: he is being honest. He knows he was sent to; he does not know what the Token reports on its own.

As played

"Morvek knows. He knows I'm here."

"Whatever you need to — if you need to give me to this dragon, that's fine. I don't have a fate either way. Either I go with this dragon, if that's the price you have to pay to leave, or I wait for Morvek to come and collect me. I have no good ending right now, so you do what you need to do."

Asked when Morvek comes, he reasoned it out loud, and it is the most useful thing anybody said all session:

  • He does not think Morvek has a tuning fork. "I don't think he has a tuning fork on him. I don't think there's one left in the basement — but I also know he's a very, very resourceful lich. He's probably going to get one very soon." He does not expect him to have the arcane capacity to plane shift today.
  • He cannot be scried across planes. Morvek knows he is on the Material Plane; he cannot come to this spot unless he can scry, and he cannot scry unless he is already here.
  • So: "Two to three days is probably the best guess. Two if he really wants to come and get me. Three to four if he has other priorities — because he knows he can track me anywhere. I'm never safe."
  • How Morvek knows, Vrenn cannot say. "He did say he knows I'm in the mortal plane — but that could be more the grave token speaking to him, and maybe its connection to me tells him where I am. I don't know how that works exactly."

6. The Night in the Dome

[[guntrah|Guntrah]] asked the dragon his name, politely, and got it.

As played — and he offered the short form himself

"Mr. Dragon? What's your name, sir?"

"Tedrovaxilliath."

"Do you have a shorter name?"

"Thax. You can call me Ted."

Then Guntrah asked two more questions in the same register — are we good till morning ("Sure"), and do you mind if I gather firewood and make a fire, it's okay if it's too much.

There was no verbal answer. A whole tree came shooting out of the woods and landed on the ground next to him.

Guntrah thanked him, chopped it with his greataxe while [[ollie|Ollie]] hauled, cleared a patch, and had Ollie light it with his flamethrower. It is cold here — autumn, not winter. He asked whether being polite earned him bonus points. The DM said maybe.

[[silas|Silas]] then spent eleven minutes on Leomund's Tiny Hut — a twenty-foot-across opaque dome, which came as a surprise to most of the table, who had it filed as much smaller. The fire stayed outside; the dome is temperature-controlled anyway, and has a dimmer.

Inside the dome — and this is where the session's real argument happened

Vrenn: "I can tell you from experience, if he was going to kill us he would have just bitten our heads off. He wouldn't have talked to us. If something that powerful wants you dead, you're just dead. If he kept us alive, it's because he wants something from us. So you're in a position of strength, if you think about it." (and, about himself) "I'm in a shitty position."

His own plan: "I'm trying to think of a way to pit the dragon against the lich. Maybe if I'm lucky the lich will die and I can collect my token." And the reason it will not work: a green who learns a lich is coming will not accept him in the deal. "He'll want somebody else instead. Or you tell him something else — not necessarily a person. That's the way green dragons work. They trade information, they trade creatures, assets."

And then the Grave Token came out, unprompted and in full. The party asked what the thing on Morvek's belt was, and got the whole of it:

As played — the most he has ever given them about himself

"He calls it a grave token. I don't know if you're familiar with the Nine Hells — they have these things called soul coins. When you own somebody's soul it's physically manifested in a coin, and it's a currency in the Hells. He went down there and learned all about it, and he wanted to bring that knowledge with him to the Abyss. And so he invented these things called grave tokens."

"I don't regret it, I suppose. But I was a fool, and I did sell him my soul."

Why did you do it in the first place? — "He had my sister."

So you traded yourself? — "I traded myself for her. Yes."

Whether destroying it gets his soul back, he does not know. Morvek wears the amulet everywhere he goes; about the phylactery, Vrenn has no idea at all.

The party refused to hand him over. Tito was the pragmatist — a lich chasing them is worse than a dragon eating a drow — and lost the argument in about four lines. Guntrah did not feel comfortable with it. Silas and Tavian closed it: he's our friend. He helped us out of a tough situation. We're friends, us four — well, he's part of our crew.

Vrenn's own last word on it: "Honestly, your best bet is don't tell him anything about me and let him take me. If Morvek shows up, great. If not, I have a new master." And, asked why he seemed so ready to go: "I just know that my fate is not my own, and I've come to accept that."

Eight hours in the dome, nobody needing to trance. Everyone woke up at level 11.

7. The Morning Deal

The dome dropped at dawn and the dragon was fully in the clearing, ten feet away, head about halfway to the canopy when he stood up straight. "Are you ready?"

Guntrah said "Morning, Ted." — "I don't have patience for pleasantries." (Silas asked whether he had patience for pastries. He did not.)

The offer, as played — and note what is in it

"What do you offer for our creature, our off-worlder?"

"I will give you free passage to leave this wood wherever you choose, but you can keep your drow. I need you to take care of a nuisance of mine."

"To the north, about six miles, at the edge of my domain, there is a crack in the ground. There are two beasts — monsters, I don't know what they are — scavenging in my forest and retreating into their cave. I need them eliminated."

He did not ask for Vrenn. The price turned out to be labour.

DM Only — the read on that, as recorded after the session

He did not want Vrenn. He never wanted Vrenn. The prisoner lie had done its work the night before, and the price turned out to be labour.

Session 5 turns the second half over: he knew he had been lied to, and says so. See [[session_5|Session 5]].

While this was going on, Vrenn's Perception of 24 caught what nobody else had, and he nudged the man who was pretending to own him: look at his wing. A tear through the membrane, scarred over, plainly usable but plainly unpleasant to use. The DM then let everybody see it.

Tito opened with "I'm afraid that offer is not enough" and then, warned that dragons negotiate well, do not like to lose, and get harder to move the more you ask for, chose not to push. He rewrote the ask instead, and it is the cleanest piece of play in the session:

As played

"How many beasts did you say there were?" — "I know of two, but there could be more."

"I will accept that deal for two beasts. However, if there is more than two, this deal will need to be renegotiated."

"Very well."

"We will bring proof of the amount of creatures we have slain." — "I will know if you do. I have ears and eyes all through the forest."

Two attempts to do something decent about the wing, both of which failed and both of which mattered:

  • [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] asked consent and laid hands on it. Ted hesitated, did not back off, and tracked him with his face the whole time — if you do something, I will eat you. Tavian, whose aura makes anyone near him immune to fear, went ahead. It did no good: the wound is scarred over, not open, and hit points do not touch it.
  • [[guntrah|Guntrah]] offered to build something. A mesh, a brace — not today, but if they ever come back through. "You will not be here that long." He pushed, flew up to demonstrate his competence, nearly gave offence ("Are you taunting me?"), and rolled 6 on Persuasion at a lowered threshold. "I'll consider it." And then: "If you want to be in my good graces, you will do what I've asked you."

8. The Walk North, and the Fox

He led them out himself, flattening a path through the brush — a little east of north, mostly straight north — and then stopped. "This is as far as I'll go. Follow this fox."

Before he went, Guntrah asked the questions that got the best answers of the session:

As played

You didn't kill these things — are they tough? — "No. I could eat them if I chose."

Why didn't you? — "Because I can't fit in the hole."

You couldn't spray your poison breath in there? — "I have. Once." So it goes pretty deep? — "I wouldn't know. Anything that I send in there gets eaten."

They're large? — "They are large. But they can fit in places I cannot."

If we're in trouble, will you help us? — "Probably not."

And when we come back out? — "I will have eyes and ears. I will see you, and they will escort you to me."

On the way in, the forest changed, and [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] — who is from the Feywild and knows what a healthy fey forest looks like — was the one who felt it. Nothing grows straight. Vines everywhere, thorns, things that look poisonous. Still a forest, but a corrupted one rather than a true one. The green mist is not just in the clearing; it permeates everything, and the DM was explicit that this is the dragon's effect and that it weakens the further you get from his lair.

Then the fox. It hopped out of the thicket, stood next to Ted for a second looking mesmerised rather than enchanted — simply in awe of him — and set off at a pace the party had to run to match, through difficult terrain with no path. Ted turned off in another direction and left.

Tito talks to a fox

Out of earshot, Tito burned his once-per-long-rest Universal Speech — four creatures, no shared language needed, one hour — on it, and told it to slow down.

It stopped, stood up, looked around and then at him, like a creature seeing for the first time in its life. It has no intelligence to speak of, but it understood him. It cocked its head and halved its pace.

Then he asked it where its master keeps his treasures. It paused, cocked its head, looked around, and stood there. He had asked a question it could not answer, and he could not tell whether that meant it did not know.

The treasure question became a running argument — Guntrah wanted to get out alive, Tito wanted to get rich and make a name, and the DM noted drily that a rich kid worrying this much about money is holding a purse that is not as full as he would like. [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] was the one who said the thing the campaign needs remembering: "We've got to touch base with Dr. Voss. She's got to be wondering where the hell we are. We went into some office and then just vanished."

9. The Breach

And then Vrenn recognised it. He stopped. The vegetation, the landscape, all of it was starting to feel familiar, and he was confident enough that he could have taken them the rest of the way himself.

As played

"Stop — this actually seems really familiar. I think this is where I— oh my God, it's the same. It's the same cave."

Almost ten years ago. And, flatly: "I can assure you that there were no creatures guarding the exit to that cave. I just ran right in."

They had been walking over an hour. Just before the cave they had to cross a stream — more than a stream, nearly a river — and topped off waterskins there, which mattered because the DM had ruled at the long rest that they were out of water and would have to find some. Beyond the crossing, an incline still inside the forest.

The Breach, as described: not a crack — a large opening, fifteen to twenty feet high, coming to a point at the top, far too tight for a dragon and easy for something slightly smaller. A good number of bones outside, more inside, and reddish splatter. And, high up where the opening narrows, scrape marks in the rock where something has been going in and out and grinding along the stone. The fox stopped and sat, and would not go closer.

Inside: about forty feet down a ten-foot-wide passage with a fifteen-to-twenty-foot ceiling, opening into a very large cavern with forty-to-fifty-foot ceilings, an underground river running through it, glowing mushrooms in pockets, and stalactites and stalagmites everywhere — some of the stalagmites ten feet high and impossible to simply step over. This is the mouth of the Underdark proper, and it is exactly where Vrenn came up.

10. The Fight

They prepared properly, for the first time in four sessions, and it showed.

  • Vrenn upcast invisibility at 7th level over all five of them — his whole remaining top slot.
  • He cast light on Roach, Tavian's spectral warhorse, in faint blue — twenty feet bright, twenty more dim — and Roach went in first as scout and lamp, with Tavian in telepathic contact.
  • [[guntrah|Guntrah]] readied his new Spell-Storing Item: web, in his greataxe, cast free, held for the first thing that showed itself.
  • [[tito|Tito]] pre-lit a bullseye lantern and kept the hood on it, on the DM's own recommendation.

Roach walked in showing no fear at all, found the water and the mushrooms, and — on Tavian's instruction — kicked a stalagmite to raise an echo. The echo went everywhere and was far louder than anybody expected. Shuffling. Deep baritone groaning.

Then, at the edge of the little ball of light, an enormous shape came in and cracked the horse across the back with a greatclub — nineteen hit points down to five. Roach turned and ran for the party; the light went with it.

Guntrah's readied web fired — DC 18 Dexterity — and caught the fomorian. It also caught Roach, who rolled a 4.

The two of them, by their table names

"Clubby" — the first one, webbed near the entrance. "Hunch" — the second, who arrived a round later. DM bookkeeping names only; neither was ever said in the fiction.

The minis were enormous, and the size of them did more work than any description. Everyone agreed that a fomorian at roughly twenty feet, badly hunched, is worse than a straight twenty feet.

Initiative: Guntrah 15, Tavian 15, Vrenn 14, Silas 13, Tito 11, Clubby 11, with Ollie and Roach riding their owners' counts.

Round one. Guntrah flew to the forty-foot ceiling and put a crossbow bolt and an Arcane Jolt into Clubby for 20. Roach landed a hoof for 13 from inside the web. Tavian's guiding bolt missed and he moved up. Vrenn cast hold monster and Clubby rolled a 3 — paralysed on top of restrained.

Then Hunch arrived, destroyed Roach with one swing — the steed simply vanished, and the cavern went black for [[tito|Tito]], the only one without darkvision — and turned on Tavian with Evil Eye. Tavian rolled a natural 20 on the Charisma save and took half: 13 psychic.

[[silas|Silas]] put phantasmal killer on Hunch, who failed at 14 against a DC of 17: 15 psychic, frightened, disadvantage on ability checks and attacks, and 4d10 more at the end of every one of his turns. It ran for the rest of the fight and did more damage than anything else on the board.

[[tito|Tito]] unhooded the bullseye lantern as a free action — a sixty-foot cone of bright light and sixty more of dim, which solved the darkness problem for everybody — and dropped chromatic orb on Clubby for 23 acid, bouncing it to Hunch for 32. Bardic Inspiration to Tavian.

Clubby broke the paralysis at the end of his turn on a 17. Still webbed. Still very angry.

Round two. Guntrah, with advantage from the web, hit twice — 14 force, then 9 plus Branding Smite for 11 radiant. Ollie closed forty feet and landed a natural 20 Rend for 15. Tavian's second guiding bolt hit for 15 radiant and lit Clubby up for the next attacker.

Then Vrenn, who had watched Tito do it, cast chromatic orb himself at 2nd level, rolled a natural 20, and did 31 thunder to Clubby and 45 on the bounce to Hunch — 76 damage out of a 2nd-level slot. Then he walked up and stood next to Tavian, because being inside a paladin's auras feels better and he does not know why.

Hunch's turn was the only dangerous one. Frightened, he could not approach Silas, so he walked straight over the top of Ollie without appearing to notice him and swung twice at Vrenn. Tavian's Interception soaked 13 of the first hit; Ollie's Deflect Attack imposed disadvantage on the second and it missed on a natural 1. Then Hunch fixed on Vrenn and used Evil Eye.

The one lasting cost of this fight, and it landed on the NPC

Charisma save: 3, plus 3 from Tavian's Aura of Protection, plus 5 from Guntrah's Flash of Genius. Eleven. Not enough.

Vrenn is cursed with magical deformities. Speed halved; disadvantage on ability checks, saving throws and attacks based on Strength or Dexterity. He repeats the save only when he finishes a long rest, and may fail it again. Plus 16 damage.

The curse is magical and does not lift when the caster dies. The DM checked, out loud, because Guntrah asked.

Phantasmal killer came due at the end of Hunch's turn — save of 10 against 17 — for 18 more.

[[silas|Silas]] finished Clubby with fireball, using Sculpt Spells so that it touched nobody on his side, burning the web off both of them for an extra 2d4 on the one still in it. Twenty damage was low for a fireball and it was enough. [[tito|Tito]] finished Hunch with Unsettling Words for −9 on the save and then blight — CON 19 minus 9 is 10, against a DC of 16 — for 34 necrotic.

Two dead fomorians, for 13 points of damage to one paladin. The DM's verdict on why they had beaten a dragon and lost to this: "They're big and ugly and not particularly strong." The whole of their advantage was a hole Ted did not fit into.

11. What Was Actually In There

  • Eighty gold pieces between the two of them. Nothing else on the bodies. Tito searched the cavern on a 17 with Tavian's help and found no treasure at all.
  • Tavian cut an ear off each as proof, on the ruling that left ears are distinguishable from right ears and two lefts is two creatures. They then dropped them, because Ted had already said he would know.
  • The back of the cavern goes deeper, through a narrow throat much like the entrance — and Guntrah, flying a circuit, heard grinding and stomping down there that seemed to be getting closer.
Vrenn on what else is down there

"These things are traditionally tribal. It's unusual to see just two of them. It's also possible they were separated from their tribe. There could be more there. They could be fugitives from their tribe, like I was."

Guntrah wanted to draw more out through the choke point and grind them. Tavian: "I'm not a smart man, but I'm pretty sure this is Milestone." They left. The noise below is unresolved and they know it.

12. The Walk Back, and the Pond

The fox had followed them into the cave and watched the entire fight from the shadows, which nobody noticed until Vrenn pointed it out afterwards. Guntrah greeted it. It looked at him and did not respond, because it is a fox.

Out, up the steep climb, and roughly two hours back to where Ted had handed them off, then thirty minutes down the side path Ted had taken — and into somewhere new.

Where Ted was waiting

A small, very swampy pond, ringed with thorny brush. Something about it is faintly unnatural: straight lines in old timber, the remains of something man-made. "Like maybe it used to be a witch's hut or something. I don't know."

Late afternoon, four o'clock or so. The fox hopped over to him; they appeared to communicate telepathically; and Ted said: "Ah. Very well. Thank you."

The closing negotiation, which established the limits of the relationship precisely:

  • "Where is it you wish to leave my forest?"
  • Guntrah, angling for more — can you help us, is there a town — got: "I owe nothing to you except to allow you to leave my forest alive."
  • They asked about the smoke to the southwest. "There are towns all around this wood. They invade my space and I eat them." Pressed on whether there is one specifically southwest: "Yes. There is one in that direction."
  • Tavian asked whether he knew of the Academy. "I know nothing of this academy you speak of."
  • He offered a guide — "I will have one of my creatures go with you" — and they took it. The fox again.
  • They asked for one more rest. "I will allow one more rest." They chose to take it back in the arrival clearing, and he sent the fox to lead them there.

Nothing material changed hands. What they got was passage, a direction, a guide, and a second night's grace.

13. Where It Stopped

Following a fox back toward the clearing, in the evening, intending to leave southwest in the morning, for the settlement whose fire Guntrah saw from above the canopy. Guntrah's closing line, entirely sincere: "I really feel like Ted should be a guy to be with us. He's a nice guy."

DM Only — RULINGS MADE AT THE TABLE (2026-09-24)

  • Natural 1s and natural 20s apply to ability checks, not just attacks. Adopted as a house rule this session — the DM already played it that way, Tavian and Tito agreed, Silas objected, and it went in anyway. Both directions: a nat 20 on a check succeeds as well.
  • Silver Tongue still eliminates natural 1s. Tito argued against his own feature for the fun of it and lost on the rules. Any Persuasion or Deception d20 of 9 or lower counts as a 10, so his floor is 23 with Bardic Inspiration. It was never actually applied; his nat 1 on the "passing through" persuasion would have succeeded anyway on the modifier alone.
  • Leomund's Tiny Hut and sound: one-way. The party can hear what happens outside; nothing outside can hear them. Ruled on the sneaky-purpose argument. This reverses the direction assumed in the prep, where the whole point was that Ted could negotiate through it. It never came up, because he withdrew before the dome went up.
  • All-Purpose Tool cannot become a telescope. Artisan's tools only; a telescope is adventuring gear.
  • Paralysed auto-crits are melee only. Advantage for everyone, auto-crit only within 5 feet. Caught mid-fight and applied correctly.
  • Frightened gives disadvantage on attacks and ability checks, not saving throws, and only restricts movement toward the source. Hunch could still smash whatever was in front of him.
  • Evil Eye's curse is magical and outlives its caster. Killing the fomorian did not free Vrenn.
  • Find steed shares the paladin's initiative and may act at the same time as him rather than strictly after — checked at the table, unlike the Steel Defender, which does go after Guntrah.
  • Visibility from a hundred feet up is about twelve miles.
  • Post-long-rest the party is out of water and has to find fresh water. A deliberate one-off carried over from the Abyss, resolved by the stream crossing on the way north.
  • Ring of the Ram regained 1 charge on a 1d6 roll. RAW is 1d3 at dawn; the table rolled a d6 and got a 1, so it makes no difference. Tavian's ring is at 1 charge.
  • Proficiency bonus was called as going from +3 to +4 at level 11. It was already +4 at level 10, so the sheets are unchanged and correct. Recorded only so that nobody "fixes" them later.

DM Only — WHAT CHANGED FROM THE PREP. Four of these supersede locked decisions.

  1. THE SENDING HAPPENED — IT WAS SIMPLY NEVER NARRATED. The 2026-09-23 design and the "option A" text both stand (DM, confirmed 2026-09-25). Morvek cast sending and [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] received it; what the table saw was a man on his knees holding his head for fifteen or twenty seconds. The words were never read out — which is exactly what the prep called for ("the exact phrasing does not matter, because the players never hear it"). The DM's read was that the conversation immediately afterwards made it evident, and it did: Vrenn came up saying "Morvek knows," then reasoned out the whole clock. So the mechanism is a sending, not a wordless Token pulse. Recorded here because the raw transcript alone does not show it, and a later reader would get this wrong.
  2. AND THEREFORE THE REPLY IS STILL UNSPENT. Sending permits an immediate twenty-five-word answer and Vrenn did not use it, out of fear. The prep's instruction was to tell the party he could have answered and didn't — that never happened, so it is still available, and it is the most revealing single fact about him. Any player who later learns what a sending is can work it out backwards. See [[what_follows_vrenn|What Follows Vrenn]].
  3. VRENN RECOGNISED THE COUNTRY, AND THE CAVE IS HIS. SUPERSEDES the 2026-09-24 ruling that plane shift is inaccurate and Vrenn is as lost as the party. He arrived unable to place it — that part held — but on the walk north the landscape came back to him, and [[underdark_breach|the Breach]] is the exact cave he came out of when he fled Menzoberranzan about ten years ago. He was confident he could have led them the last of the way. Consequences: the protection the ruling was buying (Vrenn not becoming a guidebook) is partly spent, and he is now a man who has personally been to one specific place in this forest. It cost nothing this session because Ted was still the only source for everything that mattered, but he is no longer a blank on local geography and should not be played as one.
  4. THE PARTY NAMED NEVERWINTER WOOD THEMSELVES. SUPERSEDES "do not let anyone identify it without being told." Guntrah's flight above the canopy plus Silas's History 16 got there in the first twenty minutes, off the rivers and the mixed conifers. The session lost nothing — knowing the forest's name and knowing anything useful turned out to be completely different problems, which is a better lesson than being told.
  5. THE REWARD NEVER HAPPENED. All five prepped options are unspent and the party got nothing material. They have 80 gp and no potions. THIS IS THE BIGGEST LIVE CONSEQUENCE OF THE SESSION: a level-11 party with zero healing consumables walking out of a forest into a settlement. Either the potions arrive somewhere else, or that becomes a real pressure the next session honours. See [[tedrovaxilliath|Ted's entity]], where the list is preserved intact.
  6. The three omens ran at about a third strength. The fog landed — but as "funky, mildewed, not quite rotten" with a faint green tinge low in the grass, not the chlorine/rot/too-much-pine construction that was written. The twisted trails landed later, on the walk north, as an explicit corruption gradient that weakens with distance from the lair, with Tavian's Feywild origin as the lens. The silent spies never happened. Ted simply told them: "I have ears and eyes all through the forest." And then the fox did the whole job in person and became the best NPC in the session. The staring-animals set-piece is still unspent and should probably stay that way now that the fox exists.
  7. He offered the short form himself. Asked politely for his name he said "Tedrovaxilliath", and when asked for a shorter one offered "Thax. You can call me Ted." The designed humiliation beat did not happen and cannot now — no player shortened it, so there was nothing to be silently magnanimous about. "Thax" is new and is his. This makes him materially warmer than designed; see his entity.
  8. He never told the treant story and the wing was never explained. Vrenn spotted the scar on a 24 and the party saw it; two of them tried to fix it; Ted volunteered nothing. [[the_standing_treant|The treant]] is completely unspent and the party has no idea there is a story.
  9. He stated the reason he needs them outright — "Because I can't fit in the hole" — rather than leaving it to be inferred from the wing, and added two things that were not prepped: he breathed poison into the cave once, and "anything that I send in there gets eaten."
  10. Order of events inverted. Prepped: arrive → sending → rest → Ted. Played: arrive → recon → Ted's voice and a first negotiation → Ted sends them to bed → the Token → rest → the deal. He opened the negotiation before they could rest, then chose to let them sleep, which did the intimidation and the courtesy in one move.
  11. The Breach's layout is different. The prepped fallen-giant root-plate and low fouled antechamber were not used. What was played is a large pointed opening with scrape marks high on the rock, bones and blood, a forty-foot descent, and an enormous cavern with an underground river and glowing mushrooms. [[underdark_breach|The entity]] has been rewritten to the played version.
  12. Six miles, not five, and the water crossing was a stream rather than a river. It cost nothing and served as the water resupply.
  13. The fomorian-alliance branch never opened and no language key was ever needed. Preserved below.
