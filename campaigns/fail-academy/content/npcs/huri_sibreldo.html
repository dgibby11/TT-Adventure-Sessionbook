A small, round, comprehensively cheerful widow who rents out her spare room in Triel and spends essentially every waking hour in her garden. She is delighted by visitors, feeds them immediately and without consultation, and talks continuously about soil.

The garden is immaculate in a way that goes past pride and into discipline — raised beds squared to the inch, nothing bolted, nothing crowded, not one weed. Herbs and vegetables, mostly. Alongside them, in their own beds and just as fastidiously kept, a number of things that nobody grows for a kitchen: reagents, and the sort of reagents an adventuring druid would once have needed on hand. She has apparently never stopped growing them. She has not needed a single one of them in a century and a half.

She has no interest whatsoever in whatever the party is doing. She will not ask. If told, she will nod politely and return to the subject of her tomatoes.

DM Only — the crossed-out name

The strike-through is the hook. A crossed-off name is the single most interesting thing on that page and the players will go straight to it. That is intended. [[ellery_voss|Voss]] wrote her down out of fondness and struck her out because she is retired and it serves no purpose — but she wrote her first, and struck her after, and Voss is not a sentimental woman about much. If the party asks her about it directly she will say, honestly and a little too quickly, that Huri is retired and they should not trouble her.

Keep the strike legible. On the physical prop, one clean line through the name with the city still readable underneath — a scratched-out illegible blot loses the entire beat.

DM Only — the garden

This is the DM's own callback to a previous campaign and is deliberately left as an empty container here. Fill the component beds with whatever the specific throwback actually is at your table — the point is that a player who was in that campaign recognizes what is growing in a retired gnome's vegetable patch in a town nobody has any reason to visit. Do not explain it. Do not let Huri explain it. If pushed, she deflects into gardening: how long that particular one takes to establish, what it does to the soil, whether they'd like some to take with them. She will absolutely give them cuttings.

She is not a plot device and should not become one. She rents them the room, feeds them, and declines to be involved — not evasively, just genuinely. She is done. Resisting the urge to make her useful is what makes the eyebrow-raise land; the moment she starts dispensing plot she is just another quest-giver.

DM Only — why Voss is fond of her

Soft lore, flag before canonizing. Voss is in her late forties and Huri taught 150 years ago, so they were never colleagues. The proposed connection: Huri is one of the very few people still alive who knew Voss's predecessor at the head of the Ninth Thesis, and Voss has met her a handful of times because of it. This gives the fondness real weight and creates no plot obligation — but it touches the Voss leadership lineage (see ninth_thesis.html, nerissa_voss), so overrule it freely if you'd rather the connection be something else entirely, or nothing at all.
