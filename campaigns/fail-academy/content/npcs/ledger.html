The only entry on Voss's list that is not a name. In Mirabar he is Ledger, to everyone, in every context — it is what the assayers call him, what the weighhouse clerks call him, and what he answers to. Voss knows his birth name perfectly well and did not write it down, because it would not have helped: nobody in that city would recognize it.

Grey-bearded early for a dwarf, unhurried, and permanently mid-arithmetic. He works the assay and weighing side of Mirabar's ore trade — the part of the city where every wagon that leaves is written down twice and the numbers are expected to match. He settles disputes quietly for people who would rather not involve the Council, which is why he is known and why he is careful.

What he is worth: movement. Mirabar's trade reaches the whole North, and Ledger's whole professional life is noticing when a shipment's paperwork does not agree with the shipment. If something is being moved through the North that should not exist, he is the operative most likely to have already seen the shape of it without knowing what it was.

DM Only

The obstacle is the nickname, and it should cost them days, not a roll. Mirabar runs on written records and a nickname is not something you can look up. Asking openly after "Ledger" in the wrong quarter is worse than useless: it gets back to him, he assumes someone is checking on him, and he goes quiet for a tenday. Resolve this as legwork rather than a single check — who they ask, and how. Traders, assayers, and the weighhouse are the right kind of people; guards, officials, and anyone with a badge are exactly wrong.

He finds them. Once they've asked the right people the right way, don't make them succeed at a search — have him walk up to their table two or three days later, sit down uninvited, and ask who's been saying his name. Play the first minute as genuinely dangerous. It stops being dangerous the moment Voss's name comes out.

Recruited by Voss's predecessor, not Voss — he is a few years older in the organization than she is at the head of it. He is not sentimental about that and does not trade on it, but he refers to the work in slightly older terms than Voss uses, which a sharp player might notice. There is nothing sinister in it.

Forward hook (do not push): the corrupted items reaching campus arrived somehow, and Thornwick's supply lines run through the North. Ledger is the natural person to eventually put a shipping pattern in front of the party. Keep him dormant until they ask a question that earns it — he does not volunteer, and he has no idea any of it matters.
