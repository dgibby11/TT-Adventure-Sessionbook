He was working when the party arrived — not resting, working — with heavy gloves on and something half-finished in his hands. He set it down and turned round at the sound of them landing, and the first thing out of his mouth was not a challenge:

What he said

"Oh — I wasn't — it's not time. I told you I needed another day."

Then, as it registered that they were not who he was expecting: "You're not— you need to leave. You need to leave immediately. You've got to go. This is not good."

He does not look weak so much as worn all the way through. He flinches easily. When the hoofbeats started on the bridge he said "it's too late, it's too late," crouched as low as he could get, and went under his workbench — not hidden, plainly visible, just blocking out the world. He stayed there for the whole fight.

The Scroll

Mid-fight he was seen reaching up over the edge of the bench, groping for something above him, and pulling down a scroll, which he unrolled as though about to read. He never read it.

When the second creature went down he rolled it up, came out fast, and crossed to [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] — the one who had spoken to him — and put it in his hands.

The handoff

"You need to get out of here immediately. There's going to be fifteen more of them here in about a minute. You have to go now."

"They might kill me, they might not, I don't know. But if I leave now, I'm dead anyway. So just — just go. It's the best thing."

"Where do we go?" — "Read the scroll."

It was a plane shift scroll. [[silas|Silas]] tried to cast from it cold and rolled a natural 1; the spell went out of the scroll and took nothing with it. He gave away the only thing that was ever going to get him out, and watched it burn for nothing.

What the Party Tried

Very little, and there was no time for more. Who are you? Where are we? — no answers. [[tito|Tito]] stepped in front of the others and gambled the one name they had: "Thatch sent us."

"I don't know who that is."

DM Only — Vrenn

His name is Vrenn. He simply never introduced himself and nobody asked. Per the DM (2026-08-23) the name is expected to land in Session 3. Until it does, every player-facing reference stays "the drow" — do not let "Vrenn" appear anywhere the players can read.

Do not confuse him with Wren Halloway — different character entirely, and speech-to-text will merge them if allowed to. See planning/transcript_glossary.md.

Full history is in planning/vrenn_the_captive.json and is entirely intact and unspent — nothing leaked in Session 2. Summary: a captive, not a willing worker, held long enough that something in him has bent rather than broken. He has changed hands multiple times. His craft is real and unusual — he primes raw material so that it takes enchantment unusually well — but he has been deliberately kept just short of performing the enchantment himself, so he stays useful and dependent. That is the cruellest part of his captivity and worth playing as genuine tragedy.

Playing him: he talks, sometimes at length, once someone gets him started — and almost none of it answers a direct question. Real questions get deflection, a fixation on some small procedural detail of the labour, a thought that trails off, or an answer to a slightly different question. Not refusal; more that the part of him that used to process a question and give a straight answer doesn't quite work any more. He is wary of strangers by reflex — "outsiders" have never meant rescue here.

Exactly two things are allowed to slip through, because both are small enough not to feel like answers: Vrask's name, and an offhand remark that there are more of them now than there used to be (traffic increasing). Everything else about the site, the operation, or Torvald stays out of his mouth however good the roleplay is.

The gift Session 2 handed you: "it's not time — I told you I needed another day." He mistook the party for someone he owes work to on a schedule. The players heard that clearly, and it implies a supervisor and a delivery cadence without naming either. If they ever get a real conversation with him, that is the door — and it mirrors the periodic check-in pattern in torvald_the_insider.json, which is deliberate: this is how the whole supply chain runs.

[[torvald_thatch|Torvald]]'s name entered this scene from a player's mouth, not the DM's, and the honest "I don't know who that is" gave nothing away. The guardrail held better than a refusal would have — the party now has a real data point that the groundskeeper is unknown at this end of the chain.

He can be won over, slowly, by someone making an actual effort rather than a single Persuasion roll. He has some capability in a fight and will not use it unless cornered. SUPERSEDED in part (DM, 2026-08-26): the old line that he "could even leave with them" is no longer the ruling — see the Session 3 block below. He will leave the site. He will not leave the plane.

DM Only — Session 3: his name, his plan, and what it costs him

Scope: this applies if the party hides nearby and comes back at dawn. If they head east to the structure instead, most of it is deferred — that branch is not designed yet. Full mechanism in planning/session3_draft.html.

He introduces himself, unprompted, in the middle of an instruction — the fastest way to stop being furniture. And under pressure he plays competent: procedural, clipped, a man who knows this site the way a night-shift worker knows a warehouse. For about four minutes he is the most useful person standing on the platform. That is a register the party has never seen from him and will not expect, and playing it first is what makes his collapse back into the deflecting rambler land later.

Read Aloud — about 18 seconds. He is already moving.

"Vrenn. My name's Vrenn." (hauling the tuning fork out from under the bench) "This is how things leave here. I can finish a scroll with it by dawn — but Vrask comes at dawn too. Or there's the structure east. It has a way out. It has other things as well." (beat) "Decide. I'll do either."

That plants his name, the fork, the deadline, Vrask's name and both routes in one breath, and hands the decision straight back so the rest of the clock belongs to the players.

Follow-ups — one sentence each, so nothing eats the clock:

  • Who's Vrask? — "He owns this. And me. Don't go looking for him."
  • Why won't you come? — "He's got my soul in a coin on his belt. Wherever I am, he knows. You don't want to be near me when he comes."
  • You said fifteen? — "I've watched them a year. Fifteen's what I'd bet. I've been wrong."
  • Where does the scroll send us? — "Neverwinter." That is the whole answer, and it is the whole trap — see the destination note below.
  • What is this place? — deflects into procedure: what he is behind on, what is due, how long the last batch took.
  • (pointing at the runic circle) — "That's not for going. That's for him."
  • What happens to you tomorrow with no scroll? — he dodges: "That's not your problem. Dawn's your problem." Pressed once more, and only once: "…I don't think he kills me. I'm too much work to replace."

He must say one thing plainly before they choose, or their choice is a gotcha: there is only one scroll, and Vrask is coming for it. If they take it, he meets Vrask empty-handed. He states it without asking them not to, and then hands it over anyway. Per DM ruling (2026-08-26) Vrask does not kill him and he may recur later — but the party has no way to know that, and the not-knowing is what they carry into Session 4.

A true hand

How he finishes a 7th-level scroll without being a caster. He is a natural copyist — given prepared vellum, prepared inks and the fork, he transcribes an existing scroll's text exactly, and the copy works. No spell slot. No spellcasting; he has none and is not being given any. He does not understand one character of what he copies. It is the same faculty as his priming, pointed at ink instead of metal: exactness without comprehension, which is precisely not enchantment and therefore still inside the line Vrask keeps him behind. Roughly an hour's work, and it saps him — once per day, no more, and he is worse company afterwards. The name is provisional (vrenn_the_captive.json, the_true_hand); rename freely.

He cannot use it himself. Two reasons, both true, and he only says the first: he would be taken with it, and he could not read or cast what he copies anyway. [[silas|Silas— is the only person who can cast from the finished scroll — [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian— cannot (not on the paladin list) and [[tito|Tito— is a poor fallback.

He will probably give his spare supplies to Silas before they go — vellum, inks, components, pressed on him unprompted and awkwardly, a man handing over the only thing he owns that is worth anything to anybody. This gives Silas materials, not the knack. Silas still scribes by the ordinary rules; he has simply been handed components he would otherwise have to buy.

The soul coin

Vrask carries it on his belt. It is a beacon, not an anchor (DM, 2026-08-26/29) — nothing physically stops Vrenn crossing planes, but the coin tells Vrask where its soul is, always. So leaving is not futile for Vrenn so much as lethal for whoever is standing next to him, and that is the reason he gives. Note the register: it is an argument about protecting them, from a man who handed four strangers his only scroll ninety seconds after meeting them.

The party can therefore actually take him, deliberately. Push hard enough and he can be talked into it; he resists as much as is reasonable and makes the consequence explicit first — Vrask follows, Vrask is incredibly strong, and he intends to be at the workbench at dawn regardless. Let them own the decision.

How he came to sell it: he sold it himself, and not for himself. His sister was going to die at the hands of something in the Undermountain; somebody was willing to trade for that, and what he had to trade was his soul. He does not tell this story and should not be made to — if it surfaces at all it surfaces later, in fragments, somewhere safe. The sister is alive (DM, 2026-08-29) and deliberately open-ended as a future thread; whether Vrenn knows that is undecided and should stay undecided.

What this buys retroactively: "bought and sold multiple times" in vrenn_the_captive.json stops being metaphor. The coin changed hands and he went with it, every time. He was never a person being traded — he was a receipt.

Where he will and will not go

  • Will leave the platform — to hide out in the landscape with them, or to walk east to the structure.
  • Will not leave the plane, and intends to be at his workbench when Vrask arrives at dawn.
  • The licensed slip is still unspent: "there's more of us now than there used to be." Still the cleanest way to land the increasing-traffic clue that failed to land in Session 2.
