He was working when the party arrived — not resting, working — with heavy gloves on and something half-finished in his hands. He set it down and turned round at the sound of them landing, and the first thing out of his mouth was not a challenge:

What he said, Session 2

"Oh — I wasn't — it's not time. I told you I needed another day."

Then, as it registered that they were not who he was expecting: "You're not— you need to leave. You need to leave immediately. You've got to go. This is not good."

He does not look weak so much as worn all the way through. He flinches easily. When the hoofbeats started on the bridge he said "it's too late, it's too late," crouched as low as he could get, and went under his workbench — not hidden, plainly visible, just blocking out the world. He stayed there for the whole fight.

The Scroll

Mid-fight he was seen reaching up over the edge of the bench, groping for something above him, and pulling down a scroll, which he unrolled as though about to read. He never read it.

When the second creature went down he rolled it up, came out fast, and crossed to [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] — the one who had spoken to him — and put it in his hands.

The handoff

"You need to get out of here immediately. There's going to be fifteen more of them here in about a minute. You have to go now."

"They might kill me, they might not, I don't know. But if I leave now, I'm dead anyway. So just — just go. It's the best thing."

"Where do we go?" — "Read the scroll."

It was a plane shift scroll. [[silas|Silas]] tried to cast from it cold and rolled a natural 1; the spell went out of the scroll and took nothing with it. He gave away a thing it had taken him months to make and watched it burn for nothing — and did not seem annoyed about it, only puzzled, as though he were working out why it had failed.

He worked it out. He tells them so in [[session_3|Session 3]], and it exonerates Silas entirely: he had scribed it with his own magic quill, and had only just learned that this means nobody else can ever cast from it. "It wasn't his fault. Tomorrow I'll use a standard enchanted quill and anybody can use it."

His Name, and Everything After It

Thirty seconds after the fight ended, with hooves already on the causeway, he introduced himself and produced a plan in the same breath.

Session 3, as played

"Friend — my name's Vrenn. Listen, we've got about thirty seconds to get out of here. We can find some place nearby to lay low and hide, or there's a house about an hour's walk. We can go there. Let me grab my stuff."

He was, for the next four minutes, the most useful person on that platform: he packed a satchel, took them round behind the statues to the one place the lava moat is jumpable, made them all fill their waterskins at his cistern first, and got them off the outcrop. Then, behind a rock, he answered questions.

  • His master is [[morvek|Morvek]], a lich, who owns him and whom they are not to go looking for.
  • Morvek holds his soul — "basically the Abyssal equivalent to a soul coin. He has another name for it." He could not or would not produce the name.
  • He can make another scroll, but only one, only at dawn, only after a rest — and Morvek is coming for that one.
  • He has sent about six packages. He remembered the one addressed to Thatch, the groundskeeper. He writes down the words he is given, and has never heard of the man, or of the Academy.
  • He produced a [[steel_tuning_fork|steel tuning fork]] the moment they said Faerûn. Morvek gave it to him because Morvek wanted goods going to the Material Plane; Morvek said Neverwinter for one scroll and gave him a picture rather than a name for the other.
  • Planar travel needs a thin place. Since the Spellplague it barely works at all except where the veil is worn through — which is why the ritual site was built there, and why he assumes [[the_house|the house]] is another one, since Morvek travels from it.
  • He would not leave the plane. "If I were to leave the plane he would know immediately, and he would follow me, and he would kill all of you."

He led them an hour east, picked two locks on the way in with [[guntrah|Guntrah]]'s tool at +6, upcast invisibility over the whole party when a patrol nearly caught them on the plain, talked them past the first guard in the basement on a bare-faced lie, and handed [[silas|Silas]] the tuning fork when he was asked for it — invisibly, on trust, hesitating badly, with the words "I think I've resigned to my fate."

Then two [[basement_oni|oni]] dropped their faces, and he turned around at the foot of the stairs and made a snap decision out loud: well, if I'm going to die, I might as well do what I can to help you. He fought. He stripped every loose component out of that basement so that nothing could follow them. He charged two of the four bowls himself. And when the circle fired he was standing in it, holding somebody's hand, because by then there was nothing left for him on that plane at all.

[[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] had already asked him to come.

[[session_4|Session 4]] — He Told Them Everything, and Then Offered Himself Up

He arrived not knowing where he was. "The one time I was up here on the surface, I emerged from a cave — when I fled Menzoberranzan." A hundred miles underground to get out, about ten years ago, in no direction he can name, because nothing in the Underdark is reckoned by compass points.

Then, minutes after the dragon withdrew, [[morvek|Morvek]] cast sending at him. What the party saw was a man going to his knees holding his head for fifteen or twenty seconds. They were never told it was a spell, never heard the words, and do not know that he had the right to answer and did not take it. He came up with this:

As played

"Morvek knows. He knows I'm here."

"Whatever you need to — if you need to give me to this dragon, that's fine. I don't have a fate either way. I have no good ending right now, so you do what you need to do."

And then he reasoned out the clock himself, which is the most useful thing anybody said all session: Morvek probably has no tuning fork, because the party took the one in the basement; scrying does not cross planes, so he must arrive on the Material Plane before he can locate anybody; and therefore two to three days, two if he is determined, three to four if he has other things to do. "Because he knows he can track me anywhere. I'm never safe."

The Grave Token, in full, unprompted — the most he has ever given them about himself

"He calls it a grave token. I don't know if you're familiar with the Nine Hells — they have these things called soul coins. When you own somebody's soul it's physically manifested in a coin, and it's a currency in the Hells. He went down there and learned all about it, and he wanted to bring that knowledge with him to the Abyss. And so he invented these things called grave tokens."

"I don't regret it, I suppose. But I was a fool, and I did sell him my soul."

Why did you do it in the first place? — "He had my sister." So you traded yourself? — "I traded myself for her. Yes."

His own plan, stated plainly: pit the dragon against the lich, and if he is lucky the lich dies and he collects his token. His own assessment of why it will not work: a green who learns a lich is coming will want somebody else in the deal instead. "That's the way green dragons work. They trade information, they trade creatures, assets." And his fallback: "Honestly, your best bet is don't tell him anything about me and let him take me. If Morvek shows up, great. If not, I have a new master."

The party refused. [[tito|Tito]] made the pragmatic case and lost it in four lines. He's our friend. He helped us out of a tough situation. We're friends, us four — well, he's part of our crew. Vrenn's answer to why he was so ready to go: "I just know that my fate is not my own, and I've come to accept that."

Then [[tito|Tito]] told a green dragon he was their prisoner, on a Deception of 30, and he took it without a flicker — and then looked at Tito with the obvious point: if I'm your prisoner, you have to speak for me. I can't answer the question myself. He has spent his whole life around things more powerful than a green dragon and it showed.

He Has Been Here Before

On the walk north the landscape started coming back to him, and then it stopped being a feeling.

As played

"Stop — this actually seems really familiar. I think this is where I— oh my God, it's the same. It's the same cave."

"I can assure you that there were no creatures guarding the exit to that cave. I just ran right in."

[[underdark_breach|The Breach]] is the hole he came up out of when he fled Menzoberranzan. He was confident he could have taken the party the rest of the way himself. And of the [[fomorians|fomorians]], afterwards: "they could be fugitives from their tribe, like I was."

DM Only — SESSION 4 SUPERSEDES "HE IS LOST TOO" (played 2026-09-24)

The 2026-09-24 ruling was that plane shift is inaccurate, he landed miles off, and he could give the party the forest's name and nothing else. The arrival half held. He did not know where he was and said so. The rest did not: on the walk north he recognised the country, and [[underdark_breach|the Breach]] turned out to be his own escape route.

What that costs, and it is worth being honest about: the ruling existed to stop him becoming a guidebook, and that protection is now partly spent. He is no longer a blank on local geography — he has personally been to one specific place in this wood and walked a hundred miles of country under it. It cost nothing in Session 4 because [[tedrovaxilliath|Ted]] was the only source for anything that mattered, but do not play him as knowing nothing.

What it bought is better than what it cost. The single best beat of the session came free: the escaped slave walking back to the exact hole he escaped through, ten years later, as somebody else's property again. He stood at the top of it and went down anyway.

The two Underdark geographies remain separate, which is the part that mattered. A player who does the arithmetic will ask, so pick an answer before they do.

New and free: he described himself, unprompted, as having been a fugitive from his own people. That is a third thing alongside captive and receipt, and nothing has been built on it.

Undercommon never came up. The [[fomorians|fomorians]] were killed without a word being exchanged, so his unique value as the only Undercommon speaker is entirely unspent and still available.

DM Only — HE BROKE THE COMBAT DIAL, AND HE IS NOW CURSED

The stated policy was "he acts once, decisively, out of despair rather than tactics, and then does something practical and non-combat." Session 4 blew straight through it. He spent invisibility at 7th level on all five of them going in, cast light on the horse to make a scout-lamp, cast hold monster and paralysed a fomorian, and then cast chromatic orb at 2nd level, rolled a natural 20, and did 31 and 45 on the bounce — 76 damage out of a 2nd-level slot, which was more than any player character managed in a single action.

He was also having fun — he copied the spell off [[tito|Tito]] after watching it work, and then walked up and stood next to [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] because being inside a paladin's auras feels better and he does not know why. That is a character developing, not a mistake, but it means the honest dial is now: a Wizard 13 who has decided he is part of a crew, and who is no longer conserving anything because he does not expect to live out the week. Decide whether to rein that in before Session 5, because at this rate he outshoots the party.

The counterweight arrived on its own, and it is elegant: he is the only one who got hurt. He took the greatclub (reduced by Tavian's Interception), he took the Evil Eye, and he failed the save at 11 even with Tavian's +3 aura and [[guntrah|Guntrah]]'s Flash of Genius +5 on top. He is cursed: deformed, speed halved, disadvantage on STR/DEX checks, saves and attacks. He repeats the save on finishing a long rest and may fail again; the curse is magical and did not lift when the fomorian died.

There is a long rest at the end of Session 4. Roll it — and consider failing it once. A visibly deformed drow walking into a human settlement is a scene the campaign has not had, and this party will not abandon him over it.

DM Only — the name is spent, and everything it was gating

"Vrenn" is now player knowledge and the old instruction to write "the drow" everywhere is retired. Player-facing prose may use his name freely from [[session_3|Session 3]] onward. Do not confuse him with Wren Halloway — different character entirely, and speech-to-text merges them every time. See planning/transcript_glossary.md.

Full history in planning/vrenn_the_captive.json. Summary: a captive, not a willing worker, held long enough that something in him has bent rather than broken. He has changed hands multiple times. His craft is real and unusual — he primes raw material so that it takes enchantment unusually well — and for most of his time here he was deliberately kept just short of performing the enchantment himself, so he stayed useful and dependent. That ended recently (DM, 2026-09-01): Morvek needed a scroll-maker on site and taught him plane shift, which he used to make the scroll the party wasted and was part-way through using again when they arrived. So he had the means to leave and did not use it, because of the Grave Token and nothing else. Every day he stayed was a decision rather than an incapacity.

Playing him — and note that Session 3 substantially revised this. The authored register is a man who talks at length and answers almost nothing: deflection, fixation on a procedural detail, a thought that trails off. Under pressure he played the opposite way and it was much better — clipped, procedural, competent, a night-shift worker who knows his warehouse. He answered every direct question the party asked him in Session 3, promptly and usefully. The deflecting rambler is who he goes back to being once the pressure comes off, and that contrast is now available because the table has seen the competent version first.

The licensed slip is STILL unspent: "there's more of us now than there used to be." Traffic increasing. Still the cleanest way to land the clue that failed in Session 2 and failed again in Session 3.

[[torvald_thatch|Torvald]]'s name entered his scene from a player's mouth, not the DM's, twice now, and both times the honest "I don't know who that is" gave nothing away while handing the party a real data point: the groundskeeper is unknown at this end of the chain.

DM Only — The Grave Token. SESSION 4 SPENT BOTH OF ITS SECRETS AND REVERSED A LOCKED DECISION.

TWO THINGS CHANGED AT THE TABLE ON 2026-09-24, AND THE TRANSCRIPT WINS ON BOTH. FLAGGED FOR THE DM RATHER THAN QUIETLY ABSORBED, because one of them was an explicit standing instruction:

  1. The words "grave token" were said out loud, by Vrenn, to the party. The standing note was "do not say 'Grave Token' at the table until somebody actually sees one." He said it unprompted, in the dome, as the name his owner uses. It is player knowledge now. Nothing is really lost — the party asked him directly what the thing on Morvek's belt was, and the honest answer was always going to include the name.
  2. THE NINE HELLS ARE NOW IN THE CAMPAIGN. The locked decision (DM, 2026-08-29) was that a Grave Token is "deliberately NOT an infernal soul coin — nothing in this campaign touches the Nine Hells, and it should never be described in a way that implies devils." Vrenn's explanation did exactly that, and did it deliberately and at length: soul coins are real, they are a currency in the Hells, Morvek personally went down there and learned all about them, and he "wanted to bring that knowledge with him to the Abyss" — so he invented grave tokens. A lich who travelled to the Nine Hells to steal a financial instrument is a much better lich, and it answers "why does an Abyssal operation have soul-binding at all" in one sentence. But it is a real reversal and it opens a door that was deliberately nailed shut.

The lich's-work framing survives intact and is now the mechanism rather than the deflection: a Grave Token is Morvek's own invention — a phylactery with the polarity reversed, built on a device he saw working in another cosmology. It is Abyssal because he made it there, not because the Abyss produces them.

What is still secret: nobody has seen one. Vrenn does not know whether destroying it returns his soul ("I don't know how it works, honestly"). He has no idea where the phylactery is. And nothing has been decided about whether other tokens exist, or who else Morvek owns.

[[morvek|Morvek]] carries it on his belt. It is a beacon, not an anchor (DM, 2026-08-26/29) — nothing physically stops Vrenn crossing planes, which is why he was able to. The Token tells Morvek where its soul is, always. So leaving was never futile for Vrenn so much as lethal for whoever is standing next to him, and that was the reason he gave. Note the register: an argument about protecting them, from a man who handed four strangers his only scroll ninety seconds after meeting them.

How he came to sell it — AND HE HAS NOW TOLD THEM (2026-09-24). He sold it himself, and not for himself. The standing instruction was "he does not tell this story and should not be made to — if it surfaces at all it surfaces later, in fragments, somewhere safe." Session 4 was that somewhere safe, and it worked exactly as designed: asked once, in the dome, on the first quiet night he has ever had in the party's company, he gave it in three lines and stopped. I was a fool, and I did sell him my soul · he had my sister · I traded myself for her, yes.

No details, no scene, and the Undermountain was never named. His sister was going to die at the hands of something there; somebody was willing to trade; what he had to trade was his soul. The sister is alive (DM, 2026-08-29) and deliberately open-ended; whether Vrenn knows that is undecided and should stay undecided. He spoke of her only in the past tense and did not say what became of her, and nobody asked — so all of that is still fully available.

What this buys retroactively: "bought and sold multiple times" stops being metaphor. The Token changed hands and he went with it, every time. He was never a person being traded — he was a receipt.
