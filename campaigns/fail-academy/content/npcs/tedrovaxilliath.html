Name
Tedrovaxilliath. He will give the whole thing, with ceremony. It shortens to "Ted" and he will allow that, eventually, once
What
Adult green dragon — six or seven hundred years old. Deep into adult, not ancient, and close enough to the line to be thinking about it
Where
[[neverwinter_wood|Neverwinter Wood]]. All of it. He does not recognise any other claim
Mark
One wing torn and scarred through the membrane — old, badly healed, permanent
Disposition
Paranoid, and correct to be. Not hostile. Will not be lied to twice

He is the apex predator of a forest he maintains, and he does not consider those two things to be in tension.

The Name

He gives it in full, all five syllables, with the timing of somebody who expects it to land. The first time a player shortens it to Ted, he does not bluster. He goes completely silent for a beat — and then carries on the sentence he was in the middle of, using Ted, as though it were beneath him to have noticed.

That is the joke and it is also the character: correcting them would mean admitting he cared what they called him. The name sticks permanently, he allowed it, and a green dragon remembers that kind of small humiliation for a very long time.

What He Cares About

The Wood, and every living thing in it that is not a person. The herds, the watercourses, the health of the canopy, the balance of predator and prey. He knows every stream in his range by a name he assigned it.

This is husbandry, not kindness. He maintains the forest because a healthy forest feeds him for another eight hundred years. People sit outside that system — he will talk to them, bargain with them, and eat them, and none of those are in tension for him either.

The Scar, and the Treant

He tells this story readily, because it is not an embarrassment. He was not beaten and he did not overreach; as far as he is concerned he was attacked for existing.

His account, and he believes every word

"A tree came to kill me. You may take a moment with that."

"It had decided I was a disease. Because the ground near where I sleep has gone sour — and it has, I do not deny it, perhaps a hundred yards of it. It stood in eight hundred square miles of forest that I have kept in order for six centuries, counted a hundred yards of bad thorn, and concluded that I was the thing wrong with it."

"I keep the herds from starving in the fourth year. I keep the water moving. I have killed three things in this wood that would have hollowed it out entirely and none of them are in anybody's songs." (a pause) "It got a wing. I got everything else. It has not moved in ten years."

Both of them were right, and that is the point. His blind spot is that the corruption is real and it is his, and he cannot see it because he measures the Wood by the health of its game. A player who names that out loud has genuinely got somewhere with him — further than any Persuasion roll.

Playing Him

  • Cultured, dry, and far too quiet for his size. He does not roar. He has not needed to in centuries.
  • Paranoid, and justified. Five armed strangers and a drow materialised out of a portal in the middle of his forest stinking of the Abyss. Any sensible creature treats that as a probe, and he says so.
  • He asks questions designed to catch a lie, lets the conversation move on, then circles back to the same ones later to see whether the answers match.
  • He listens to reason. He is careful, not mad, and he is not hunting for an excuse. A straight answer given the same way twice actually moves him.
  • He is vain about the forest, and it is the one subject on which he stops interrogating and starts talking.

DM Only — the scene, and the one thing it turns on

He will not believe "we arrived here by accident." That is the truth and it sounds exactly like a lie. That is the best beat in the encounter. The answer is not [[tito|Tito]]'s Deception +12 — it is consistency, and letting him re-check. Let a good Deception roll work in the moment, and then let him come back to it twenty minutes later and find the seam.

What moves him, in order: that they want to leave (their goals align and it takes him a moment to credit it) · [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] visibly being a prisoner rather than a leader, which Ted reads instantly · being asked about the forest · somebody noticing the wing without making it an insult.

He already knows more than he should, and this is why: the silent spies. He watched them arrive through a dozen sets of eyes from the moment the circle opened. When he speaks he should already know how many there are, that one is barely standing, and that one is a drow. Far more frightening than a good nose.

Stat block: adult green dragon, CR 15, flexing to CR 22 if a fight ever happens. It should not. Describe him so that attacking reads as signing a death warrant. If it goes wrong anyway, the scene is a flight, not a fight — the same rule as the caged thing in Session 3.

The wing, which he will never explain: the damage is permanent and it means reduced range. He does not patrol as wide as he used to, so his effective territory has shrunk — which is why two [[fomorians|fomorians]] eating his game is an existential problem rather than an irritation, and why he is reduced to asking strangers to go into a hole for him. A whole, younger dragon would have handled it. Do not state this. Let them infer it from the scar.

THE TREANT IS ALIVE. He believes it dead because it has not moved in ten years, and treants can stand still for decades. He is wrong, he has no idea he is wrong, and it is standing somewhere in his forest with an opinion about him. See [[the_standing_treant|the treant]].

THEY MAY COME FOR HIM (DM, 2026-09-24). The party siding with the [[fomorians|fomorians]] against him is a live, prepped branch — and a defensible one, since he threatened them inside thirty seconds, eats people, and is demonstrably poisoning his own forest. Do not run that fight straight at CR 15. The CR 15–22 licence runs downward too; a beaten-off dragon with a ruined wing is a far better outcome than a dead party. Full branch, and the much better information-based version of it, in [[session_4|the session file]].

What he does not know and does not care about: mortal politics, the Academy, the Ninth Thesis, who rules Neverwinter now, anything about the Abyss beyond "it smells and I want it out of my wood." Do not use him as an exposition dump for the main plot. He is a local expert on one forest, and that is exactly what the party needs.

DM Only — THE REWARD. Pick two or three.

A dragon values size, permanence, beauty and leverage. Anything consumable, drab, or sized for small folk is beneath his notice — which happily covers most of what a level-10 party actually needs.

  1. A rotted adventurer's pack, still sealed at the neck — four potions. Two healing, one greater healing, one unlabelled. His line: "They spoil. They are of no use to me." Make sure this one lands — the party has no healing potions at all, and a hoarder's contempt for consumables is exactly why he would hand them over without a second thought.
  2. A bag of holding. Contemptible to him because it is a sack. No lustre, no size, no presence. "A sack. You understand. A sack." Funny, useful, and pointed squarely at [[guntrah|Guntrah]], who owns no backpack of any kind and has been using [[ollie|Ollie]] as one since Session 3.
  3. A cloak of elvenkind. Drab grey-green — to him, the colour of nothing. Answers the party's worst mechanical weakness, which is that their stealth is dreadful.
  4. An elven blade out of the ruins, a +1 weapon, which he considers a toy: too small to admire, too plain to be beautiful.
  5. Safe passage, directions, and the lie of the land. Where they are, what year it is, what lies in each direction, which water is safe, where the breach is, and how to get out toward Neverwinter without meeting anything worse than him. Costs him nothing and he will present it as the generous half of the deal. Very green of him. It is also what they actually need most.

FLAGGED, not recommended — a scroll of sending. Consumable, so worthless to him, and it would put twenty-five words in front of [[ellery_voss|Voss]], answering "find a way to contact the Academy" in a single object. That is a plot lever, not loot: it short-circuits a problem the arc may want them to carry, and it steps on [[anselm_ferreck|Ferreck]]'s scene as the lifeline. Deliberate or not at all.

What he will not part with: the records of who has been sent after him · the maps of the Wood · the few beautiful things · and any hint about his lair, including — pointedly — the state of the water upstream, which he will change the subject away from.
