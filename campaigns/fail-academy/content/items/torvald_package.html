A plain shipping container, closed, indistinguishable from any other crate in the room except for what was written on the outside of it by hand — and for the fact that it had been sitting unprocessed while everything around it was labelled, logged and shelved.

[[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] found it, searching in the dark by picking things up more or less at random and holding them out to be identified.

What Was Written On It

  • The address: to Torvald Thatch. The groundskeeper. Not to a department, not to [[ellery_voss|Dr. Voss]], not to the Academy.
  • Beneath it, in Common, in a plain unhurried hand: an instruction, also addressed to Torvald, that Dr. Voss is not to see this. By name.
  • Beneath that, several lines in Celestial. Nobody read them. [[guntrah|Guntrah]] identified the script; [[silas|Silas]] — the only Celestial speaker in the party — was across the room and got one word off it while walking over.

What Happened When It Was Opened

Guntrah tore it open. The package disappeared, and so did Silas and Tavian — a rift opened directly behind where they had been standing and pulled them through it. [[tito|Tito]] asked, in the few seconds available, whether they could bring the writing with them in case it carried instructions. It had already gone with the box.

Guntrah, who has studied portals, got a look at what it opened: not a teleportation circle, not a dimension door-class effect of limited range — something carrying considerably more power, and a doorway to another plane. Where, he couldn't say. Only that it left this one, and that it would not stay open long.

DM Only — What It Actually Was

A 7th-level glyph of warding (spell glyph) storing plane shift, keyed to the package being opened. Everyone within 20 feet made a DC 20 Charisma save; failures went through, and the rift stayed open one round so anyone left could follow. Silas and Tavian failed. Guntrah and Tito resisted, then jumped.

Design history worth keeping straight. The locked 2026-08-19 version keyed the glyph to a spoken trigger word inside the Celestial text, so that only someone literate enough to translate it could set it off — which made the trap inert to [[osric_morne|Osric]] and to Torvald, and meant it was aimed at whoever intercepted the package rather than whoever carried it. That was a genuinely elegant clue and the party could have reasoned their way to it. The DM changed it at the table to fire on opening, which is now canon (ruling confirmed 2026-08-23) — but it costs that inference, because anyone can open a box. If you still want the party to be able to conclude something about the sender from the trap's design, it needs another route.

Why it was sitting there. Osric can read the first note. He cannot read the second. He had been holding a package addressed to one colleague carrying written instructions to conceal it from another, with no rule covering that, and it had been eating at him for days. It is the real reason he stayed back from Commencement and a large part of why the argument with Voss went as badly as it did. He is not complicit.

Where it went. Through with the party, to the site that made it — see [[ritual_platform|the ritual platform]]. An empty crate of identical unmarked construction sits near the workbench there, findable by anyone who got a good look at this one. Nobody looked.
