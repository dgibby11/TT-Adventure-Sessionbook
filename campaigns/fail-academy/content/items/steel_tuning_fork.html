No. That is the entire point of it

[[morvek|Morvek]] gave it to Vrenn because Morvek wanted goods going to the Material Plane, and every plane needs its own metal. Vrenn produced it out of his satchel the moment the party said the word Faerûn — and [[guntrah|Guntrah]], who works with tools for a living, recognised what it was on sight.

It is the direction of the spell. Without it a plane shift has nowhere to point. With it, whoever is holding it can aim at the Material Plane — and it is not used up, which makes it the single most valuable object anyone in this party is carrying.

The handoff, [[session_3|Session 3]]

Invisible, standing next to a man who cannot see him and has to take on trust that the voice is still his: "Hey — can I have the tuning fork? The steel one you used to get here."

He hesitates a long time. Then: "Did you figure out how this works?"

"I'm pretty sure I did."

He hands it over. "I think I've resigned to my fate."

[[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] saw it come out of the bag and disappear into thin air, and worked out what had happened. [[tito|Tito]] missed the whole thing.

DM Only — what this is for

This is the ache, and it survived the route change intact. Plane shift is a 7th-level spell. [[silas|Silas]] is a Wizard 11 whose highest slot is 6th as of [[session_4|Session 4]] — one level closer and still two slot levels short. The party is carrying the means to go home and cannot use it. Do not soften that, and do not let anybody quietly hand them a scroll to make it moot. The DM said it out loud at the table this session: "you're getting close to being able to plane shift for yourself… that's a game changer."

RE-POINTED (2026-09-23, confirmed at the table 2026-09-24)

The old framing is retired. This file used to sell the payoff as "a Silas who eventually casts plane shift himself gets to choose his destination, which is exactly the thing neither Vrenn's scroll nor the basement circle could do." That distinction is dead: the circle could be aimed all along, and Vrenn aimed it — the caster names the destination, which is the whole reason the party is in this forest.

The ache survives on the plain RAW reading, which is better anyway: the fork gets you to the Material Plane, one fork per plane, and the reason Silas cannot use it is simply that the spell is 7th level and he is not. Nothing was ever said out loud that this contradicts. What the DM did say at the table is the correct version: "you have to have the proper tuning fork for the plane that you want to go to, and they are kind of hard to come by."

AND NOW IT IS THE BRAKE ON THE LICH (played 2026-09-24)

This object stopped being only a promise and became the thing keeping the party alive. On arrival [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] reasoned out why [[morvek|Morvek]] cannot follow immediately, and the first item was this:

As played

"I don't think he has a tuning fork on him. I don't think there's one left in the basement. But I also know he's a very, very resourceful lich. He's probably gonna get one very soon."

Morvek wanted goods going to the Material Plane, kept one steel fork for it, and gave it to the drow who then gave it to the party. He cannot scry across planes, so he must arrive before he can locate anybody — and he cannot arrive without a fork. The party's casual acquisition in a basement is what turned the clock from hours into two-to-four days.

NOBODY AT THE TABLE HAS JOINED THIS UP. Vrenn said it; no player repeated it; nobody looked at Silas's pack. The moment somebody does is a scene — and the corollary arrives with it: hand the fork back and Morvek comes tomorrow. Keep it and they are the reason he is late. The fork is now simultaneously the way home, the reason they are being hunted slowly, and a thing a lich will eventually want returned.

Scope note (DM, 2026-09-02): the thin place requirement is about how deep [[ritual_platform|Thanatos]] is, not a universal law of planar travel. It should not quietly become a second condition on this payoff.

Worth roughly 250 gp as a component and enormously more than that as capital. It carries most of the operation's value on the Abyssal end, and it is the reason Morvek visited that platform in person. He will notice it is gone.

The cost to Vrenn, which nobody has counted yet: he handed over the one object standing between him and his owner's temper, to somebody he could not see, while pretending to run an errand. He said "I think I've resigned to my fate" and then went and fought. That line should be worth something later.

What it is not: an escape hatch, a scroll, or a way to summon anything. It is a component. Anyone with it still needs the spell, and — on the far side, if they ever go back — a thin place to cast it in.
