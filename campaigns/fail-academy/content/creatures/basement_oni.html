What they looked like
Two humans in tattered clothes, hunched over workbenches, working
What they were
Oni. Both of them
Where
The basement of [[the_house|the house]] — [[session_3|Session 3]]
Status
Both dead. One to a pair of Divine Smites, one to a 4th-level blight

They were sitting in exactly the pose the party first found [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] in: bent over a bench in rags, working, in a place where everybody is somebody's property. That is the whole trick, and it very nearly worked — the party's working assumption for most of the scene was that these were two more prisoners.

What Gave Them Away

  • The first one's eyes tracked [[guntrah|Guntrah]] while he was invisible. Guntrah sidestepped out of the stairway doorway, rolled Perception 15, and caught it. He told nobody, readied an action, and sat on it for the whole of the next scene. They can see through invisibility.
  • They laughed at the grate. When the snarl came out of the back wall, both of them chuckled — and it did not sound like a person laughing. That was the moment the room turned.
  • They knew exactly what the party was doing. Nobody standing near the circle looking casual fooled either of them for a second.
The turn

"I think you should probably leave now."

"Why?"

"You're not supposed to be here."

"But we're helping Vrenn."

"No, you're not."

What the Party Learned Fighting Them

  • They change shape. Guntrah put two bolts and an Arcane Jolt into one for 35 damage and it dropped the disguise mid-hit.
  • They fight with what is to hand. Both picked up glaives off their own workbenches. One kept using it. The other fought with claws that do slashing and a second, colder kind of damage on top.
  • They fly, and used it to cross the room over the party's heads rather than run round them.
  • They vanish at will. One turned invisible as a bonus action, twice, and hunted [[silas|Silas]] and then [[tito|Tito]] with it.
  • Cold underperforms. Tito's chromatic orb came in noticeably light. Necrotic did not — the killing blow was thirty points of it.
  • They are intelligent and they had a job. Both went straight for whoever was filling the bowls and ignored everything else. They were not defending themselves; they were denying an objective.
  • They crit. Three times, in one fight, against a party that had not rested since Commencement.

DM Only — the build, and where it departs from the block

Two oni, CR 7 each — 3,900 XP apiece, 11,700 adjusted for a pair, against four level-10 PCs at Hard 7,600 / Deadly 11,200. Just past Deadly by design (DM, 2026-09-01, replacing the quarantined glabrezu). It played exactly on that number: no deaths, one PC at 0 with two successful death saves, and the party won because [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] and [[tito|Tito]] each had one burst turn left.

Two departures from the printed stat block, both now witnessed by the party and therefore canon:

  • Truesight. RAW oni have darkvision 60 ft. These were run with truesight, and the DM said so out loud — "They can see literally everything." That is what made the party's heavy investment in invisibility worthless in the basement, and it is why one of them tracked Guntrah in the first place. Keep it if these recur; the party will plan around it now.
  • Necrotic on the claws. The clawing one dealt slashing plus necrotic on every hit (12+10, 11+8, 6+10). Not on the printed block. Suits a lich's hired help and reads well — keep it.

Regeneration was never used or mentioned, and neither was darkness, cone of cold, or the change-shape-to-Large option. The fight was short and lethal enough without them. If oni recur, the printed Regeneration 10 (stopped by acid or fire) is worth adding back — the party has both in quantity and has already shown, against the armanites, that it hunts for exactly that kind of counter.

The ambush trigger, and why the played version was better

The design said the party's decency is the ambush trigger — that they would try to free two more captives. What actually happened was better: Guntrah caught one of them watching him, said nothing to his own party, and spent a full scene deciding in character whether shooting first would be murder. Then he shot first. The trigger was one player's suspicion and one player's willingness to be wrong about it, and it cost the DM nothing to run.

Unspent

Nobody ever asked them about [[torvald_thatch|Thatch]]. They were designed as the second independent source at the far end of the chain who has never heard of him — the available deduction being he is a delivery address, not a partner. The conversation went from "take what you need" to crossbow bolts too fast. That job is now unfilled and needs rehoming.

Also never established: who hired them. The DM narrated that stopping the party was "what they were hired to do", which implies [[morvek|Morvek]] placed them and implies they were paid rather than bound — a meaningful distinction in a house full of slaves and skeletons, and one nobody has followed up.
