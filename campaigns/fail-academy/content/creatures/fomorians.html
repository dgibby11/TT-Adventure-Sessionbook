They came up out of the deep places and did not go back down. Since then they had been taking deer, elk and anything else large enough to be worth the walk — and, crucially, not hunting so much as stripping. Ted made a point of the distinction and expected nobody to understand it.

They are enormous, bent, and asymmetrical, with one eye grown larger and more intelligent than the other. They are not stupid. They are cruel in a patient, planning way, and they had already beaten a dragon once by the simple method of going somewhere he did not fit.

Ted's assessment, and it turned out to be the accurate one: "No. I could eat them if I chose." The whole of their advantage was a hole. The DM's verdict afterwards was the same: "they're big and ugly and not particularly strong."

How They Died

Tracked at the table as "Clubby" (the first, webbed near the entrance) and "Hunch" (the second, who arrived a round later). DM bookkeeping names only; neither was ever said in the fiction, and the party never learned that fomorians are called fomorians until the mini came out of the case.

  • They were never spoken to. The whole talk-to-them branch — Undercommon, Giant, [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] as the key — was never touched. Nobody tried, and no language problem ever arose.
  • Round one: Clubby destroyed a spectral warhorse's worth of scouting and walked straight into [[guntrah|Guntrah]]'s readied web (DC 18), then failed [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]]'s hold monster on a 3 and was paralysed on top of restrained.
  • Hunch arrived, killed the horse outright, and put Evil Eye on [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] — who rolled a natural 20 on the Charisma save and took half. Then [[silas|Silas]]'s phantasmal killer landed on him and ran for the rest of the fight, doing more damage than anything else on the board.
  • Round two was a firing squad. Advantage from the web on every attack, [[tito|Tito]]'s bullseye lantern solving the darkness, Ollie's natural 20 Rend, and Vrenn's natural 20 chromatic orb at 2nd level for 31 and 45 on the bounce.
  • Silas finished Clubby with a Sculpted fireball (burning the web off for an extra 2d4); Tito finished Hunch with Unsettling Words for −9 and then blight for 34 necrotic.
  • They landed exactly one thing, and it landed on the NPC. Hunch's second Evil Eye cursed Vrenn — who failed at 11, with Tavian's +3 aura and Guntrah's Flash of Genius +5 already on the roll.
  • Loot: 80 gp between them. Nothing else.

DM Only — THE FIGHT, AS DESIGNED vs AS IT WENT. Read the post-mortem first.

The encounter maths said just-past-Deadly and the fight was a walkover. Both are true and the gap is the lesson. Priced at ~11,700 XP adjusted — the same number as the two oni in Session 3, which produced a PC at 0 with two death saves. This time nobody took a point of damage. Four reasons, and they are all reproducible, so plan around them:

  1. The party prepared, for the first time in four sessions. Mass invisibility at 7th level, a disposable glowing scout, a readied control spell, a pre-lit hooded lantern. Reward that — but price the next fight against a party that does it.
  2. Web on round one ended it. DC 18 on a Huge giant with a bad DEX, and everything afterwards had advantage. Control landing first is worth more than any amount of damage against two big bodies with no ranged options.
  3. The played cavern has 40–50 ft ceilings (see [[underdark_breach|the Breach]]), so Guntrah simply flew to the roof and was untouchable all fight. The prepped low antechamber would have prevented that entirely.
  4. Two Huge melee creatures with 15 ft reach and no answer to flight, fear or restraint is not a threat to a level-11 party at range, whatever the XP says. Evil Eye is the whole block and it is one save.

What the design got right: Evil Eye was the dangerous thing, it did come down to a Charisma save, and Tavian's Aura of Protection was the counter. It landed twice: Tavian made it on a natural 20, and Vrenn failed it at 11 even with the aura and Flash of Genius stacked on, which is the lesson arriving anyway — just on the NPC.

[[tito|Tito]]'s Magic Resistance was never tested. The 2026-09-24 ruling that it applies to Evil Eye stands and is unspent.

Reference — the original prep, for any future fomorians

Two fomorians, CR 8 each. 3,900 XP apiece, 7,800 doubled to ~11,700 adjusted for a pair — four level-10 PCs sit at Hard 7,600 and Deadly 11,200, so this is just past Deadly: the same number as the two oni in Session 3. That fight produced one PC at 0 with two death saves. Price this one the same way.

Do not let them take it unrested. If the party skip the long rest and go straight at the cave, drop one fomorian or give the second a reason to be elsewhere.

Shape of the block (pull the real one before the table): Huge giant, AC 14, around 150 HP each, darkvision 120 ft, Multiattack of two greatclub swings — or one club and an Evil Eye.

EVIL EYE is the thing to plan around. A magical curse on one creature it can see within 60 ft, Charisma save or take psychic damage and suffer a lingering curse. Against this party that is pointed:

  • [[silas|Silas]] has CHA 8 and 72 HP at AC 11. He is the obvious target and he will fail.
  • [[guntrah|Guntrah]] has CHA 12, though a Cloak of Protection helps.
  • [[tito|Tito]] is nearly immune — CHA 18 plus satyr Magic Resistance means advantage on every save against a spell. RULED (2026-09-24): Magic Resistance DOES apply, even though a curse is not strictly a spell. He has earned it and it is a nice moment for the player.
  • [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]]'s Aura of Protection is the counter: +3 to all saves for anybody within 10 feet of him. The tactical answer to Evil Eye is for the casters to stand next to the paladin, which is a lesson worth letting them learn in real time.

Terrain is the encounter. They fight in a cave they fit and the party does not — see [[underdark_breach|the Breach]]. Low ceilings in the antechamber mean the fomorians stoop and their reach matters less; the crack at the back means they can disengage downward exactly as they did on the dragon, and if they run, the party has to decide how far into the Underdark they are willing to follow. That decision is the real climax, not the hit points.

THEY CAN BE TALKED TO. INT 9 is not stupid — they can bargain, and the DM is prepared for the party to recruit them against [[tedrovaxilliath|Ted]] rather than kill them. They speak Undercommon and Giant, and no PC speaks either; the keys are [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]] (drow, Undercommon), Tito's Universal Speech, or Silas's free daily comprehend languages. They will agree to nearly anything and betray the party at the first profitable moment — the hoard is right there. Full branch in [[session_4|the session file]].

TIMELINE — do not let the cave speak to the Wood's past. The [[tedrovaxilliath|dragon]]-[[the_standing_treant|treant]] fight was about ten years ago; these two arrived in the spring. They were not here and they know nothing about it. Anything found in this cave has to point down, not backwards.

The better hook, and it has no timeline problem: why did they come up NOW? They are recent arrivals from a long way below, and nothing has been decided about what pushed them or sent them. Evidence about the deep country — something they carried up, something they are avoiding, something following them — costs nothing, contradicts nothing, and pays off the crack at the back of [[underdark_breach|the Breach]] rather than the forest's history. It also sits next to [[the_captive_drow|Vrenn]], who came up out of that country himself. Optional; entirely undecided.
