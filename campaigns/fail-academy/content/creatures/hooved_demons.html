The party has no name for these. Two of them crossed a stone bridge onto the ritual platform within seconds of the party's arrival there, and were on them before anyone could find cover.

What the Party Learned the Hard Way

  • They are built out of weapons. Dense hooves, claws ending in curling talons, and a long tail — ten feet of it — whose serrated ridges can flense the flesh off a victim. They attack with all three in the same breath: gore, claws, tail.
  • They charge. A straight run at a target hits considerably harder than a standing attack.
  • At least some of them throw lightning. That crackling arcane hum [[tito|Tito]] picked out of the hoofbeats before they were visible was not decoration — one of the two loosed a bolt at [[guntrah|Guntrah]] sixty feet in the air for 34 damage. It did not do so twice, which suggests it has to recharge.
  • Cold barely troubles them. Tito's first chromatic orb hit for 43 cold and visibly underperformed; acid worked far better.
  • They are not clever. When [[silas|Silas]] dropped a wall of stone in front of the lead one, it ran into the wall and started hitting it, and kept hitting it. That single decision effectively ended the fight.
  • Two is not a normal number. They travel in formations and herds; a pair is a scouting detail, not a war party. Whoever said so was not exaggerating — the drow at the workbench put the follow-up at fifteen.

What Session 3 Added

  • They speak. A lone one spotted the party crossing the plain at seventy or eighty feet, shouted something none of them understood, and ran the other way to fetch the rest. It was speaking Abyssal, which [[guntrah|Guntrah]] reads and speaks — and he was not close enough to catch it. He will be listening next time.
  • They hunt properly. Six or seven came back within minutes, quartered the ground, looked for tracks and prints, and shouted instructions at each other. One passed within twenty feet of the party. Stupid, but with good instincts was the assessment, and it held.
  • THEY TURN ON EACH OTHER. When the search failed, they started blaming one another, and then clawing and stamping at one another, and six of the seven died in the ash without a single party member lifting a finger. [[guntrah|Guntrah]] shot the bloodied survivor twice for 35 and finished it.
  • Nothing to loot, confirmed. They carry nothing. They are the weapons.
  • There are hundreds of thousands of them on this plane. Vrenn's number, given flatly.

DM Only — Armanites

These are armanites, CR 7 each. The name has never been spoken at the table — the DM was asked directly and deliberately declined ("I don't know if I want to tell you that"), so the party has no word for them at all beyond [[tito|Tito]]'s improvised "blight" mid-fight. Keep it that way unless someone with planar knowledge identifies them later; the anonymity is doing real work.

AC 17, ~114 HP. Resistant to cold, fire and lightning; immune to poison damage and the poisoned condition; magic resistance. Multiattack: gore plus two claws. Charge deals extra damage after moving at least 20 ft straight at the target. Lightning Lance is a recharge ability.

Printing: Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes (DM ruling 2026-08-24 — "whichever matches the transcript; if both match equally, MToF"). The read-aloud text at the table — the dense hooves, the claws ending in curling talons, the serrated tail ridges that flense flesh, and the note that they are rarely seen in packs as small as two — is carried in both printings essentially unchanged, so there is no way to tell them apart from the recording. The tie-break therefore applies and MToF is canon for this campaign. Note this is a deliberate exception to the standing "newest official sourcebook wins" rule, which would have pointed at MPMM.

Practical warning about the link: D&D Beyond marks the MToF armanite as legacy content — Volo's and MToF were pulled from its marketplace on 17 May 2022, so the live site serves the Monsters of the Multiverse version and the legacy entry sits behind a notice. If the physical MToF book isn't to hand at the table, the MPMM stat block is the practical fallback; the differences are minor and none of them changed how this fight played.

How the fight actually went, and the lesson in it: the encounter was designed as "genuinely hard" for four level-10 PCs and played close to trivial, because a 60-foot wall of stone is a hard counter to melee-only enemies with a single ranged option. Plan around Silas's wall of stone, don't walk into it. The fifteen inbound are not a scaled-up version of the same fight — if they arrive, the answer is a chase, not a stand.

They are guards for a fabrication site on Thanatos belonging to [[morvek|Morvek]]. They respond to intrusion; they do not negotiate, and they were not sent for the party specifically.

The infighting was a DM improvisation in Session 3 and it is worth keeping as canon. It pays off the "they are not clever" characterisation established in Session 2, it handed the party a free kill they had genuinely earned by hiding well, and it makes the plain feel like a place where things go wrong on their own rather than a monster dispenser. Failed hunt → recrimination → slaughter is now how these behave.

The fifteen never arrived. Vrenn's estimate was always a hedge (DM, 2026-08-26) rather than a countdown, and the party was off the outcrop before it could be tested. Seven is the number that actually turned up, on the road rather than at the site.
