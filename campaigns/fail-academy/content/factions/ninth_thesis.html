Origins

Every graduate of the Academy's first class — nine students in all — was required to write a thesis on what they intended to become. Eight wrote the expected declarations of glory: dragons to fight, kingdoms to serve. The ninth, a quiet, watchful student named Wren Halloway, wrote something else entirely — not what she intended to become, but what she'd learned. Four years of noticing things: conversations that stopped when she entered a room, professors who locked their doors twice, classmates who weren't quite who their letters of introduction said they were. Her thesis broke from the expected form so completely that it was quietly pulled from the official record rather than filed alongside the other eight.

Nerissa Voss noticed it go. As the Academy's first archivist — younger sister to founder [[aldric_voss|Aldric Voss]], but never one of the three credited founders herself — she was the one person positioned to see a true document quietly disappear from the record and do something about it. She kept it rather than let it vanish, and built a private organization around it, alongside the Academy's official wards, not instead of them. Not a conspiracy, and not her brother's doing: Nerissa acted on her own initiative, in her own quiet corner of the institution, and didn't alter or embellish a word of what Halloway wrote — she just refused to let it be lost. The organization has been led by her own line of descendants ever since, down to [[ellery_voss|Dr. Ellery Voss]] today.

What It Does

The Ninth Thesis is small, and deliberately so — a loose network of roughly a dozen active operatives, mostly Academy alumni, scattered across the region. No application process, no roster anyone outside the organization has ever seen. Voss selects people personally and runs it personally; she is the only point of contact for any operative, and most don't know who else is in it. Their mandate is broad by design: investigate credible leads on hidden evil, wherever the lead goes, authorized case by case rather than under standing orders.

Presently, the organization's whole attention is on the pattern of planted-item incidents at the Academy this year — see [[session_1|Session 1]].

"The Secret Society"

Students who've heard whispers of a "Secret Society" on campus have heard something real and something unrelated, tangled together into one story. See [[secret_society|The Secret Society]] for the full shape of that confusion — in short, the name properly belongs to nothing. It's campus rumor-bleed between [[brennan_ashcroft|Brennan Ashcroft]]'s entirely mundane Alumni Society and half-true whispers about this organization, which actually is secret. The clandestine gathering the prior party stumbled into at the [[founders_statue|Founder's Statue]] during [[one_shot_2|One Shot 2]] — what campus rumor filed under "the Secret Society" at the time — was, in fact, genuine Ninth Thesis activity.

DM Only

Tone: Write this as prudence, not manipulation. Nerissa preserved a true document and built quiet contingency around it — she didn't fabricate, coerce, or conspire, and neither does Ellery today. Consistent with the founders rewrite: sincerely well-intentioned people, not a secret cabal. Keeping Nerissa distinct from the three officially-credited founders also keeps their public legacy uncomplicated by any of this — the Ninth Thesis was always a quieter, more personal initiative, not founding-era institutional business.

Resources for operatives: Modest but real funding for a specific lead (travel, lodging, replacement gear), with an expected accounting — not out of distrust, just how Voss runs everything. Information access is whatever the organization already knows that's relevant to a given case. Off-campus, she can vouch for operatives or open doors with contacts case-by-case; on-campus, current students need no cover at all, which is the entire point of recruiting them.

Secrecy expectations: Discretion requested, not demanded — no blood oath, no threat if someone talks. She'd rather operatives not advertise it, for the same reason outside operatives can't work openly on campus: it tips off whoever's responsible.

Sample existing operatives (texture, not yet named on-screen): a retired battle-cleric working the eastern borderlands investigating cult activity; a pair of Academy alumni working the trade routes as caravan guards, cover for tracking rumors and contraband; one alumnus embedded in a distant city's magistrate's office, useful for records and legal cover.
