What Students Hear

Before Session 1, the Ninth Thesis was campus gossip and not much more — the sort of thing a fifth-year has heard referenced without ever having been near it. Real, exclusive, operating somewhere in the background of the institution, and impossible to confirm. Students who paid attention knew it was probably genuine. Nobody outside it knew what it actually did.

What Voss Told the Party

The Academy's first graduating class numbered nine students, and all nine wrote a thesis. Their original theses are on display in a case in [[old_library|the Old Library]] to this day — where there are only eight. Eight were what you'd expect from graduating students: declarations of what they intended to go on and become.

The ninth was written by a woman who shone a light on something more common than most people realize — that everybody has secrets. The line Voss keeps returning to in her own notes is hers: "a secret, once it is understood for what it is, is a kind of power." Voss believes one such secret concerned the founding of the Academy itself. She did not say whose thesis it was, what happened to it, or what the secret was.

She is, by her own admission, descended from one of the Academy's founders — loosely, in her words. There has been a Voss at this Academy in one capacity or another for hundreds of years, and some of them have led the Ninth Thesis. She did not say which founder.

Working For It

The terms Voss laid out, in full:

  • The work: gather information; occasionally act on it, though that's uncommon. She handles the campus herself. Operatives work the wider world.
  • The pay: around 50 gold a week, negotiable, paid weekly — in coin, not Academy credits — with pay in arrears if an operative is somewhere payment can't reach. She considers it generous because the work carries real danger, and graduates are not shielded from it the way students are.
  • Supplies: provided as needed for specific assignments, in addition to wages.
  • The contract: a small written one, drafted and sent afterward. The organization is, in her words, very much by the book, even though it isn't talked about in the open.
  • Discretion: expected, not sworn. No oath, no ceremony, no handshake, and — despite a certain bard's enthusiasm — no hand signal.

She also admitted she is bad at recruitment pitches and that they aren't normally her job; someone else usually handles them. She wanted this one kept further off the books than usual.

DM Only — Origins

Every graduate of the Academy's first class — nine students in all — was required to write a thesis on what they intended to become. Eight wrote the expected declarations of glory. The ninth, a quiet, watchful student named Wren Halloway, wrote something else entirely — not what she intended to become, but what she'd learned. Four years of noticing things: conversations that stopped when she entered a room, professors who locked their doors twice, classmates who weren't quite who their letters of introduction said they were. Her thesis broke from the expected form so completely that it was quietly pulled from the official record rather than filed alongside the other eight.

[[nerissa_voss|Nerissa Voss]] noticed it go. As the Academy's first archivist — younger sister to founder [[aldric_voss|Aldric Voss]], but never one of the three credited founders herself — she was the one person positioned to see a true document quietly disappear from the record and do something about it. She kept it rather than let it vanish, and built a private organization around it, alongside the Academy's official wards, not instead of them. Not a conspiracy, and not her brother's doing: Nerissa acted on her own initiative, in her own quiet corner of the institution, and didn't alter or embellish a word of what Halloway wrote — she just refused to let it be lost. The organization has been led by her own line of descendants ever since, down to [[ellery_voss|Dr. Ellery Voss]] today.

Tone: Write this as prudence, not manipulation. Nerissa preserved a true document and built quiet contingency around it — she didn't fabricate, coerce, or conspire, and neither does Ellery today. Consistent with the founders rewrite: sincerely well-intentioned people, not a secret cabal. Keeping Nerissa distinct from the three officially-credited founders also keeps their public legacy uncomplicated by any of this.

DM Only — Structure & Operations

The Ninth Thesis is small, and deliberately so — a loose network of roughly a dozen active operatives, mostly Academy alumni, scattered across the region. No application process, no roster anyone outside the organization has ever seen. Voss selects people personally and runs it personally; she is the only point of contact for any operative, and most don't know who else is in it. Their mandate is broad by design: investigate credible leads on hidden evil, wherever the lead goes, authorized case by case rather than under standing orders.

Named operative cities (per the Session 1 transcript): Neverwinter, Baldur's Gate, and Silverymoon were the three Voss actually named at the table, with "several around" implied beyond them. Everlund operatives also exist per session_1.html's off-screen debrief — simply never named aloud to the party, so there's no conflict.

Resources for operatives: Modest but real funding for a specific lead (travel, lodging, replacement gear), with an expected accounting — not out of distrust, just how Voss runs everything. Information access is whatever the organization already knows that's relevant to a given case. Off-campus, she can vouch for operatives or open doors with contacts case-by-case; on-campus, current students need no cover at all, which is the entire point of recruiting them.

Secrecy expectations: Discretion requested, not demanded — no blood oath, no threat if someone talks. She'd rather operatives not advertise it, for the same reason outside operatives can't work openly on campus: it tips off whoever's responsible.

Sample existing operatives (texture, not yet named on-screen): a retired battle-cleric working the eastern borderlands investigating cult activity; a pair of Academy alumni working the trade routes as caravan guards, cover for tracking rumors and contraband; one alumnus embedded in a distant city's magistrate's office, useful for records and legal cover. See session_plan.json for whether one of these becomes the party's Neverwinter contact.

DM Only — "The Secret Society"

Students who've heard whispers of a "Secret Society" on campus have heard something real and something unrelated, tangled together into one story. See [[secret_society|The Secret Society]] for the full shape of that confusion — in short, the name properly belongs to nothing. It's campus rumor-bleed between [[brennan_ashcroft|Brennan Ashcroft]]'s entirely mundane Alumni Society and half-true whispers about this organization, which actually is secret. The clandestine gathering the prior party stumbled into at the [[founders_statue|Founder's Statue]] during [[one_shot_2|One Shot 2]] — what campus rumor filed under "the Secret Society" at the time — was, in fact, genuine Ninth Thesis activity.

Gated because none of this is current-party knowledge: it's prior-party material plus authorial explanation. The new party has never encountered the Secret Society rumor on screen.
