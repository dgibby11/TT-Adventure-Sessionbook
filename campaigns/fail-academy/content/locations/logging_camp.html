[[logging_crew|The crew]]

The trees simply stop. Stumps, open sky, and a burn pile the size of a cottage, kept going day and night to clear brush — the smoke [[guntrah|Guntrah]] saw from above the canopy. Two rows of canvas tents, an ox line, and a big open-sided cook tent with plank tables under it. Down on the bank, fir logs are roped onto barges for the river.

It is a workplace, not a village: no inn, no shop, no temple, no families. Beyond the last tents stand the roofless walls of an old town, with young trees growing up through its floors.

CANON (DM, 2026-10-05)

  • About thirty people, with tents, tools and carts. Suspicious of strangers but decent, and somewhat helpful. No secrets — a pass-through.
  • It used to stand in the wood proper. Many years ago [[tedrovaxilliath|the dragon]] killed a dozen good men, and the camp withdrew.
  • It now stands just beside the ruins of Thundertree, the town destroyed when Mount Hotenow erupted several decades ago.
  • The ruins are quiet. A young green dragon and zombies held them about twenty years ago, and earlier adventurers killed them.
  • They cannot afford adventurers. Neverwinter's would cost more than they can pay to deal with the dragon. They joke that once the party is stronger, perhaps they will come back and do it.
  • They are here for the firs — a particular fir that grows no nearer the city, prized for its suppleness and so for shipbuilding.
  • The logs go by barge, bound and escorted down the Neverwinter River to the city. The carts carry the camp — tents, tools and the like — not the timber.
  • It has no name on any map, and none the party would know.

The name (proposal)

The crew call it Kettle Bend. This entity's id is logging_camp and its display name is "The Logging Camp", so the name can change without breaking a link.

The ground around it (proposal)

  • The wood proper — east. They cut its edge and go no further in.
  • The old camp — in the wood proper, abandoned since the dozen died. On [[neverwinter_wood_map|the regional map]] the "Logger's Camp" marker sits there; reading that marker as the old site is a suggestion, not a ruling.
  • The ruins of Thundertree — beside the camp. The dead town's ground was already cleared and its old road already ran to the city, which is why the camp is here.
  • The river — the barges' road to Neverwinter.
  • [[thundertree_road|The old road]] — west, to Neverwinter.

How the camp runs (proposal)

  • Up before dawn, asleep at dark.
  • Nobody talks at meals. You eat, you leave. The talk happens at the fire afterwards.
  • No drink except on the rest day.
  • Hardly any coin in camp. The crew are paid at season's end with a company note cashed in Neverwinter. Deliberate: it shows the party what a credit note is before they find [[tac_credit_note|one]] on the road.
  • They call the dragon "the Green", and have no idea he has a name.

What the party can get (proposal)

  • Supper and the spare tent: about 5 sp a head, paid up front, gone by first light.
  • Supplies from the tool wagon: rations, rope, waterskins, blankets, lamp oil, a hatchet. Plain goods at a fair price.
  • No potions, no magic, no mounts.
  • Directions, freely, once the camp boss has decided they are not trouble.

What they can answer (proposal)

Where are we?
"Kettle Bend. That's old Thundertree behind you, and that's Neverwinter Wood you just walked out of."
Nearest town?
Neverwinter, west by the old road or the river. About a day and a half on foot from Thundertree by the map.
The Green?
"He killed twelve of ours, years back. We keep to the edge now."
The ruins?
Thundertree. "The mountain did that." Quiet for twenty years, since adventurers killed the young dragon and the dead things in them.
The smoking mountain?
Mount Hotenow. "Always smokes. It's what did for Thundertree."
Why work here?
The firs.
A barge to the city?
"Next one's days off. You'd walk it quicker."
News from the city?
As old as the last barge crew's return.
Other strangers lately?
None.
Anything else
The Academy, liches, drow business, giants in a cave: "Can't say I'm familiar."
