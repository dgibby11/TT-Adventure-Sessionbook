Enormous closed canopy, old growth, letting down a grey-green half-light that gives no clue about the hour. Wet, cold, and alive — which after a day and a night on a sulphur plain is almost overwhelming on its own. Game trails thread it at two heights: narrow ones pushed through by small regular traffic, and much wider ones where something heavy has come through often enough that the saplings gave up.

There is running water everywhere, and the party can hear it from the moment they arrive.

The Three Omens

Everything within six miles of the dragon's lair is subtly and accumulatively wrong. These are the Wood telling you whose it is.

The Choking Fog

A fog clings to the forest floor — ankle-deep in the open, knee-deep under the trees, with a faint green cast you keep telling yourself is the light through the canopy. It is not the light.

It smells of three things that should not be together: the sharp chemical bite of chlorine; the sweet heavy rot of something large decaying out of sight; and pine, far too much pine, thick and sugary, like a scent laid deliberately over a smell.

The Twisted Trails

Thorny thickets grow fast here and they do not stay where you left them. A trail narrows behind you. Long whipping canes lie across a path in a way that looks less like growth and more like a hand closing — and the other way is open, and inviting, and leads somewhere you had not intended to go.

You are not being stopped. You are being steered.

The Silent Spies

The squirrel on the branch has not moved in a while. It is not foraging, not grooming, and it did not break for cover when five people walked underneath it. It is looking at you. So is the thrush above it, and two crows further back on the same limb, silent. There is a snake laid along a root by your boot, perfectly still, head up, watching.

Nothing in this forest is making any noise at all.

DM Only — what the omens are doing

  • They replace a map. The party cannot navigate and have no idea where they are, so the forest itself has to carry the information. Every omen says something owns this before anything says what.
  • The spies are how [[tedrovaxilliath|Ted]] already knows. He watched them arrive through a dozen sets of eyes from the moment the circle opened. When he speaks he should already know their number, that one of them is barely standing, and that one is a drow. Much more frightening than a good nose — and it is the omen paying off rather than a coincidence.
  • The trails are your geography control. The thickets decide how easy it is to go anywhere, which keeps [[session_4|Session 4]] in and around the clearing without ever saying "you can't."

The water gradient is a real, findable clue. The creek by the arrival clearing is already slightly wrong — a green cast, a taste, fewer things living in it than there should be. Walk it upstream and it degrades badly: silt, then colour, then nothing alive in it at all, with the growth thickening and twisting alongside. The worst ground is directly over [[teds_lair|the sinkhole]]. Any druid, ranger or attentive traveller can read that gradient. Do not point at it.
