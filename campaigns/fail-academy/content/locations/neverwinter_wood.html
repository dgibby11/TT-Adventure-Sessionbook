What
Ancient temperate old-growth forest on the Sword Coast North — and the territory of one adult green dragon who does not acknowledge any other claim
Arrived
[[session_4|Session 4]], in a clearing near the centre, out of a planar circle. Late autumn, two hours before sunset
The party's knowledge
They named it themselves, in the first twenty minutes. They still do not know the year, and they know almost nothing else
Held by
[[tedrovaxilliath|Tedrovaxilliath]], and they have met him and struck a deal
Leaving
South-west, toward a settlement whose fire they saw from above the canopy

Enormous closed canopy, old growth — sixty to seventy feet up and shut over completely, letting down a filtered half-light that gives no clue about the hour or even which way the sun is. Mostly deciduous with a little fir mixed in. Wet, cold, and alive, which after a day and a night on a sulphur plain is almost overwhelming on its own. Heavy undergrowth away from the clearings, threaded with small game trails a person cannot use.

There is running water everywhere, and the party could hear it from the moment they arrived. The unusual number of rivers is the forest's identifying feature, and it is how the party worked out where they were.

What the Party Established, and How

Nobody told them. They found out. [[guntrah|Guntrah]] used his Winged Boots and forced his way up through the canopy — thick enough that he had to climb it as much as fly it — and got a couple of feet clear of the top.

The view from a hundred feet, on an 18 at disadvantage

Small mountains at the limit of vision, perhaps fifteen miles off — four or five in a row, and one of them looks volcanic. No sign of the great range; far too far.

A great many rivers, breaking the canopy in several places. Striking enough to be worth reporting.

And low, toward the setting sun, a faint smudge of smoke — or possibly just cloud. Impossible to tell whether a settlement or a caravan's fire.

Bearings as settled: mountains north-west, the fire west-south-west. Ruled at the table: from a hundred feet up you see about twelve miles.

Then [[silas|Silas]], on a History check of 16: fir scattered through means this is not the south; only a handful of forests have this many rivers; the one that is neither northern nor southern with that mixture of trees is probably the Neverwinter Wood. He told the others he thought that was where they were.

[[tedrovaxilliath|Ted]] never confirmed or denied it. Nobody asked him what the forest was called, what year it is, or what lies in three of the four directions — and he would have answered all of it for free.

The Three Omens — and how much of them actually landed

Everything within six miles of the dragon's lair is subtly and accumulatively wrong. These are the Wood telling you whose it is. About a third of this got played in [[session_4|Session 4]]; see the DM note below for which.

The Choking Fog

A fog clings to the forest floor — ankle-deep in the open, knee-deep under the trees, with a faint green cast you keep telling yourself is the light through the canopy. It is not the light.

It smells of three things that should not be together: the sharp chemical bite of chlorine; the sweet heavy rot of something large decaying out of sight; and pine, far too much pine, thick and sugary, like a scent laid deliberately over a smell.

The Twisted Trails

Thorny thickets grow fast here and they do not stay where you left them. A trail narrows behind you. Long whipping canes lie across a path in a way that looks less like growth and more like a hand closing — and the other way is open, and inviting, and leads somewhere you had not intended to go.

You are not being stopped. You are being steered.

The Silent Spies

The squirrel on the branch has not moved in a while. It is not foraging, not grooming, and it did not break for cover when five people walked underneath it. It is looking at you. So is the thrush above it, and two crows further back on the same limb, silent. There is a snake laid along a root by your boot, perfectly still, head up, watching.

Nothing in this forest is making any noise at all.

DM Only — WHICH OMENS RAN (played 2026-09-24), and what the fog became

THE FOG LANDED, in a plainer form than written. Not the chlorine/rot/too-much-pine construction. What the table got was: it does not smell like a healthy forest — not quite rotten, a little bit funky, and then, low in the taller grass, a very faint fog with a greenish tinge to it, everywhere, not coming from any direction, and it had been there the whole time. The players supplied "mildew" themselves. Play the plainer version from now on; it worked and it is easier to say. It was also established explicitly that the mist permeates the whole territory, not just the clearing.

THE TRAILS LANDED LATE AND BETTER. Not as thickets closing on the party, but as an explicit corruption gradient described on the six-mile walk north: nothing grows straight, vines everywhere, thorns, plants that look poisonous — "more of a corrupted forest than a true forest" — and it visibly weakens the further you get from his lair. [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]]'s Feywild origin was the lens, and he was the one who felt it. The party have now been handed the whole mechanism for finding [[teds_lair|the sinkhole]] without being told what it leads to.

THE SILENT SPIES DID NOT HAPPEN. The set-piece above was never read. Instead [[tedrovaxilliath|Ted]] told them flatly, "I have ears and eyes all through the forest," and then [[teds_fox|a single fox]] did the entire job in person — guide, escort, and a witness that followed them into a cave and watched a fight from cover. The fox is much better than the tableau and should be the standing mechanism. Keep the staring-animals version on the shelf for somewhere the fox cannot be.

  • They replace a map — and this half failed usefully. The party did not wait to be told: they flew above the canopy and deduced the forest's name off the rivers inside twenty minutes. The omens still did the "something owns this" job; they simply never had to do the navigation job.
  • The trails are your geography control, and they were never needed — the party stayed where the plot was without being steered at all.

The water gradient is a real, findable clue. The creek by the arrival clearing is already slightly wrong — a green cast, a taste, fewer things living in it than there should be. Walk it upstream and it degrades badly: silt, then colour, then nothing alive in it at all, with the growth thickening and twisting alongside. The worst ground is directly over [[teds_lair|the sinkhole]]. Any druid, ranger or attentive traveller can read that gradient. Do not point at it.

HALF OF IT HAS NOW BEEN SAID OUT LOUD (2026-09-24), and the party do not know what they have. The vegetation half of the gradient was described in full on the walk north, including the key fact that the corruption weakens with distance from the lair — which is the same statement as it is strongest at the lair. The water half was not: they crossed a stream, filled waterskins from it and nobody examined it. They can now find the sinkhole by walking uphill into the worst growth, and it would not occur to them yet. Leave it. It also means that if they ever do go looking, they arrive already knowing he has been lying to himself.

WHERE EVERYTHING IS — see [[neverwinter_wood_map|the regional map]] (DM, 2026-09-24)

  • The party landed at the dot of the "i" in Neverwinter on the map's "Neverwinter Wood" label — near the centre of the forest, south of the river system, north of Falcon's Hunting Lodge and west of the Circle of Thunder.
  • [[underdark_breach|The Breach]] is about SIX miles north, across a stream — Ted's own figure, given at the table, and the same as his stated territorial radius. The crossing was a stream rather than a river, cost nothing, and served as the water resupply. Roughly an hour and a bit each way through difficult terrain; the return leg ran about two hours.
  • Nearest named places: Sharandar north-west, Logger's Camp west-south-west, Falcon's Hunting Lodge south-east, the Circle of Thunder east. The party can still name none of them — but the fire [[guntrah|Guntrah]] spotted was west-south-west at about twelve miles, and Ted confirmed there is a town in that direction, so Logger's Camp is almost certainly where they are walking. The DM has not named it; treat as very likely rather than settled. SETTLED 2026-10-05: it is a logging camp, and the working camp now stands beside the ruins of Thundertree — see the logging-camp block at the foot of this file.
  • The volcanic-looking mountain Guntrah saw, fifteen miles off toward the north-west, reads as Mount Hotenow — one of the four post-Session-2 directions, sighted by the party without anybody naming it. Free, and it is already tied to Ted's hoard.

Scope: six-mile radius, standard green dragon regional effects. Ted stated the six miles himself at the table, as both the distance to the Breach and the extent of his domain. The arrival clearing sits inside it but well out — omens present from the first breath, lair nowhere near adjacent. He also claims control of "the critters and growth" throughout, and demonstrated it twice: a tree thrown into the clearing as firewood, and [[teds_fox|the fox]].

What is actually out here, if the party wander: the [[underdark_breach|Breach]] and its [[fomorians|fomorians]] to the north; [[the_standing_treant|a treant that Ted believes is dead]]; the ruins the Wood has swallowed; and, on the western edge, Thundertree. Beyond the trees: Mount Hotenow northwest, the Triboar Trail south, Agatha's Grove east, and the city of Neverwinter out past the western verge.

DM Only — THE LOGGING CAMP, SETTLED (DM, 2026-10-05). Not yet visited.

The settlement to the south-west is a logging camp — about thirty people under canvas, with tents, tools and carts. Suspicious of strangers but decent, and somewhat helpful. A pass-through, with no secrets in it. It has no name on any map, and none the party would know.

  • It used to stand in the wood proper. Many years ago [[tedrovaxilliath|the dragon]] killed a dozen good men, and the camp withdrew.
  • It now stands just beside the ruins of Thundertree, the town destroyed when Mount Hotenow erupted several decades ago.
  • The ruins are quiet. The Lost Mine material in this repo is set about twenty years before this campaign. The young green dragon and the zombies it puts in Thundertree were slain by earlier adventurers.
  • They cannot afford adventurers. Neverwinter's would cost more than they can pay to come and deal with him. They joke that once the party is stronger, perhaps they will come back and do it.
  • They are here for the firs — a particular fir that grows no nearer the city and is prized for its suppleness, which makes it excellent shipbuilding timber. The same scattered fir is how [[silas|Silas]] placed the forest in [[session_4|Session 4]].
  • The logs go by barge, bound and escorted down the Neverwinter River to the city. The carts carry the camp — tents, tools and the like — not the timber.

OPEN — how far it is. The DM's figure is fifteen miles from the arrival clearing. But on [[neverwinter_wood_map|the regional map]] Thundertree is about twice as far from the clearing as the "Logger's Camp" marker — 5.2 grid squares against 2.7. That marker sits in the wood proper, which is where the camp used to be. Reading it as the old, abandoned site is the assistant's suggestion, not a DM ruling.
