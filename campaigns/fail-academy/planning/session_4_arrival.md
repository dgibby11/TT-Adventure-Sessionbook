## 1. Why they landed in Neverwinter Wood — SUPERSEDES PRIOR CANON

**The destination is not built into the scroll or the circle.** The caster names
it. Vrenn, casting in a hurry with one round on the clock, named **the first
above-ground place on the Material Plane he could think of.**

He could think of Neverwinter Wood because **he has been there once before.** He
surfaced near it during his escape from **Menzoberranzan**, and was tracked down
by drow and returned to the city.

**It has nothing to do with the scroll, nothing to do with Morvek, and nothing to
do with where the artifact shipments go.** More of his backstory is available
later if it is wanted; it is not needed to run this.

## 2. Morvek's Sending — arrives immediately

**On arrival, Vrenn receives a *sending* from Morvek.** Twenty-five words or
fewer, exact text TBD. **Vrenn does not reply, out of fear** — and *sending*
allows a reply, so the silence is a choice the party can be told about.

Vrenn relays the gist to the party: **he has a couple of days at best before
Morvek comes to take him back.** What they do with that is entirely theirs.

> This closes the open "beacon" question. The answer is **fast**, not slow: the
> Token works, Morvek knew the moment Vrenn crossed, and there is a hard clock
> on the table from the first minute of the session.

## 3. The clearing

Near the **centre of the wood**. A clearing ringed by thick vegetation. **Small
and large game trails** lead in and out. A **flowing creek is audible nearby.**

Note the party has just come off a plane where drinkable water was a survival
resource they had to ration by the hour. Running water is worth a beat.

## 4. The green dragon

A **large green dragon** whose territory this is. It comes to investigate the
disturbance — **not aggressive, just checking for threats to its home.**

- It **homes in on the portal's location** and **addresses the party even if it
  cannot see them.** Concealment does not prevent the conversation.
- Its opening position: **they reek of the Abyss**, and a threat to leave.
- **Handled well, it is an information source.** Where they are, when they are,
  and a great deal about Neverwinter Wood. It knows **little of mortal politics
  or struggles** — do not use it as an exposition dump for the Academy plot.
- **Handled very well, it offers work and a reward.**

### The dragon's problem — the side quest

**Two fomorians** have camped **just inside a nearby entrance to the Underdark**
and have been preying on the local wildlife. The dragon **tried to deal with them
itself and failed**: they slipped deeper into the cave, and it did not pursue.

That is the hook's logic — it needs people small enough to follow where it cannot
go.

# DM answers, round 2 (2026-09-23)

## Why the scroll failed — Silas never had the spell

**Silas does not have *plane shift* and does not know how it works.** That is the
thematic reason his first attempt failed. **Vrenn assumed he knew**, and so never
told him the caster must *envision a destination* — Vrenn expected the scroll
would simply open onto roughly where the party had come from.

When it was his own turn to cast, **Silas was unconscious on the basement floor**,
so Vrenn had no choice but to do it himself and envision the only above-ground
place he knew.

**Nothing to retcon.** Note for the record that the party were told a *different*
reason at the table in Session 3 — Vrenn's line that he had scribed the scroll
with his own magic quill, so only he could cast from it. Both can stand: the
quill is what **Vrenn believes and said out loud**, and it may well be true of
that scroll. The destination rule is a separate mechanic that governs the casting
Vrenn actually performed. If a player ever pushes on it, the interesting answer
is that Vrenn offered the quill in good faith and was being kind.

## How Morvek finds them, and why he is not here yet

**The Grave Token tells him Vrenn left the plane. It does not give him a precise
destination.** He must locate Vrenn first, then *plane shift* himself.

**He cannot do it immediately** — no slot available — and his situation may
prevent it the following day as well. **The DM chooses when he arrives.**

### Suggested mechanics, if a concrete procedure is wanted

RAW is unusually helpful here and gives three natural delays:

- ***Scrying* (5th) explicitly fails across planes.** So Morvek cannot simply
  look at Vrenn from the Abyss — the Token is all he has while he is over there.
- The clean two-step is therefore: **Token gives him the plane &rarr; he *plane
  shifts* to the Material Plane in general terms &rarr; now on the same plane, he
  *scries* to pin Vrenn down.** That is a 7th-level slot and then a 5th, on
  separate days if he is short.
- ***Scrying* allows a Wisdom saving throw**, and the DC is modified by how well
  the caster knows the target and whether he has a body part or possession of
  theirs. **Morvek owns Vrenn's soul**, so he should succeed easily — but if the
  DM ever wants one more day of delay, a made save is a diegetic way to buy it.

### Why he is busy — a reason already on the board

If a convenient obstacle is wanted, one is already established and costs nothing
new: **Morvek is a tenant.** He arrived at the platform at dawn to two dead
guards, an emptied workbench, a missing tuning fork and no drow. Meanwhile, in
the house he rents, **two of his hired oni are dead, the landlord's caged beast is
awake and agitated, and the landlord was due back that same day.** He has to
answer for all of that to a demon who is roughly his equal, dislikes him, and now
has a grievance. That is a perfectly good reason a lich spends a day or two not
chasing anybody.

## Vrenn may simply leave

**The DM is fine with this.** The party is not expected to push him away, but
they may well let him go voluntarily, and that moots the pursuit prep. Do not
build anything that depends on him staying.

## The dragon

- **Adult.** Exact age is not critical yet; he will be **described** so that
  attacking him reads as signing a death warrant. If a fight does happen, the DM
  will flex between **CR 15 and CR 22** for the best table experience.
- **Name and profile still wanted.**
- **Arrival is played by ear.** The DM wants the party to **rest before any
  encounter** and expects *Leomund's Tiny Hut* almost immediately on arrival.
- **He can smell the Abyssal residue and lies in wait.** Still talks, still does
  not attack.

### RULING: can he negotiate with them through the Tiny Hut? YES.

*Leomund's Tiny Hut* bars **creatures and objects** from passing, and blocks
**spells and other magical effects** from extending through or being cast through
it. **It says nothing about sound.** The dome is not soundproof, so **an ordinary
conversation works in both directions.**

That produces a better scene than either alternative:

- **The dome is opaque from outside and transparent from inside.** The party can
  see the dragon perfectly. **The dragon cannot see them at all** — it is
  negotiating with a featureless dome it knows it cannot open.
- **Neither side can cast at the other**, which is exactly why it talks.
- **It is 10-ft radius and immobile, and it collapses if Silas steps out.** Five
  bodies (four PCs plus Vrenn) fit inside the nine-creature limit, but they are
  pinned in one spot for eight hours, in a clearing an adult green dragon is
  standing in.
- **An opaque dome appearing in a forest clearing is a beacon**, which is a
  perfectly good reason he finds them fast.

Both options the DM raised remain available and can be used together: **he talks
to them through the dome, and he is still there when it drops.**

*One rules argument worth pre-empting only if it ever turns hostile: whether a
dragon's breath weapon counts as a "magical effect" barred by the dome. It is a
monster ability rather than a spell, and the RAW is genuinely ambiguous. Decide
before the table asks; it does not matter while he is talking.*

## Vrenn's backstory — separated

**Menzoberranzan is from his youth** and is **completely separate** from the
capture. The capture happened **while rescuing his older sister in the
Undermountain.** The continuity flag raised above is therefore resolved: two
different Underdark episodes, decades apart, not one confused one.

Not intended to be fleshed out further for now.

## Thundertree and the main plot — DECIDED

**Neverwinter is NOT connected to the corrupted-artifact plot.** Strike the
supply-chain link entirely. The landing site proves nothing about where shipments
go, and the Thundertree seed is gone rather than needing a new carrier.

**Thundertree survives as a location the DM likes** and is fair game for
something unrelated if the party heads that way.

## The DM's expected path

Gut read, not a rail: **deal with the fomorians &rarr; use that to get their
bearings from the dragon &rarr; head to Neverwinter to find Anselm Ferreck and a
way back to the Academy, or a way to contact Voss.**

> **If that holds, [[anselm_ferreck]] is the next prep target after the dragon**,

# TEDROVAXILLIATH — the green dragon of Neverwinter Wood (2026-09-23)

**Name:** Tedrovaxilliath. **Shortens to "Ted"** the moment a player tries it.

**Playing the abbreviation:** he gives the full name with ceremony, clearly
expecting it to land. Somebody says "Ted." **He does not bluster.** He goes
completely silent for a beat, then carries on the sentence he was in the middle
of, using "Ted", as though it were beneath him to have noticed. Correcting them
would be admitting he cared what they call him. The name sticks permanently,
*he* allowed it, and a green dragon remembers that kind of small humiliation.

## What he is

**Adult green dragon**, roughly six or seven hundred years old — deep into adult,
not ancient, and near enough to the boundary to be thinking about it. **Baseline
CR 15, flex to CR 22** if it ever comes to a fight. It should not come to a
fight: describe him so that attacking reads as signing a death warrant.

**One wing is torn and scarred**, and the damage is permanent.

## What he cares about

**The wood, and everything living in it that is not a person.** Herds,
watercourses, the health of the canopy, the balance of predator and prey. He is
genuinely a conservationist inside his own borders — and it is **husbandry, not
kindness.** He is the apex predator of a system he maintains because a healthy
forest feeds him for another eight hundred years.

People sit outside that system. He will talk to them, bargain with them, and eat
them, and none of those are in tension for him. **Do not soften this.** The
players should work out his ethics from how he tells a story, not from a speech.

## Personality — slightly paranoid, but reasonable

His suspicion is **justified**, which is what makes it playable rather than
tiresome. Four armed strangers and a drow materialised out of a portal in the
middle of his forest, stinking of the Abyss. *Any* sensible creature treats that
as a probe.

- He assumes they are an advance party for something else, and says so.
- He asks questions designed to catch a lie, and circles back to the same ones
  later to see whether the answers match.
- **He will not believe "we arrived here by accident."** That is the truth and it
  sounds exactly like a lie — which is the best scene in the encounter. The
  answer is not [[tito|Tito]]'s Deception +12. The answer is consistency, and
  letting him check it.
- **He listens to reason.** A straight answer, given the same way twice, actually
  moves him. He is careful, not mad, and he is not looking for an excuse.
- **He will not let them anywhere near his lair.** The fomorian job is
  deliberately in the opposite direction, and that is not an accident.

## The scar, and the treant — his own account

He will tell this story, because **it is not an embarrassment to him.** He was
not beaten and he did not overreach; as far as he is concerned he was attacked
for doing nothing but existing.

**A treant came to kill him, and they fought, and he won.**

His version, which he believes: a green dragon's presence changes the ground
around its lair. Growth twists. Thorns come in where they were not. The water
goes wrong. Over a century of him living there, it spread — and the treant
eventually decided that he was a disease in the wood and came to remove him. He
regards this as absurd. *He* is the reason the herds are healthy. *He* keeps the
watercourses clear. The treant looked at a hundred yards of bad ground and
ignored eight hundred square miles of forest he has personally kept in order.

**Both of them were right, and that is the point.** Ted's blind spot is that the
corruption is real and it is his, and he cannot see it because he measures the
wood by the health of its game. A player who names that out loud has genuinely
got somewhere with him.

> **THE TREANT IS ALIVE.** Ted believes it is dead because **it has not moved in
> ten years.** Treants can stand still for decades. He is wrong, he has no idea
> he is wrong, and the thing is standing somewhere in his forest with an opinion
> about him. Deliberately left as a thread to come back to.

## The wing, and why he needs strangers

**Do not state this. Let them infer it from the scar.**

The torn wing means reduced range. He does not patrol as wide as he used to, so
his effective territory has shrunk, which makes what is left more precious —
which is why **two fomorians eating his game is an existential problem rather
than an irritation.** It is also why he is reduced to asking strangers to go into
a hole for him. A whole, younger dragon would simply have handled it.

**He is a slightly diminished predator managing a slightly diminished kingdom**,
and that makes him a far better negotiating partner than a dragon at full
strength.

## THE LAIR — DM ONLY, do not surface

### Where it is

**A collapsed limestone sinkhole in the deep wood**, well away from the arrival
clearing and well away from the fomorian cave. From above it reads as nothing:
an unremarkable dense tangle of thorn and vine, a hundred yards across, that
nothing walks through.

**The entrance is under water.** A feeder channel of the same creek the party can
hear from the clearing runs beneath the thorn-mass and opens into a drowned
antechamber. Ted goes in submerged, and has to fold the bad wing to do it. He
hates that, and it is part of why he flies less than he should.

### How it can be found without a map

**Follow the water upstream and the corruption inward.** This is the treant's
entire grievance made physical, and it is a far better way to find a dragon's
lair than a map:

- The creek near the clearing runs *out* of his territory and is clean.
- Walk it up and the water degrades — silt, then a green cast, then a taste, then
  nothing living in it.
- The growth changes with it: thicker, thornier, more twisted, fewer species.
- **The deadest, worst ground is directly over the sinkhole.**

Any druid, ranger or even an attentive traveller can read that gradient. It also
means that if the party ever go looking, **they will arrive already knowing he
was lying to himself**, which is a strong way to open a second conversation.

### What the hoard actually looks like

**Not a heap of gold.** A green's hoard is half-drowned, silted and grown over.

- **The floor of the main chamber is a shallow standing pool**, and most of the
  coinage is *under* it — silted in, corroded, much of it fused into masses that
  will never be counted. Centuries of caravan takings off the High Road and the
  Triboar Trail.
- **Trophies sit on dry ledges above the waterline**, arranged with care. This is
  the part he thinks of as the hoard.
- **Three sources, and he can tell you which is which:** overland trade, the
  elven ruins the wood has swallowed, and **debris scoured out of Neverwinter
  when Mount Hotenow erupted** — which is a quiet tie to one of the four
  directions if that ever matters.
- **Roots have come through the ceiling** and grown down into it. He has never
  cut them. The lair is slowly being eaten by the forest he maintains, and he
  does not appear to have noticed the irony.

### What he actually values — and it is not the gold

He is a green. **He hoards leverage and information**, and he would trade a great
deal of metal for either:

- **Maps and records of the wood**, some centuries old, several of his own
  making. He knows every watercourse in his range by name — names he assigned.
- **Anything identifying who sent an intruder.** He keeps the *sigils, letters
  and commissions* found on people who came to kill him, long after he has
  discarded their gear. He has a record of every party ever sent, and by whom.
- **A small number of beautiful things** he simply likes and will not discuss.

**None of that is on the table as a reward**, and if the party ask for the
records specifically, he becomes extremely interested in why.

## THE REWARD — what he will part with

A dragon values **size, permanence, beauty and leverage.** Anything that gets
used up, anything drab, and anything sized for small folk is beneath his notice —
which happily includes most of what a level-10 party actually needs.

**Pick two or three. Recommended core: the potions plus one other.**

1. **A rotted adventurer's pack, still sealed at the neck — four potions.**
   Two *healing*, one *greater healing*, one unlabelled. **His line: "They
   spoil. They are of no use to me."** This is the single best fit in the list:
   **the party has no healing potions at all** after spending their Provisions
   credits elsewhere, and a hoarder's contempt for consumables is exactly why he
   would hand them over without a second thought.

2. **A bag of holding.** He finds it contemptible **because it is a sack.** No
   lustre, no size, no presence. *"A sack. You understand. A sack."* Very funny,
   genuinely useful, and pointed at [[guntrah|Guntrah]], who owns no backpack of
   any kind and has been using [[ollie|Ollie]] as one since Session 3.

3. **A cloak of elvenkind.** Drab grey-green — to him, the colour of nothing.
   Answers the party's single worst mechanical weakness, which is that their
   stealth is dreadful (Guntrah +1 with armour disadvantage, [[tavian_stormnet|
   Tavian]] at &minus;2 in plate).

4. **An elven blade out of the ruins**, a +1 weapon, which he considers a toy —
   too small to be admired, too plain to be beautiful.

5. **Safe passage, directions, and the lie of the land.** Costs him *nothing*,
   and he will present it as the generous part of the offer. Where they are, what
   lies in each direction, which water is safe, where the Underdark entrance is,
   and how to get out of the wood toward Neverwinter without meeting anything
   worse than him. **This is what the party actually needs most**, and he knows
   it, and pricing it as a favour is very green of him.

### One optional item, flagged rather than recommended

**A *scroll of sending*.** Consumable, therefore worthless to him, and it would
let the party get twenty-five words to [[ellery_voss|Voss]] — answering "a way to
contact the Academy" in one object. **That is a significant plot lever**, not a
loot drop: it short-circuits a problem the arc might want them to carry for a
while, and it steps on [[anselm_ferreck|Ferreck]]'s scene as the lifeline. Use it
deliberately or not at all.

### What he will not part with

The records of who has been sent after him. The maps. The few beautiful things.
And **any information about his lair's location** — including, pointedly, the
state of the water upstream, which he will change the subject away from.

# THE REGIONAL MAP (DM, 2026-09-24)

Saved to `assets/neverwinter_wood_map.png` and registered as the dm-only entity
**`neverwinter_wood_map`**, so it opens inside the app during the session.

## The two fixed points

- **The party landed at the dot of the "i" in *Neverwinter***, on the map's
  "Neverwinter Wood" label — near the centre of the forest, south of the river
  system, north of Falcon's Hunting Lodge, west of the Circle of Thunder.
- **[[underdark_breach]] is about five miles north, ACROSS THE RIVER.**

**The river crossing is new** and was not in the original five-mile walk. Noted in
the session file: Tavian is in plate at DEX &minus;2, Silas has STR 7 and Athletics
&minus;2. It is a crossing, a delay, and a good place for something to be watching
from the far bank — or for nothing to happen at all, which after two sessions of
relentless pressure is its own kind of beat.

*Scale looks like roughly ten miles to a grid square, judging by Neverwinter to
Thundertree, so five miles is about half a square. Worth confirming against
whatever the source map states.*

## What else is on it — and the four connections that matter

**ALL FOUR of the post-Session-2 directions are on this map**, which makes it the
single most useful prop for the moment the fork finally opens:

| Direction | On the map |
|---|---|
| West | **Thundertree** |
| Northwest | **Mount Hotenow** (and **Gauntlgrym** beyond it) |
| South | **the Triboar Trail**, lower right |
| East | **Agatha's Lair** — the plan's "Agatha's Grove" |

### 1. The Shrine of Savras — the best find on the map

Lower right, south-east of Conyberry. **The campaign already has a live, planted
Savras thread and nobody has connected it to a place yet:** [[archcleric_senna]]
makes the Rings of Augury herself, nobody took one at the Conferral, and
`session_2.html` records the Savras question as explicitly open. [[silas]] also
carries *augury* as a ritual in his spellbook.

**That thread now has a physical destination** roughly a week's travel from where
the party are standing. Free, and it costs nothing to leave on the map until it
is wanted.

### 2. The Dread Ring — a Thayan necromancy site, and this campaign has a lich

Centre-south of the Wood, close to the party. Undeveloped here and **not connected
to [[morvek]] by anything at all** — but a ring of Thayan necromancy sitting a
few miles from a party currently being hunted across planes by a lich is the kind
of coincidence a campaign can choose to stop being a coincidence. **Flagged, not
proposed.**

### 3. Lost Mine geography, already built in this repo

**Thundertree, Cragmaw Castle, Conyberry, Old Owl Well and the Triboar Trail** are
all Lost Mine of Phandelver locations, and `campaigns/lost-mine/` already contains
built entities for several of them — including `ruins_of_thundertree` and
`venomfang`, the young green dragon squatting there. If [[tedrovaxilliath]] holds
this wood, that relationship is free plot: parent, rival, predecessor, or the
reason a younger green had to settle for a ruin on the edge.

### 4. Neverwinter itself

Far west on the coast, past the western verge of the Wood. **This is where
[[anselm_ferreck]] is**, and it is the DM's expected destination once the
fomorians are dealt with. Helm's Hold sits south of it on the High Road.

## The rest, unclaimed and unconnected

Sharandar (north-west, in the Wood) · Logger's Camp · Falcon's Hunting Lodge ·
Circle of Thunder · Woodland Manse · Xinlenal (a fallen Netherese enclave) ·
Vellosk · Tower of Twilight · Berun's Hill · Starmetal Hills · Butterskull Ranch ·
Dragon Barrow.

**None of these are claimed by anything in the campaign.** They are a menu, not a
commitment — and several sit inside or on the edge of Ted's six-mile radius,
which means he can name and describe any of them accurately when the party ask
him where they are.
