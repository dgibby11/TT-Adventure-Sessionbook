The State of Play at Round One

No time has passed. No short rest, no long rest — they have been going since the morning of Commencement. On the stone: two armanite corpses (nothing to loot; salvageable components at most). Most of a 60-ft U of wall of stone with one panel knocked through, [[silas|Silas]] still concentrating on it — cast on round one, ten-minute duration, so it is an asset with a visible expiry. [[the_captive_drow|The drow]] still under his workbench. A spent plane shift scroll. [[guntrah|Guntrah]] airborne after a bonus-action dash; Ollie near [[tito|Tito]].

Resources, as they stand: Tito has spent all four Luck points and is nearly out of Bardic Inspiration; he has burned a 5th, a 4th and a 1st. Silas is holding a 5th on the wall. Tavian's bless has lapsed; his guiding bolt is spent. Guntrah took 34 lightning. Nobody has healed. This matters — Route A's whole first half is about getting a long rest.

The Clock

At the table the drow said "fifteen more of them here in about a minute," and that is what the party heard. It is an estimate, not a fact (DM, 2026-08-26): he has watched these things for a year and knows their numbers and patrol patterns, and fifteen-in-a-minute is where he puts the highest probability. He was speculating.

DM Only — what the hedge buys you

  • It converts a countdown into a decision window. You are not obliged to drop fifteen armanites on the platform on a stopwatch.
  • A forward patrol of three or four arriving ahead of the main body is the most useful shape: it pressures them to move rather than fortify behind Silas's wall, and it makes the wall's ten-minute expiry felt rather than explained.
  • Whatever arrives, the answer is a chase, not a stand. Two armanites played close to trivial because a 60-ft wall of stone hard-counters melee-only enemies. Do not run the follow-up as a scaled-up version of the same fight.
  • If they ask him how sure he is, he tells them the truth: "I've watched them a year. Fifteen's what I'd bet. I've been wrong."

Vrenn

He comes out from under the bench and is, for about four minutes, the most useful person on the platform. He gives his name unprompted, produces the tuning fork, and lays out two options in roughly eighteen seconds — then hands the decision straight back so the rest of the clock belongs to the players.

DM Only — the full scene

The monologue, the follow-up bank, the true hand, the Grave Token, and where he will and will not go are all on his live entity — see [[the_captive_drow|the drow]], "Session 3: his name, his plan, and what it costs him." Companion depth doc: planning/vrenn_the_captive.json (the_true_hand, the_grave_token, session_3_role).

The one thing to hold in your head while running it: play him competent first. Procedural, clipped, a man who knows this site the way a night-shift worker knows a warehouse. The deflecting, trailing-off rambler is who he goes back to being once the pressure comes off — and the collapse only lands if the competence came first.

He must state plainly, before they choose: there is one scroll, and Vrask is coming for it. If they take it he meets Vrask empty-handed. He says it without asking them not to. (Vrask does not kill him — DM, 2026-08-26 — but the party cannot know that, and the not-knowing is the payload into Session 4.)

The Decision

The question is not "how do we escape." The only exit takes until morning to build, so the question is "where do we survive the next twelve hours" — and both routes are answers to it.

  • Route A — go to ground nearby and come back at dawn. Vrenn transcribes a fresh plane shift scroll in about an hour. Vrask arrives at dawn for it.
  • Route B — the structure east. There is a way out inside it. It is not undefended, and Vrenn will not pretend it is a good idea.

ROUTE A — Go to Ground, Come Back at Dawn

DM Only — the shape of the branch, and why it is not a non-event

[[silas|Silas]] has Leomund's Tiny Hut, and it is a ritual (character sheet, 2026-08-30) — no slot, no preparation needed, available every night whatever he prepped. He does not have Rope Trick. So do not design this branch around whether they can survive the night. They can. Design it around the four things the hut cannot do:

  1. Ten minutes to cast. A ritual is not an escape button. They cannot raise it with something crossing the bridge at them; they have to get clear first.
  2. The dome is opaque and coloured from the outside. It is a twenty-foot hemisphere of solid colour standing in a landscape with nothing else in it. Placement is the entire puzzle.
  3. It is immobile, and it ends if Silas leaves it. Once it is up, that is where they are, all night, and nobody scouts out and back.
  4. Eight hours, exactly. A long rest is eight hours. With roughly twelve hours until dawn, that leaves about two hours of exposure at each end — and the hut cannot cover both.

So the branch is three scenes: the break (getting off the platform), the night (where the dome goes, and what walks past it), and the return (taking the platform back and holding it for an hour). Nothing gets through the hut. Everything happens on either side of it.

Expect Alarm too — also a ritual, also 8 hours. Free early warning, and fine; let it work.

A1. The Break — getting off the platform

The site is an island. One permanent stone bridge crosses the lava moat, and that is where the hooves are coming from. The moat is roughly fifteen feet across and lit from below.

DM Only — running it

The bridge is contested by definition, so the interesting question is whether they cross the moat somewhere else. Fifteen feet over open lava is a real jump and they can mostly make it: [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] has STR 16 and a running long jump of sixteen feet, [[tito|Tito]] has Mirthful Leaps (+1d8), [[guntrah|Guntrah]] flies, and Silas has Misty Step, Fly and Feather Fall. Silas himself is the problem — STR 7, AC 11, and the one person who absolutely must not fall in. Let the party solve that; it is a good three-minute problem and it makes Tavian and Guntrah useful in a scene that would otherwise belong to the casters.

Vrenn crosses without fuss. He has done it a thousand times and knows a place where the lip is closer. If the party never asks him, he does not volunteer it until somebody is about to do something stupid.

Silas's wall is still up and has minutes left on it. Dropping concentration to cast something else costs them the wall; keeping it costs him a 5th-level slot he will want back. Either way it expires during this scene, and it should expire visibly.

If they try to fight the forward patrol instead: let them, briefly, and let it go badly enough to be informative. Three or four armanites in the open with no wall is a genuinely dangerous fight for a party with no slots and no healing. The scene's lesson is that the platform is not holdable tonight.

A2. The Night

The ground beyond the moat — read-aloud material

Off the outcrop, the land is a low rolling plain of ash and slag, shin-deep in places, grey-black and soft underfoot. Vents breathe smoke out of it at intervals, which is where all that haze comes from — it hangs at chest height and thins as it rises, so anything standing on the ground can see about forty feet, and anything above it can see for miles.

The plain is not empty. Long low spines of cooled slag run across it toward the mountains, ten to twenty feet high, hard underfoot and dark. Scattered between them: the spoil of old workings nobody has touched in a long time — collapsed shafts, the stubs of retaining walls, a sink deep enough to stand in and not be seen.

And the ash takes footprints, and holds them.

DM Only — terrain is a PROPOSAL, swap freely

Everything above is invented to fit what the recording already established (desolate, smoke, rumbling underfoot, distant screaming, mountains barely visible, one stone bridge, a causeway south). It is built to do three specific jobs, so if you replace it, keep the jobs:

  • Cover exists in specific places, not everywhere — so choosing where to camp is a real decision instead of a formality.
  • Behind a slag ridge or down in the sink, the dome is not visible from the platform. Out on the open ash, it is visible from a long way off. That is the whole puzzle.
  • The ash holds a trail. Four people and a construct walking out leave a line pointing straight at wherever they stopped. The answer is available and unstated: walk the slag ridges, which are hard and take no print. Let them find it; do not offer it.

The structure east is visible from the ridge tops — that is how Route B gets offered to the eye rather than only in dialogue. Do not detail it; it is not designed.

Roughly twelve hours until Vrask comes. The hut covers eight of them.

DM Only — the night, in three beats

Beat 1 — they find the bodies. While the party is still out in the open, or just as the dome goes up, noise carries from the platform: the main body arriving and finding two dead armanites and sixty feet of stone wall that was not there yesterday. Play it entirely as sound. It tells the party, accurately, that going back is now a problem.

Beat 2 — the search. Something quarters the ash field for a couple of hours. This is where placement pays out:

  • Well placed (behind a ridge, in the sink, trail covered) — they hear it pass, twice, and nothing happens. Reward that with a clean night and say so plainly; do not manufacture a scare they earned the right to skip.
  • Badly placed (open ash, or a trail leading to the door) — it finds the dome. It cannot get in. So it sits down outside and waits. That is far worse than a fight and costs no hit points: eight hours of something breathing on the other side of a wall they cannot see through, and it is still there in the morning. The long rest still happens. The exit is now the problem.

Beat 3 — Vrenn talks, if he came. This is the alcove scene the original design always wanted and never got: a defensible dead-end with nothing to do for hours. It is the one place he opens up, and it should be the only quiet in the session. Keep to his budget — the fork, the schedule, Vrask's name, "there's more of us now than there used to be." Nothing about himself, nothing about the sister, nothing about the Grave Token beyond the one flat sentence. If they push kindly and well, give them one thing that is not on the list, and make it small.

If Vrenn stayed behind, the party spends the night not knowing whether he is alive, and the return in A3 carries that. Both versions are good; do not steer.

They get the long rest. Full HP, full slots, Tito's four Luck points back, and — the one that decides the ending — Guntrah's five uses of Flash of Genius. That is the reward for solving placement, and it is what makes A6 winnable. Make them believe they might not get it; then give it to them.

A3. The Return — taking the platform back

They need to be at the workbench with a full hour of dark left. Coming back at first light is coming back too late.

DM Only — this is Route A's actual climax, not the casting

The platform is occupied. Two of their own turned up dead here; leave a standing watch on it — two or three armanites, not fifteen, posted rather than patrolling. The party has to take it back quietly, because noise at this hour brings the rest of them, and then hold it for an hour while Vrenn writes.

That is the encounter: a defence with a clock and a win condition that is not "kill everything." It is the right fight for this party and the right use of what they have — and note that this time you know wall of stone and wall of force are coming, so plan around them rather than being blindsided the way Session 2 was. Wall of force in particular cannot be broken through at all, so give the encounter a reason they cannot simply seal themselves in and wait: Vrenn has to be at the bench, the bench is where the bench is, and something has to be able to reach it or there is no scene.

Escalate on a timer, not on damage. Something arrives at the twenty-minute mark and again at the forty; the last of it lands as Vrenn is rolling the scroll up. If they are winning too easily the answer is more arrivals, not tougher ones — the horror of this place is quantity.

If Vrenn stayed behind: he is at the bench, alive, and has already started. He guessed they would come back. He is wrong about most things and was right about this, and it should land as the first warm moment in three sessions.

A4. The transcription

How it works

Vrenn has a few prepared vellums ready, along with his prepared inks, components and calligraphic supplies. With the tuning fork to hand, transcribing the working takes him about an hour. He casts nothing and spends no spell slot — he is a natural copyist, and he reproduces the text exactly. It works because it is exact, not because he understands it.

It saps him. Once per day and no more, and he is visibly worse afterwards.

DM Only — the knack, and why it does not break him

Named provisionally "a true hand" (vrenn_the_captive.json → the_true_hand) — rename freely, nothing depends on the words. It is the same faculty as his priming, pointed at ink instead of metal: exactness without comprehension. That is specifically not enchantment, so the locked cruelty stands — Vrask still keeps him short of the final step on everything that matters. He can copy a working. He could not originate one, alter one, or aim one anywhere the original was not already aimed.

Why Vrask tolerates it at all: a site that ships needs somebody who can post the parcel, and a lich is not coming down every week to do it himself.

He cannot use it himself. He would be taken with it — that is the reason he gives — and he could not cast it in any case.

DM Only — scroll economics, for when the party asks what this is worth

Verify the numbers before the table. XGtE's Crafting a Spell Scroll table puts a 7th-level scroll at roughly 50 days and 25,000 gp; the DMG's rarity system is the other route (a 7th-level scroll is Very Rare there). (An earlier figure of 5,000 gp / 64 days circulated in this planning thread and was wrong.)

Where the money actually sits, so nothing has to evaporate:

  • The fork is the capital cost — site-grade, plane-attuned, permanent, reusable. Call it the great majority of the value. This single allocation explains why there is only one on site, why Vrask collects it personally, and why stealing it is a serious theft.
  • The consumables are the vellum and the inks — hide prepared to hold a planar working, which is literally Vrenn's job description, and ink carrying something that has itself crossed planes.

Vrenn will probably press his spare supplies on [[silas|Silas]] before they go — vellum, inks, components, handed over unprompted and awkwardly. This gives Silas materials, not the knack; he scribes by the ordinary rules. Note he is already carrying ink, an ink pen and ten sheets of parchment on his own sheet, so he will understand exactly what he is being handed and what it is worth. Settle the vellum count before a player asks.

A5. One scroll

There is one, and Vrask is coming for it. If the party takes it, Vrenn is standing at his workbench at dawn with nothing to hand over.

DM Only — play this straight, not as a guilt trip

He says it plainly and then hands it over anyway; he does not ask them to stay, does not bargain, and dodges any follow-up — "That's not your problem. Dawn's your problem." Pressed once: "…I don't think he kills me. I'm too much work to replace." He is right, per the 2026-08-26 ruling, and the party has no way to verify it. Do not resolve it in this session.

A6. The casting

[[silas|Silas]] is the only one who can do it. Vrenn cannot — he would be taken with it. [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] cannot use the scroll at all (plane shift is not on the paladin list). [[tito|Tito]] is a poor fallback at a much worse modifier and should only come up if Silas is down.

DM Only — the odds, and the actual dial

Casting a spell above your level from a scroll is an ability check with your spellcasting modifier, DC 10 + spell level = DC 17. Silas is +9, so he needs an 8.

  • Unaided: 35% failure. (Not 20% — worth knowing, since that number was in play during design.)
  • + [[guntrah|Guntrah]]'s Flash of Genius — reaction, +5 to an ally's ability check within 30 ft, five uses per long rest, and he has not spent one all campaign: needs a 3. 10% failure.
  • + [[tito|Tito]]'s Bardic Inspiration (d10) on top: fails only on a natural 1 and a 1 on the d10. About 1 in 200.
  • Tito's four Luck points are also back after the long rest, which is a third layer if they want it.

So the real dial is not the DC — it is whether they think to stack it. The first attempt failed because they had ninety seconds and no plan; this one should be winnable because they prepared and rested. The DM has said he will hint that they may want to make sure this casting does not fail like the last one. Plan C (a second failure) is deliberately not planned yet — flagged, held.

Plane shift carries up to eight willing creatures joined in a circle. Four PCs plus Ollie fits comfortably.

A7. The fork — take it or leave it

The component is not consumed by the casting, so the fork goes with whoever is holding it. That is a real choice made under a clock: physical proof of everything they have seen and a genuine future asset, versus the one object standing between Vrenn and Vrask's temper in about an hour.

DM Only

Do not signpost it. If nobody thinks of it, Silas is holding the fork when the spell goes off and they find out later what they took. It is not an escape hatch either way — plane shift is 7th level and the party's ceiling is 5th (confirmed on his sheet: slots run 4/3/3/3/2 and stop). They will carry the means to go home and be unable to use it for a long time. Do not soften that; the ache is the point. The payoff is real, though: a Silas who eventually casts plane shift himself does get to choose the destination.

A8. Where they land

DM Only — the destination, and the oopsie

Vrenn can make the scroll. He cannot aim it. He copies the one working he was taught, and the only surface place he has ever had a name for is "Neverwinter." Ask him where it sends you and that is the entire answer he has.

Which is the trap, and it is a good one: Neverwinter is a region as well as a city. Plane shift's own text says you specify a destination in general terms and "appear in or near that destination," at the DM's discretion. They name Neverwinter. They arrive in Neverwinter Wood — the right name, the wrong Neverwinter.

Nobody at the table should be told this was an error. To the party it is simply an unfamiliar forest, and the read they should reach is "we need to find one of Voss's names," not "let's go home." This preserves the locked Neverwinter Wood landing, the quiet Thundertree seed, and the four-direction fork at the top of Session 4 — and it delivers all of it through a person rather than an inspection roll nobody made. They arrive as cargo.

Background, no urgency (DM, 2026-08-29): Vrask is the one who personally cast plane shift for the FAIL Academy consignments. That end of the lane is his, not Vrenn's. Not yet developed and not needed this session.

ROUTE B — The Structure East

DM Only — STUB. Deliberately not designed yet.

What is fixed so far: it is a nearby structure belonging to a minor demon lord (identity undecided). It holds a teleportation circle on an underground level below the main floor, with a tough but beatable guard on it. Getting in, and then finding the room, both need obstacles — likely a mix of evasion and further fights. Vrenn will accompany the party there if asked; he still will not leave the plane.

Open before this can be written: which demon lord · how far away and what the approach looks like · whether the circle is ruled planar (a teleportation circle is same-plane by RAW, so this one needs to be a planar gate or an explicit exception, and being the demon lord's private line off-layer is a clean reason for it to be guarded) · and where it drops them, which is the big one, because if it is not Neverwinter Wood then Session 4's fork moves and a second arrival needs prepping.

One thing to design around from the start: Silas has Clairvoyance (1 mile) and Arcane Eye. This party can scout the inside of a building thoroughly without entering it, and an infiltration built on not knowing the layout will simply be bypassed. Build it so the interesting problem survives being seen in advance.

Held at the DM's instruction. Do not build on it.
