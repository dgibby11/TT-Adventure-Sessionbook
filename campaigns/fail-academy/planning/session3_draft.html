The Clock

At the table the drow said "fifteen more of them here in about a minute," and that is what the party heard. It is an estimate, not a fact (DM, 2026-08-26): he has watched these things for a year and knows their numbers and patrol patterns, and fifteen-in-a-minute is where he puts the highest probability. He was speculating.

DM Only — what the hedge buys you

  • It converts a countdown into a decision window. You are not obliged to drop fifteen armanites on the platform on a stopwatch.
  • A forward patrol of three or four arriving ahead of the main body is the most useful shape: it pressures them to move rather than fortify behind Silas's wall, and it makes the wall's ten-minute expiry felt rather than explained.
  • Whatever arrives, the answer is a chase, not a stand. Two armanites played close to trivial because a 60-ft wall of stone hard-counters melee-only enemies. Do not run the follow-up as a scaled-up version of the same fight.
  • If they ask him how sure he is, he tells them the truth: "I've watched them a year. Fifteen's what I'd bet. I've been wrong."

Vrenn

He comes out from under the bench and is, for about four minutes, the most useful person on the platform. He gives his name unprompted, produces the tuning fork, and lays out two options in roughly eighteen seconds — then hands the decision straight back so the rest of the clock belongs to the players.

DM Only — the full scene

The monologue, the follow-up bank, the true hand, the Grave Token, and where he will and will not go are all on his live entity — see [[the_captive_drow|the drow]], "Session 3: his name, his plan, and what it costs him." Companion depth doc: planning/vrenn_the_captive.json (the_true_hand, the_grave_token, session_3_role).

The one thing to hold in your head while running it: play him competent first. Procedural, clipped, a man who knows this site the way a night-shift worker knows a warehouse. The deflecting, trailing-off rambler is who he goes back to being once the pressure comes off — and the collapse only lands if the competence came first.

He must state plainly, before they choose: there is one scroll, and Vrask is coming for it. If they take it he meets Vrask empty-handed. He says it without asking them not to. (Vrask does not kill him — DM, 2026-08-26 — but the party cannot know that, and the not-knowing is the payload into Session 4.)

The Decision

The question is not "how do we escape." The only exit takes until morning to build, so the question is "where do we survive the next twelve hours" — and both routes are answers to it.

  • Route A — go to ground nearby and come back at dawn. Vrenn transcribes a fresh plane shift scroll in about an hour. Vrask arrives at dawn for it.
  • Route B — the house east. There is a way out inside it. It is not undefended, and Vrenn will not pretend it is a good idea.

ROUTE A — Go to Ground, Come Back at Dawn

DM Only — the shape of the branch, and why it is not a non-event

[[silas|Silas]] has Leomund's Tiny Hut, and it is a ritual (character sheet, 2026-08-30) — no slot, no preparation needed, available every night whatever he prepped. He does not have Rope Trick. So do not design this branch around whether they can survive the night. They can. Design it around the four things the hut cannot do:

  1. Ten minutes to cast. A ritual is not an escape button. They cannot raise it with something crossing the bridge at them; they have to get clear first.
  2. The dome is opaque and coloured from the outside. It is a twenty-foot hemisphere of solid colour standing in a landscape with nothing else in it. Placement is the entire puzzle.
  3. It is immobile, and it ends if Silas leaves it. Once it is up, that is where they are, all night, and nobody scouts out and back.
  4. Eight hours, exactly. A long rest is eight hours. With roughly twelve hours until dawn, that leaves about two hours of exposure at each end — and the hut cannot cover both.

So the branch is three scenes: the break (getting off the platform), the night (where the dome goes, and what walks past it), and the return (taking the platform back and holding it for an hour). Nothing gets through the hut. Everything happens on either side of it.

Expect Alarm too — also a ritual, also 8 hours. Free early warning, and fine; let it work.

A1. The Break — getting off the platform

The site is an island. One permanent stone bridge crosses the lava moat, and that is where the hooves are coming from. The moat is roughly fifteen feet across and lit from below.

DM Only — running it

The bridge is contested by definition, so the interesting question is whether they cross the moat somewhere else. Fifteen feet over open lava is a real jump and they can mostly make it: [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] has STR 16 and a running long jump of sixteen feet, [[tito|Tito]] has Mirthful Leaps (+1d8), [[guntrah|Guntrah]] flies, and Silas has Misty Step, Fly and Feather Fall. Silas himself is the problem — STR 7, AC 11, and the one person who absolutely must not fall in. Let the party solve that; it is a good three-minute problem and it makes Tavian and Guntrah useful in a scene that would otherwise belong to the casters.

Vrenn crosses without fuss. He has done it a thousand times and knows a place where the lip is closer. If the party never asks him, he does not volunteer it until somebody is about to do something stupid.

The wall runs out during this scene — ten minutes total, and most of it is already spent. It was cast on round one of a roughly six-round fight, so call it nine minutes left when Session 3 opens. Nothing later in Route A may depend on it still standing: by the time they come back at dawn it is twelve hours gone.

One real decision hiding in that, though. Wall of stone's own text: "If you maintain your concentration on this spell for its whole duration, the wall becomes permanent and can't be dispelled." So [[silas|Silas— can spend the next nine minutes concentrating and leave a permanent sixty-foot U of stone standing on the platform — which is a serious gift to A3's hour-long defence, and it is exactly the sort of thing this party is smart enough to work out. The cost is that he can cast nothing else that needs concentration while they are breaking out, and he keeps burning the 5th-level slot he would rather have back. Do not prompt it. If they take it, A3 gets much easier and they earned it; if they drop it, the wall is gone and A3 is the fight as written.

If they try to fight the forward patrol instead: let them, briefly, and let it go badly enough to be informative. Three or four armanites in the open with no wall is a genuinely dangerous fight for a party with no slots and no healing. The scene's lesson is that the platform is not holdable tonight.

A2. The Night

The ground beyond the moat — read-aloud material

Off the outcrop, the land is a low rolling plain of ash and slag, shin-deep in places, grey-black and soft underfoot. Vents breathe smoke out of it at intervals, which is where all that haze comes from — it hangs at chest height and thins as it rises, so anything standing on the ground can see about forty feet, and anything above it can see for miles.

The plain is not empty. Long low spines of cooled slag run across it toward the mountains, ten to twenty feet high, hard underfoot and dark. Scattered between them: the spoil of old workings nobody has touched in a long time — collapsed shafts, the stubs of retaining walls, a sink deep enough to stand in and not be seen.

And the ash takes footprints, and holds them.

DM Only — terrain is a PROPOSAL, swap freely

Everything above is invented to fit what the recording already established (desolate, smoke, rumbling underfoot, distant screaming, mountains barely visible, one stone bridge, a causeway south). It is built to do three specific jobs, so if you replace it, keep the jobs:

  • Cover exists in specific places, not everywhere — so choosing where to camp is a real decision instead of a formality.
  • Behind a slag ridge or down in the sink, the dome is not visible from the platform. Out on the open ash, it is visible from a long way off. That is the whole puzzle.
  • The ash holds a trail. Four people and a construct walking out leave a line pointing straight at wherever they stopped. The answer is available and unstated: walk the slag ridges, which are hard and take no print. Let them find it; do not offer it.

The structure east is visible from the ridge tops — that is how Route B gets offered to the eye rather than only in dialogue. Do not detail it; it is not designed.

Roughly twelve hours until Vrask comes. The hut covers eight of them.

DM Only — the night, in three beats

Beat 1 — they find the bodies. While the party is still out in the open, or just as the dome goes up, noise carries from the platform: the main body arriving and finding two of their own dead on the working floor. Play it entirely as sound. It tells the party, accurately, that going back is now a problem.

On the corpses (DM ruling, 2026-08-30). They stay where they fall and they rot where they lie, and bodies scattered across the plain in various states of decay are completely unremarkable here — this place chews through its own constantly. Two useful consequences: out on the ash the party can use carcasses as cover and as a reason a search passes them by, and nothing about a dead armanite is inherently an alarm. On the platform it reads differently, because two guards dead on the floor of a working shipping site is not weather, it is an event. Note also that the armanites themselves are established as not intelligent — they may well not draw any conclusion at all. It is whoever supervises them who notices, and that is a lead the party can pull on if they ever ask Vrenn who checks up on these things.

Beat 2 — the search. Something quarters the ash field for a couple of hours. This is where placement pays out:

  • Well placed (behind a ridge, in the sink, trail covered) — they hear it pass, twice, and nothing happens. Reward that with a clean night and say so plainly; do not manufacture a scare they earned the right to skip.
  • Badly placed (open ash, or a trail leading to the door) — it finds the dome. It cannot get in. So it sits down outside and waits. That is far worse than a fight and costs no hit points: eight hours of something breathing on the other side of a wall they cannot see through, and it is still there in the morning. The long rest still happens. The exit is now the problem.

Beat 3 — Vrenn talks, if he came. This is the alcove scene the original design always wanted and never got: a defensible dead-end with nothing to do for hours. It is the one place he opens up, and it should be the only quiet in the session. Keep to his budget — the fork, the schedule, Vrask's name, "there's more of us now than there used to be." Nothing about himself, nothing about the sister, nothing about the Grave Token beyond the one flat sentence. If they push kindly and well, give them one thing that is not on the list, and make it small.

If Vrenn stayed behind, the party spends the night not knowing whether he is alive, and the return in A3 carries that. Both versions are good; do not steer.

They get the long rest. Full HP, full slots, Tito's four Luck points back, and — the one that decides the ending — Guntrah's five uses of Flash of Genius. That is the reward for solving placement, and it is what makes A6 winnable. Make them believe they might not get it; then give it to them.

A3. The Return — taking the platform back

They need to be at the workbench with a full hour of dark left. Coming back at first light is coming back too late.

DM Only — this is Route A's actual climax, not the casting

The platform is occupied. Two of their own turned up dead here; leave a standing watch on it — two or three armanites, not fifteen, posted rather than patrolling. The party has to take it back quietly, because noise at this hour brings the rest of them, and then hold it for an hour while Vrenn writes.

That is the encounter: a defence with a clock and a win condition that is not "kill everything." It is the right fight for this party and the right use of what they have — and note that this time you know wall of stone and wall of force are coming, so plan around them rather than being blindsided the way Session 2 was. Wall of force in particular cannot be broken through at all, so give the encounter a reason they cannot simply seal themselves in and wait: Vrenn has to be at the bench, the bench is where the bench is, and something has to be able to reach it or there is no scene.

Escalate on a timer, not on damage. Something arrives at the twenty-minute mark and again at the forty; the last of it lands as Vrenn is rolling the scroll up. If they are winning too easily the answer is more arrivals, not tougher ones — the horror of this place is quantity.

If Vrenn stayed behind: he is at the bench, alive, and has already started. He guessed they would come back. He is wrong about most things and was right about this, and it should land as the first warm moment in three sessions.

A4. The transcription

How it works

Vrenn has a few prepared vellums ready, along with his prepared inks, components and calligraphic supplies. With the tuning fork to hand, transcribing the working takes him about an hour. He casts nothing and spends no spell slot — he is a natural copyist, and he reproduces the text exactly. It works because it is exact, not because he understands it.

It saps him. Once per day and no more, and he is visibly worse afterwards.

DM Only — the knack, and why it does not break him

Named provisionally "a true hand" (vrenn_the_captive.json → the_true_hand) — rename freely, nothing depends on the words. It is the same faculty as his priming, pointed at ink instead of metal: exactness without comprehension. That is specifically not enchantment, so the locked cruelty stands — Vrask still keeps him short of the final step on everything that matters. He can copy a working. He could not originate one, alter one, or aim one anywhere the original was not already aimed.

Why Vrask tolerates it at all: a site that ships needs somebody who can post the parcel, and a lich is not coming down every week to do it himself.

He cannot use it himself. He would be taken with it — that is the reason he gives — and he could not cast it in any case.

DM Only — scroll economics, for when the party asks what this is worth

Verify the numbers before the table. XGtE's Crafting a Spell Scroll table puts a 7th-level scroll at roughly 50 days and 25,000 gp; the DMG's rarity system is the other route (a 7th-level scroll is Very Rare there). (An earlier figure of 5,000 gp / 64 days circulated in this planning thread and was wrong.)

Where the money actually sits, so nothing has to evaporate:

  • The fork is the capital cost — site-grade, plane-attuned, permanent, reusable. Call it the great majority of the value. This single allocation explains why there is only one on site, why Vrask collects it personally, and why stealing it is a serious theft.
  • The consumables are the vellum and the inks — hide prepared to hold a planar working, which is literally Vrenn's job description, and ink carrying something that has itself crossed planes.

Vrenn will probably press his spare supplies on [[silas|Silas]] before they go — handed over unprompted and awkwardly. Two prepared vellums (DM, 2026-09-01), on top of the one he spends finishing the scroll, plus the inks and components. This gives Silas materials, not the knack; he scribes by the ordinary rules — and note he is already carrying ink, an ink pen and ten sheets of parchment on his own sheet, so he will know exactly what he has been handed and what it is worth.

What two vellums actually are: two future spell scrolls, of spells Silas knows and can prepare, at XGtE's ordinary time and cost. A small, real, permanent asset rather than a plot device. They do not shortcut the ceiling problem — scribing requires the spell prepared, so he cannot make himself another plane shift. Expect a player to ask that in the first minute.

A5. One scroll

There is one, and Vrask is coming for it. If the party takes it, Vrenn is standing at his workbench at dawn with nothing to hand over.

DM Only — play this straight, not as a guilt trip

He says it plainly and then hands it over anyway; he does not ask them to stay, does not bargain, and dodges any follow-up — "That's not your problem. Dawn's your problem." Pressed once: "…I don't think he kills me. I'm too much work to replace." He is right, per the 2026-08-26 ruling, and the party has no way to verify it. Do not resolve it in this session.

A6. The casting

[[silas|Silas]] is the only one who can do it. Vrenn cannot — he would be taken with it. [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian]] cannot use the scroll at all (plane shift is not on the paladin list). [[tito|Tito]] is a poor fallback at a much worse modifier and should only come up if Silas is down.

DM Only — the odds, and the actual dial

Casting a spell above your level from a scroll is an ability check with your spellcasting modifier, DC 10 + spell level = DC 17. Silas is +9, so he needs an 8.

  • Unaided: 35% failure. (Not 20% — worth knowing, since that number was in play during design.)
  • + [[guntrah|Guntrah]]'s Flash of Genius — reaction, +5 to an ally's ability check within 30 ft, five uses per long rest, and he has not spent one all campaign: needs a 3. 10% failure.
  • + [[tito|Tito]]'s Bardic Inspiration (d10) on top: fails only on a natural 1 and a 1 on the d10. About 1 in 200.
  • Tito's four Luck points are also back after the long rest, which is a third layer if they want it.

So the real dial is not the DC — it is whether they think to stack it. The first attempt failed because they had ninety seconds and no plan; this one should be winnable because they prepared and rested. The DM has said he will hint that they may want to make sure this casting does not fail like the last one. Plan C (a second failure) is deliberately not planned yet — flagged, held.

Plane shift carries up to eight willing creatures joined in a circle. Four PCs plus Ollie fits comfortably.

A7. The fork — take it or leave it

The component is not consumed by the casting, so the fork goes with whoever is holding it. That is a real choice made under a clock: physical proof of everything they have seen and a genuine future asset, versus the one object standing between Vrenn and Vrask's temper in about an hour.

DM Only

Do not signpost it. If nobody thinks of it, Silas is holding the fork when the spell goes off and they find out later what they took. It is not an escape hatch either way — plane shift is 7th level and the party's ceiling is 5th (confirmed on his sheet: slots run 4/3/3/3/2 and stop). They will carry the means to go home and be unable to use it for a long time. Do not soften that; the ache is the point. The payoff is real, though: a Silas who eventually casts plane shift himself does get to choose the destination.

A8. Where they land

DM Only — the destination, and the oopsie

Vrenn can make the scroll. He cannot aim it. He copies the one working he was taught, and the only surface place he has ever had a name for is "Neverwinter." Ask him where it sends you and that is the entire answer he has.

Which is the trap, and it is a good one: Neverwinter is a region as well as a city. Plane shift's own text says you specify a destination in general terms and "appear in or near that destination," at the DM's discretion. They name Neverwinter. They arrive in Neverwinter Wood — the right name, the wrong Neverwinter.

Nobody at the table should be told this was an error. To the party it is simply an unfamiliar forest, and the read they should reach is "we need to find one of Voss's names," not "let's go home." This preserves the locked Neverwinter Wood landing, the quiet Thundertree seed, and the four-direction fork at the top of Session 4 — and it delivers all of it through a person rather than an inspection roll nobody made. They arrive as cargo.

Background, no urgency (DM, 2026-08-29): Vrask is the one who personally cast plane shift for the FAIL Academy consignments. That end of the lane is his, not Vrenn's. Not yet developed and not needed this session.

ROUTE B — The Bonehold

An hour's walk east, and the building has a way out in the basement. It is not undefended, and Vrenn will not pretend otherwise.

DM Only — naming

outpost is already the docs' word for the ritual platform, so the building needs its own. Filed here as the Bonehold — rename freely. This is a filing label for these documents and must not be spoken at the table (see the Name Budget block above). Vrenn calls it "the house," and so should you. He would not grant it a grander name, the flatness is the characterisation, and it costs the players nothing to remember.

DM Only — whose house it is, and the hierarchy question

The Bonehold belongs to a minor demon lord, unnamed, in fealty to Orcus. Vrask is billeted on him and he cannot refuse — Orcus's writ, carried down through Harthoon. He is roughly Vrask's equal in power, slightly under, which is exactly what makes it a humiliation rather than an arrangement.

He is NOT a rung in the chain of command. That was the DM's own concern (2026-08-31 — "too heavy in middle management") and the answer is that he is lateral. The chain stays Vrenn → Vrask → Harthoon → Orcus, and Harthoon is Phase 3 material who will not be named for a long time. What the party can actually perceive this session is two rungs and one name: the drow, the one he owes work to, and "somebody above him."

He dislikes his tenant, and that is a deliberate asset (DM, 2026-08-31). He would be pleased if something inconvenient happened to Vrask — not pleased enough to act, but enough to look the other way. Flagged as a future diplomatic option rather than a Session 3 element: nothing in this session needs him to appear, speak, or be named.

Three things this buys for free: it explains the building's split personality (a demon's hall with a lich's working basement fitted badly into it); it explains why the glabrezu is bound rather than commanded, being a borrowed guard in a borrowed house; and it makes the thing behind the north gate the landlord's insurance, which Vrask has to keep his valuables next to and quietly resents.

Alternative if he ever feels like clutter: cut him and the Bonehold is simply Vrask's. Nothing on the map goes unexplained — a skull is Orcus's own symbol, so the crest over the gate needs no landlord.

DM Only — where Vrask is tonight

Out, and back at dawn. He is on his rounds and he leaves for the ritual platform from here, which puts both routes on the same clock and means the party may be inside his house when he departs, or cross him on the road. Vrenn knows the schedule and will say so if asked.

The hard constraint holds either way: Vrask does not appear in person and is not a combat presence at the Bonehold. He is an absence, an authority, and a deadline.

B1. The Walk — one hour, mostly exposed

Minor elevation changes, dead trees, large boulders and strange rock formations give intermittent cover. The majority of the crossing is moderately exposed to whatever inhabits the plain.

DM Only — the balance fix, and it matters

Route B contains no long rest. The party would hit the basement on Session 2's fumes — no Luck points, Tito's Bardic gone, Silas down a 5th, everybody hurt. That is genuinely lethal against what is down there.

The hour's walk is a short rest — IF it is uneventful (DM ruling, 2026-09-01). A short rest is a full hour, so an encounter on the road breaks it. Uninterrupted, it gives them Hit Dice, Tito's Bardic Inspiration back (Font of Inspiration), Silas's Arcane Recovery (five levels of slots), and Tavian's Channel Divinity. Guntrah's Flash of Genius stays spent either way, which is correct — that is Route A's reward and Route B should not also get it.

So decide what happens if the road goes badly, because the basement does not care. Arriving on Session 2's fumes against a glabrezu plus waves is genuinely lethal. Two valves, and the first is better:

  • Let them rest outside the house. Another hour, in cover, before they go in — which costs clock, and the clock is Vrask returning at dawn. That puts the decision on the players and makes an eventful road a real price rather than a punishment.
  • Or drop a wave from B7. If the road cost them a fight, the basement escalation loses one arrival. Silent adjustment, no table conversation.

Do not do both, and do not let them walk in tired with the full escalation waiting.

Corpses are scattered across this plain in every state of decay and are completely unremarkable here (DM, 2026-08-30). They are usable as cover and as a reason a search goes past.

DM Only — pick two of three

  1. Vargouilles, four to six. The threat is not the damage, it is the shriek — a screaming cloud over open ash draws whatever else is out there. Exactly the right pressure for a branch about not being noticed.
  2. A roaming armanite pair. Consistency: they know these now, and this time they have terrain and warning. Entirely avoidable with good play.
  3. The thing that screams — use this one. Pays off [[session_2|Session 2—'s own arrival text ("loud, very distant screaming monsters", plural, large, never explained). Do not stat it. It crosses the middle distance. The correct answer is to lie flat in the ash and not move, and anyone who attacks it dies. Zero prep, zero combat, and it makes the plain feel inhabited rather than populated. It also sets up the north gate — see B6.

B2. Getting In

Vrenn points out the weak spots. There is no exterior access to the basement; everything goes through the ground floor.

  • The front door — guarded and trapped.
  • The back entrance — less guarded, and untrapped.
  • The gaps — collapsed masonry and vents, big enough for some of them.

DM Only — the gaps split the party, on purpose

By the squeezing rules a Medium creature in a Small space moves at half speed, attacks at disadvantage, and is attacked at advantage. Practically: [[tito|Tito— (5'8") gets through and Ollie certainly does; [[silas|Silas— (5'10") probably does; [[guntrah|Guntrah— at 7'7" and [[tavian_stormnet|Tavian— at 6'6" in plate genuinely cannot. They will try anyway — let them fail on it rather than forbidding it.

So the natural play is that the small ones go in and unbar the back door from inside, which means bar the back door from the inside and let the gaps be its answer. For a few minutes two PCs are alone in a lich's house. That is the scene.

The front door gets a glyph of warding — a deliberate callback, since this party has personally been through one on a package and should feel it in their stomach. Make it an alarm glyph, not a damage glyph: in this branch the currency is noise, not hit points. DC 15 Investigation to spot (Guntrah's +9 finds it), and Silas has dispel magic, so it is solvable rather than punitive.

DM Only — two spells that will bypass all of this if unplanned

  • Stone Shape (4th) — RULED (DM, 2026-09-01): the walls are ordinary stone and it works. Silas carves a 5-ft passage through up to 5 ft of stone and makes his own door, bypassing the front door, the back door and the gaps in one action for a 4th-level slot. That is a real reward and the DM has said explicitly that if Silas works it out, good for him. Let it be clean and let it feel clever.
  • But it should not skip the basement — PROPOSED, needs a nod. If the floor is also five feet of ordinary stone, he can cut straight down into the vault and the entire sneak and stair evaporate. Recommendation, and the map supports it: the basement is hewn into living rock — note the rough black stone around the chamber's edges — with well over five feet between the ground floor and the vault below. So stone shape buys them the building, which is a genuine and satisfying bypass of B2, and does not buy them the set piece.
  • Knock (2nd) opens the barred door without the gaps, and it is loud — the spell announces itself with a knock audible 300 feet away. That is a fair trade and a fun one.
  • Both Silas and Tito have greater invisibility. Party stealth is otherwise poor (Guntrah +1 with armour disadvantage, Tavian −2 in plate), so do not build the sneak to require everybody to roll Stealth. Build it so the interesting choice is which resources they burn.

B3. Inside — the short sneak

A small distance to the basement stair. Vrenn knows exactly where it is, having been brought here a few times.

DM Only

Three or four spaces, one patrol, one thing that must not be woken. Keep it short — the basement is the set piece and this should not compete with it. Whatever they fail to deal with up here is what comes down the stairs during the climax (see B7), so their sloppiness sets the difficulty of the ending. Say nothing about that at the time.

Ambient roster, scenery rather than fights: skeletons and zombies doing labour (mindless, quiet, no alarm unless they see), shadows in the dark corners (Strength drain, and a stealth threat rather than a combat one), and at least one ghast — because Tito's Ring of Free Action makes him immune to its paralysis, and cashing in a ring he chose two hours earlier is worth building a monster around.

A deathlock as a steward is a good optional: something that can be talked past, bribed or fooled rather than fought.

B4. The Basement

Battle map on file with the DM. Roughly 75 by 130 feet (count the squares), with a ritual circle some 35 to 40 feet across worked into the floor at the centre in red, four braziers standing at its corners, a wide stair up at the south, and a barred gate under a demon-skull crest at the north.

DM Only — what each feature is for

  • The circle is big enough that the fight happens around the objective, not in a corner. That is a gift — the party cannot turtle up and wait.
  • The four braziers are the mechanism, not decoration. The circle is something they have to start, not step onto. That makes the climax a defence with a timer around an objective — structurally the same shape as Route A's hold-the-platform hour, which is good: one session, one shape, two flavours.
  • The embalming slab, manacles and basin in the north-east are the lore object. Something is half-prepared on it.
  • The crates in the south-east are the Session 2 make-up: put an empty unmarked crate matching the one from the Provisions Annex there, same build, same grain, same banding. That is the load-bearing clue that died in Session 2, sitting in a corner the map already drew, findable by anyone who got a good look at the original.
  • The bone-work and tool benches (south-west) hold the same half-finished work the party saw at Vrenn's bench — the connection between the two sites, available without a roll to anyone who looks.
  • The grated openings in the west wall are drainage, and the seep for shadows and small things. Minor escalation channel only.
  • The blood. Six or seven pools, and a DC 13 Medicine or Investigation says it is not all one kind, and some of it is fresh. The freshest is the pool nearest the north gate. That is how they work out it gets fed, without anyone telling them.

B5. The Guard

DM Only — GLABREZU. Quarantined — this whole block is the swappable part.

A planar bound glabrezu (CR 9, 5,000 XP). INT 19, telepathy 120 ft, truesight, magic resistance, and dispel magic at will — which is a genuine answer to Silas's walls and a welcome change from Session 2. Bargaining is its entire design brief: it talks, it offers, it lies by omission, and it fights when the deal collapses.

The hook that makes it more than a monster: the binding sigil is part of the same floor-work as the circle. Powering the circle means lighting the braziers, lighting the braziers slackens the binding, and therefore the demon's escape is their escape. So it is helpful, informative and charming, and its help is the trap. The fight comes at the end, on the circle, as they are leaving, which is the right place for it.

Variant if you would rather not release a demon into the campaign: the braziers do not actually touch the binding, the glabrezu believes they will, and its fury when it does not work is the fight trigger. Same scene, no loose demon.

Its disclosure budget — what it may safely give: that Vrask owns the place; that Vrask answers to somebody above him; that things are made here and shipped to the surface; that the traffic has been increasing; what the circle costs. Those middle two are precisely the takeaways that failed to land in Session 2 — this is the second shot at both. It also knows about Vrenn's Grave Token and finds it funny, which is a far harder way for the party to learn his situation than hearing it from him.

What it must NOT say: Harthoon. The name is Phase 3 and vrenn_the_captive.json is explicit that he has no presence at this level. The glabrezu was a gift from someone above Vrask and it will not say who — which is a better beat unnamed anyway. Corvin, Thornwick and Torvald are all naturally safe: it has never heard of any of them, exactly as the drow had not.

It does not say "Thanatos" — DEFERRED (name budget, 2026-08-31). This was previously flagged as a deliberate coin-flip and the answer is now no: the layer's name plus a Religion check would tell them whose realm they are in, which is a real reveal and a fifth new proper noun in a session that already has enough. Keep it for when it can land on its own.

If they try "Thatch" on it, it has never heard of him. Same honest answer the drow gave, from an independent source at the same end of the chain — and a genuine deduction for a table already working that puzzle.

Power note: alone it is 5,000 XP, which is Medium for four level-10s and softer than "tough but beatable". Do not buff the demon — escalate instead (B7). The conversation has to be survivable or there is no conversation.

To swap it out: replace this block with a Boneclaw (CR 12, 8,400 XP, MToF/MPMM) — undead, canonically created and bound to an evil spellcaster master, a quiet ambusher with long reach and shadow-teleport, and a straight Hard fight with no dialogue. Nothing else in Route B changes.

B6. The North Gate

Barred and warded beneath the skull crest. Nothing behind it makes a sound — until the room gets loud, and then something very large adjusts its weight.

DM Only — the loaded gun

Behind the gate is a beast too dangerous even for this realm (DM, 2026-08-31). It is an in-case-of-emergency measure, it belongs to the landlord rather than to Vrask, and it exists to apply pressure — not to be fought.

The rule that makes it work: it cannot leave the basement. So releasing it does not add a monster to the room, it makes the only exit unreachable, because the circle is in here with it. Threat and escape occupy the same floor. That also explains why the thing is caged beside the teleporter rather than at the front door: it is not defending the building, it is defending the asset — which quietly tells the party what the circle is worth.

Make it one of the things they already heard. Session 2's arrival gave them "loud, very distant screaming monsters", plural and unexplained, and it was never paid off. This is one of those, caught and kept. Free continuity, and the party has a sense of the scale of it before they ever look at the door.

Who can open it: the glabrezu cannot — it is bound and the ward is not its to touch. But it knows exactly what is in there, it enjoys saying so, and it will threaten to open the gate if the party gets difficult. A lie the party has no way to check, from a creature whose entire nature is load-bearing lies. If the pressure ever needs to be real, Vrask or the landlord's steward can open it from outside.

The better gift: the party can open it themselves. A group losing badly might crack that gate deliberately for the chaos and run for the circle. It is a genuinely terrible option, available at all times, and that is the most interesting kind.

Do not stat it. The moment it has a block somebody will want to fight it. If it comes out, the scene is a flight, not a fight — the same rule as the thing that crosses the plain. If you want a block in your back pocket, Nightwalker (CR 20, MToF) is the pick: undead, so it belongs in a lich's basement, and canonically a thing that other undead are afraid of.

Sensory detail: the wear marks are on the inside of the bars, and the stone around the frame is polished smooth where it has pressed.

B7. Running the Climax

DM Only

The braziers are the clock. Lighting and feeding all four takes long enough to matter — tune the number of rounds to how the fight is going, but decide the shape before the table: it is a defence with an objective, and the win condition is not "kill everything".

Escalate up the stairs, behind them. The north gate is not the reinforcement door any more — it is the loaded gun. Waves come from the ground floor: whatever they slipped past in B3, plus ghasts, shadows, or a deathlock. Rough numbers, arriving in waves rather than together: glabrezu (5,000) plus four ghasts (1,800) prices out above Deadly if simultaneous, which is exactly why it should not be simultaneous.

Escalate on the timer, not on damage. If they are winning too easily the answer is more arrivals, not tougher ones. The horror of this place is quantity.

Watch the west grates for a cheap "something is already inside with us" beat between waves.

B8. The Circle, and Where It Lands

DM Only

Ruled planar, and the reason it is guarded: a teleportation circle is same-plane by RAW, so this one is the landlord's — or Vrask's — private line off the layer. Do not argue the rules at the table; it is the Abyss and it is theirs.

They can use it. They cannot aim it. Same limitation as Vrenn's scroll, for the same reason: it is keyed to where this operation ships. Both routes therefore land in Neverwinter Wood, and you prep one arrival — see A8 for the "Neverwinter is a region as well as a city" reading, which works identically here.

Changed 2026-08-31, deliberately. An earlier note in this thread argued Route B should land them somewhere different as the price of the risky path. Scrapped: the consequence budget for Route B is spent on the glabrezu (and on what they may have let out of a gate), which is a better cost than a different patch of forest.

B9. Traps

DM Only — two, and neither does damage

A lich with a standing staff and mindless guards does not trap his own corridors — he would lose servants to them. Traps belong where strangers arrive and on the one door worth protecting.

  1. The glyph of warding on the front door (B2). An alarm, not a blast. It does not hurt anybody; it tells the house.
  2. A shriek-ward on the basement stair. This is the branch's difficulty dial. Find and dispel it and the glabrezu conversation is a private one; miss it and the ground floor starts coming down the stairs from round one.

Free tension needing no design: Tavian in plate on stone stairs. That is a Stealth check, not a trap.

B10. Vrenn on This Route

DM Only — and the quiet moral difference nobody will notice at first

He walks them out, points out the weak spots, and shows them where the basement stair is. Then he goes back, because he intends to be at his workbench when Vrask arrives, and that is simply what he does. Let that departure be undramatic; he does not make a speech about it.

Here is the thing worth noticing: Route B costs him nothing. He has all night to make Vrask's scroll as normal, so he meets dawn with the work done. Route A saves the party and wrecks his morning; Route B is riskier for them and leaves him unharmed. He will state it once, flatly, without pleading, and then let them choose:

If they are weighing the two

"If you go to the house, I still have tomorrow's work to give him. If you take the scroll, I don't."

He does not repeat it and he does not argue. If they take the scroll anyway he hands it over exactly as written in A5.
