Before the Ceremony — Voss's List

Read Aloud / Performance

[[ellery_voss|Voss]] catches the four of you in a side corridor while the rest of the graduating cohort is still filing toward the seating — robes half-adjusted, no time for a real conversation. She presses a folded slip of paper into whichever of you is nearest. "Before I forget, and before today gets its hooks into all of us. Names and cities — people I trust, if you're ever somewhere I'm not and need to be somewhere safe. Memorize it, don't lose it, don't need it. Most operatives never do." A beat, and something almost like a smile. "Go. You're about to be late for your own graduation."

Give the players the physical prop if you want one — a short list of first names and cities along the Sword Coast (Neverwinter, Silverymoon, Everlund are the safe bet, matching [[ninth_thesis|the Ninth Thesis]]'s already-established operative network; see ninth_thesis.html's dm-only "sample existing operatives" note for texture if you want to attach a name to one of them). This is a plant, not a puzzle — it should read as routine onboarding, not foreshadowing, and the players have no reason yet to think they'll need it before the day is out.

Commencement Begins

Held at [[founders_statue|the Founder's Statue]] — the geometric center of the original campus, where the three main paths meet. Rows of folding chairs for family and underclassmen fan out from the plinth; the graduating cohort stands in ranks facing it. Give this the full description pass: banners in department colors strung between the colonnades, the particular quality of late-morning light through the old trees, the low murmur of several hundred people trying to be quiet at once, family members craning for a first look at their graduate. None of the four have any reason to know this ground means anything beyond tradition — it's just where Commencement has always been held.

Dowe's Opening — Read Aloud / Performance

[[headmistress_dowe|Headmistress Dowe]] opens the ceremony herself, brief and characteristically dry. "You were told, on your first day here, that this Academy exists to make you formidable. I remember saying it — I say it every year, and every year I mean it a little differently than the year before. Whether it also makes you wise is, I'm afraid, still up to you." She lets that sit a beat longer than a normal opening would, then moves on, brisk again. "This year we're fortunate enough to have a guest join us before we get to the part you're actually here for. Please welcome Councilor Bertram Hollis, of Silverymoon's Merchant Council."

The Guest Speaker

The Speech — Read Aloud / Performance

Councilor Bertram Hollis is a stout, well-fed human in Merchant Council formal dress, clearly delighted to have been asked and utterly unaware of how little that delight is shared. He speaks for longer than the invitation strictly called for, in the specific cadence of a man who has given this exact speech, or a close cousin of it, at several other institutions this year already. "Each and every one of you has the power to make a real difference in this world..." — pick your own filler from there; the content should be warm, generic, entirely divorced from anything FAIL Academy actually does, and completely forgettable. Play the crowd's attention visibly draining over the course of it: fidgeting, a student two rows over audibly asleep, Professor Crumb cataloguing something on a slate rather than listening, at least one small child loudly asking a parent when it will be over. None of the four PCs are expected to retain a single word of it, and that's the point — this is atmosphere and comic relief, not information.

Ring Selection — The Conferral

Per established campus lore (see the Rings reference in the resource dropdown, now sixteen patterns), this Academy's diploma is the ring — there's no separate document conferred. Once Hollis finally sits down, Dowe calls each graduate up in turn, in no order any of the four can predict (not alphabetical, not by department), to a long table set up before the crowd: a folio, a Registrar's clerk, and a glass case of identical gold bands.

The Conferral — Read Aloud / Performance

When your name is called, you cross to the table. The clerk asks, quietly, just for you to hear: "Pattern?" You name one from the sixteen. She checks it against the folio, taps the matching ring with a small enchanted stylus — a brief, cold flicker of light along the band, gone before you can be sure you saw it — and slides it across. All bands are struck identical: plain gold, the Academy crest to the face, the year inside. Nothing about your choice will be visible to anyone once it's on your hand.

Then Dowe, to the whole crowd, says your name properly and gives you one line — not ceremonial boilerplate, something that says she's actually watched you for five years. [Improvise this per PC at the table, pulled from actual play: something specific to the bulette fight, the parting gifts, or an earlier one-shot beat. "Known for: remembering every student's name, every one" is the entire point of this beat.]

Let the players choose their pattern in character, with the live Rings reference in front of them — no mechanical restriction beyond what fits the moment. Don't steer them: if someone wants X-ray Vision for the joke value, that's a better table moment than the "optimal" pick.

DM Only

Nothing sinister about the ritual itself — this is a genuine, mundane school tradition, not a Ninth Thesis or Thornwick mechanism, and the public staging is real pageantry, not a security measure. If a player asks whether the enchanted stylus does anything besides register the choice, the honest answer is no.

Suggested reads based on established character sheets, offered as prompts only: Ring of Protection or Ring of Resistance for Silas (squishiest party member, AC 11); Ring of Mind Shielding fits Guntrah's investigator streak thematically; Ring of Free Action or Feather Falling suit Tavian's front-line role; Tito already has Magic Resistance from his subclass, so something orthogonal (Ring of Evasion, Water Walking) avoids redundancy. The new Ring of Augury is a live option too — its actual source is intentionally undetermined (see session_plan.json's open_items); if a player takes it, that's fine, just don't improvise an answer to "who's really behind this" at the table before the DM has decided.

Faces in the Crowd

No further vignettes — just glimpses, there for players who are looking. [[commander_orvyn|Commander Orvyn]], rigid and upright near the Fighters' section, catches Tavian's eye and gives the smallest nod — the promise of "another story" still open. [[viola_gossamer|Viola Gossamer]] is visible near the Bards' rows, wings doing that stuttering shimmer, gone the instant Tito looks over. [[oswald_crumb|Professor Crumb]] glances up from his slate only for the actual crossing. [[torvald_thatch|Torvald]] stands at the treeline's edge rather than in the seating — present, at a slight remove, as always.

Where's Voss?

Once all four have crossed and received their rings, and the ceremony is winding toward its close (remaining names, closing remarks — background noise at this point, no need to narrate them in detail), prompt a passive Perception check, or let anyone actively looking around the crowd roll. DC 12 is enough to notice: [[ellery_voss|Dr. Voss]] isn't there. Not late, not in a back row — absent. Given the players' five years of history with her, this should read as immediately, quietly wrong rather than a puzzle to solve — she's not an administrator, has no ceremonial duty here, and still has never once missed a major ceremony in their memory.

DM Only — What's Actually Happening

Voss used the ceremony's own cover — everyone accounted for, [[academy_provisions|Provisions]] reliably unmanned — to try to get into the back room herself and find out what's been sitting on the delayed requisition she's been chasing since session_1.html. She did not expect to find [[osric_morne|Osric Morne]] there; he stayed back deliberately (see his own private reasons in the_prov_investigation.json's osric_beat — paperwork has been going missing on him, and he no longer trusts the normal channels either, so he chose to keep an eye on the office himself rather than attend). The two of them have a real argument — not for player ears, played out entirely off-screen: Osric refuses to let her past the counter, on principle, the same principle that applies to anyone; Voss, uncharacteristically, pushes harder than her usual composure allows before catching herself. She leaves angry, more with herself than with him, and returns to the tail end of the ceremony looking, if anyone happens to notice her slip back in, slightly out of breath and entirely unlike herself.

The party has no way to know any of this yet unless they go find her and ask. If nobody rolls the Perception check or nobody follows up, that's fine — see the branch below.

If the Party Goes to Voss

Voss, Read Aloud / Performance

She doesn't perform composure for them the way she would for anyone else. "I tried Provisions. I know, I know — today, of all days. I couldn't make myself wait any longer for that requisition to surface on its own." A short, humorless laugh. "Osric wouldn't budge. Wouldn't even hear me out. Thirty years of that man telling students no and apparently that includes me now too." She rubs at her temple, catches herself doing it, stops. "I shouldn't ask this of you today. But if the four of you wanted to see what's actually sitting back there — quietly, carefully, and you tell me the moment you find anything, nothing more — I wouldn't stop you. I'd be glad of it, honestly." She doesn't dress it up as an order. It isn't one.

This is the party's clean, in-character way into the Provisions break-in below. She does not accompany them and does not know exactly when they'll go — this is deliberately loose, matching how she's always run the Ninth Thesis (see ninth_thesis.html's operational profile).

If the Party Doesn't Go to Voss

Genuinely open — improvise from the table's own momentum rather than forcing this. A few options if you need one: Torvald mentions in passing that he saw Voss "look rattled" near the Prov earlier and leaves the thread dangling; Osric himself, unusually shaken for a man who never shows it, mentions to whoever he's most comfortable with in the party (if anyone stops by the shop) that "Dr. Voss and I had words earlier — wouldn't trouble yourself over it" in a tone that invites exactly that; or simply let the party's own curiosity about her absence carry them there without her prompting at all, in which case they arrive at the same break-in with less explicit permission and more risk if caught. All three routes converge on the same scene below.

The Provisions Break-In

Reuses the existing bypass menu from the_prov_investigation.json's getting_past_osric section (waiting him out, Sleep/Charm, stealth-and-distraction, or a written note if Voss gave them one) — still fully valid, just recontextualized: this happens sometime after the ceremony, and Osric, fresh off an actual confrontation with Voss, is likely more alert than usual for the rest of the day. Play that as flavor (he's audibly still irritated, ledger work done a little harder than necessary) rather than a mechanical penalty unless a table wants the extra tension.

Once inside the back room and the Intake Annex behind it (same location as the original design — a locked door, DC 12, or Osric's own description if he's been talked into helping), they find a single newly arrived, unprocessed package addressed to Dr. Voss. No other markings of any kind — no shipping label, no sender, nothing to trace. A Detect Magic check reveals a strong aura, Conjuration school specifically, unmistakably related to planar transit rather than anything defensive. Unlike the original crate design, there's no visible ward or sigil to puzzle over — it just looks ordinary, which is its own kind of warning sign to a party that's already primed to be careful.

The Portal

The Trigger — Read Aloud / Performance

The instant the package is actually opened — not merely handled, examined, or moved — a seam splits open in the air beside it: not light, its opposite, a bruised violet-black that seems to drink the light around it, dilating wet and slow like a pupil. Its surface ripples, thick and faintly luminous, closer to liquid than to any portal in a textbook. Copper first, then ash. Sound drops out almost entirely; a character's own heartbeat becomes the loudest thing in the room. Whoever opened it feels a patient, total suction — not a shove, more like the room exhaled and they were standing exactly where the breath went — and is gone in under a second.

Anyone within 10 feet of whoever opened the package gets a reactive DC 15 Dexterity save to avoid being pulled through as well — meant to plausibly catch two or three party members, not necessarily the whole group and not necessarily just one, so the other side isn't a solo scene. Anyone who succeeds is left behind in the Annex with an open, silent, violet-black rift and no way to know what's on the other side of it.

The Other Side

Draw physical description from the shared battle map — an octagonal ritual platform, weathered and ancient. A grand staircase to the north rises to a portico flanked by two large carved idol-busts, faces demonic and worn. A central circular dais, concentric rings and a sigil worked into the stone, dominates the platform's middle — this is the site's working ritual anchor, not decoration. Four small round alcove chambers sit in quatrefoil surrounds, linked to the center by covered colonnades. A single straight causeway runs south off the platform into thick smoke and haze. The whole structure sits on a rock outcrop ringed by a moat of open lava; heat and a reddish glow press in from every side, and nothing beyond the haze is visible at all.

DM Only — What This Place Is

A fabrication/ritual outpost on Thanatos, the 333rd layer of the Abyss where Orcus resides (see campaign_arc.json's premise) — one of the sites where the corrupting items seeded around campus this year (the catalog item, the component pouch, the practice-closet instrument, this package) are actually made, in service of Thornwick/the cult. This is DM-facing knowledge; how much of it the table ever hears by name is your call at the table, not a requirement — reuse the_prov_investigation.json's how_much_to_reveal guidance (safe to confirm: someone built this on purpose, this is where the pattern originates, a name like "the Consortium" connects to Voss's notes if you want to drop it; keep gated: Orcus by name, Corvin, Torvald's identity, the ring, the veil's location).

The Captive at the Workbench

A drow, gaunt and clearly exhausted, is hunched over a workbench in one of the colonnades when the party arrives — working, not resting, on something in the same style as the corrupted items already seen this campaign. Placeholder name: Vrenn — rename freely.

DM Only — Vrenn

A captive, not a willing worker — kept here doing forced labor for long enough that something in him has bent rather than broken outright: a version of Stockholm syndrome, not full loyalty to his captors but not resistance either, just a numb, institutionalized compliance. He does not instinctively help the party — his first reaction to strangers is wary, possibly even hostile-by-reflex rather than grateful, since "outsiders" have never meant rescue before in his experience here. He has some capability in a fight, but he's weak and worn down from the labor, and won't engage unless directly provoked or cornered — he's not looking to die for his captors, but he's also not looking to die at all.

If the party has a moment to actually talk to him rather than just fight through the scene, he can come around — slowly, and only if someone makes a real effort (a Persuasion or Insight-informed approach, not a single line) rather than assuming he'll be an easy ally. If he does come around, he may help them find a way out (see The Escape below) and could even leave with them if persuaded, but this is not a long-term party addition — he has his own unresolved situation and won't stay once he's safely away from this place. His actual knowledge of what he's making, who runs this site, or where it connects is deliberately limited and NOT for the DM to embellish beyond what's below — his backstory and any deeper significance are being held back intentionally by the campaign's own design and shouldn't be improvised at the table.

One thing he does know, from watching resupply drops: roughly how the central dais gets "fed" to open a transit — not why, not where it goes, just the mechanical gesture/component of it. This becomes relevant if the party needs his help forcing an escape (see below).

Playing him at the table: expect to carry a fair amount of dialogue as him — he talks, sometimes at length, once someone gets him started — but almost none of it should actually answer a direct, valuable question. Real questions ("who runs this place," "what are you making," "where are we," "how long have you been here") get deflection, a fixation on some small procedural detail of the labor itself, a half-finished thought that trails off, or an answer to a slightly different question than the one asked — not a flat refusal, more like the part of him that used to process a question and give a straight answer doesn't quite work anymore. Let him ramble; just don't let the ramble accidentally contain real information. Small, human, non-plot detail is fine and even good (a habit, a fixation, a fragment of a life before this) — anything that would actually move the party's understanding of the site, the cult, or Torvald forward is not.

His own way out, never used: tucked into a gap behind a loose stone in his alcove — findable with a DC 13 Investigation check, or shown directly if he's been won over — is a scroll, brittle and dust-covered, plainly untouched in a long time. It's real: his own prepared escape, at some point, however he came by it. He's never used it. Don't force a single explanation for why — let it stay a little unclear, consistent with how underspecified he already is; play it as some tangle of having nowhere better to go and having simply stopped being able to act on his own behalf, and let the table's read of the roleplay decide which it feels like in the moment. If he's been won over, handing it to the party is a real beat — the only thing that was ever going to get him out, given away rather than used. If he hasn't been won over, it's still findable without him, just harder and without his help actually activating it.

The Fight

Shortly after arrival — give the party a few tense rounds to look around, find the package's twin items on the workbench, maybe attempt to talk to Vrenn — two armanites cross the southern causeway. A fight is not strictly unavoidable, but should be played as very likely; these are guards responding to an intrusion, not negotiators.

DM Only — Armanite Cheat Sheet

CR 7 each (Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes / Monster Manual). AC 17, ~114 HP, resistant to cold/fire/lightning, immune to poison damage and the poisoned condition, magic resistance. Multiattack: gore plus two claws; Charge deals extra damage if the armanite moves at least 20 ft straight toward the target first. Two of these against four level-10 PCs is a genuinely hard fight — appropriately so, consistent with how Session 1's bulette was played harder than expected. The party is not required to win outright; the scene is built around needing to escape either way, so a fighting retreat is a completely valid, even expected outcome. Vrenn does not join the fight unless directly provoked or cornered.

The Escape

The rift the party arrived through flickers a couple of times, then vanishes on its own, sometime during or shortly after the fight — there is no going back the way they came. A few options present themselves, not all of them actually leading anywhere useful:

  • The causeway south — the way the armanites came from. Leads further into the site and, presumably, deeper into Thanatos itself, not to safety. A viable retreat to buy time or regroup, not an actual way home; let the party discover this the hard way if they commit to it rather than telling them outright.
  • The alcove chambers — including whichever one Vrenn was being kept in. Small, defensible, dead-ends rather than exits — but a good place to hole up for a round if the fight is going badly, and where Vrenn is most likely to actually talk if he's going to.
  • The central dais — a real way out. Forcing it to reopen a transit under duress (Arcana check, or trivial with Vrenn's help if he's been won over) is unstable without proper ritual preparation. It works, but it doesn't send them anywhere they intended. Exposed — it's out on the open platform, likely still in reach of the armanites.
  • Vrenn's scroll — a second real way out, if found (see The Captive at the Workbench, above). Old and unattuned to anyone using it but him, so reading it cold is unreliable — DC 15 Arcana or the relevant spellcasting ability to activate correctly; a failure just wastes the round rather than backfiring, and trying again is fine. Trivial to activate if Vrenn's been won over and walks them through it himself. Safer to attempt than the dais — the alcove is defensible — but no more precise about where it actually sends them.

DM Only — Two Exits, One Kind of Outcome

Deliberately built as a real choice (push to the exposed dais vs. retreat to the alcove and work the scroll) without being a choice about the ending — both are one-way, imprecise transits away from this site, and you're free to land the party in Neverwinter Wood (see below) regardless of which one they actually use. No need to design two different landing scenes.

Where They Land

The dais dumps the party — and Vrenn, if he came with them — somewhere in Neverwinter Wood, though the players have no way to know that name or their exact position; to them, it's simply an unfamiliar forest, disorienting after where they've just been. Far enough from the Academy that walking home isn't a realistic option, close enough to Neverwinter itself that word of the Ninth Thesis contacts on Voss's list (see Before the Ceremony, above) becomes immediately relevant — the party's own read on their situation should be "we need to find one of Voss's names," not "let's go home."

Session 2 ends here, disoriented and choosing a direction with no idea what's actually in any of them. This is intentional — see Design Notes below for what's waiting in each direction, none of which needs to be prepped until Session 3.
