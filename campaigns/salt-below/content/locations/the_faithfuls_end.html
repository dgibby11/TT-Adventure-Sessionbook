The Faithful's End

LOCATION · Deep Kingdom

Past [[the_vigil_wrack]], a small side-chamber holds what's left of a [[drowned_choir]] pilgrimage cell that made it further than any sanctioned expedition ever has. Bedrolls, cook-gear, and a scatter of hymnal pages are arranged like a camp that was never broken down — not raided, not fled, simply stopped, mid-purpose, and never resumed.

What's left of the pilgrims themselves is here too, and it isn't gentle. Whatever changed them didn't kill them cleanly first.

What the Party Might Notice

  • The hymnal pages scattered here carry the same hymns as [[the_drowned_hymnal]], annotated in a hand that grows more desperate, then more certain, then stops (Investigation DC 14).
  • Nothing about the camp suggests panic or a fight — bedrolls are laid out in order, gear packed with care, as though the group meant to press on the next morning and simply never did (Perception DC 13).
  • The marginalia's final legible line reads less like fear and more like relief — a detail worth sitting with rather than explaining (no check needed to read it; understanding it is left to the player).

DM Notes

[[the_faithful_husk]] — one or more of these transformed pilgrims — may still be present and hostile, but play this room as tragedy first, threat second, consistent with [[drowned_choir]]'s "sincere, half-right" framing. These were true believers who got exactly as far as their faith could carry them and found out too late what "freeing" the kingdom would actually cost. Do not use this room to make the Choir seem foolish or villainous — the relief in the marginalia is the point: they believed they were nearly done being right. A good site to reintroduce [[isolt_vane]] or [[sister_meck]] afterward if either is still active in the campaign, learning what happened to people they knew.

Layout & Hazards

No environmental traps. The camp itself is safe to search — nothing here is rigged or hazardous, consistent with the "stopped mid-purpose" framing above.

Creature placement. 1-2 [[the_faithful_husk]] among the arranged bedrolls, motionless until approached or the hymnal pages are disturbed. If two are used, stagger them a few feet apart rather than adjacent, so the party can potentially notice and choose to avoid the second one after the first turns hostile (or after a party that chooses mercy leaves both undisturbed) rather than facing both as an automatic pile-on. A Drowning Grasp kill here is an especially strong beat given how many named Choir pilgrims are already on record — consider having a spared or fled husk drop the makings of an identifiable body for [[isolt_vane]] or [[sister_meck]] to recognize later.
