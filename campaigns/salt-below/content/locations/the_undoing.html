The Undoing

LOCATION · Deep Kingdom

At the bottom of the shaft below [[the_open_wound]], every passage rejoins. There are no walls here in any conventional sense — the containment given physical shape, a hollow scooped out of the world rather than built into it, where the boundary between "kingdom" and whatever it kept holds thinnest. This is as close as three centuries of careful engineering ever meant anyone to get.

Whatever the party has learned above arrives here with them: the covenant's exact terms from [[the_last_accord]], the lattice's failing structure from [[the_binding_lattice]], the empty socket at [[the_renewal_font]], the cost paid at [[the_vigil_wrack]] and [[the_faithfuls_end]]. This room doesn't explain the choice. It's where the choice gets made.

What the Party Might Notice

  • The three paths the site has offered — sever the lattice, seat the seal, or neither — are all physically enactable from this room, in whatever combination the party has prepared for (no check needed; this is a decision point, not a puzzle to solve).
  • [[it_that_kept_the_kingdom]] is present here in a way it has been nowhere else — not fully manifest, but no longer only pressure and proxy either (Wisdom save at the DM's discretion on first clear sight of it).
  • Nothing about the chamber pressures a decision on a timer beyond whatever tension the table has built — It has waited three centuries and can, within reason, wait for the party to finish talking.

DM Notes

The finale plays out as three branches, per [[why_the_kingdom_drowned_mystery]] and [[the_breaking_deal_mystery]]:

  • Destroy: striking the failure-points identified at [[the_binding_lattice]] finishes the collapse the deal was already undergoing — It is freed, but weakened and disoriented by an ending it didn't choose, on a timer the party actually influences going forward rather than a clean kill. This should read as a genuinely dangerous, open-ended outcome, not a victory lap.
  • Renew: seating [[the_wardwrights_binding_seal]] at [[the_renewal_font]] buys the deal significant time — generations, not centuries — at a real cost the DM should design to fit the table (a piece of the party's own vitality, memory, or luck; something old-kingdom in flavor, not a simple gp cost). It does not go away; it goes back to waiting.
  • Invent: per Rule 9 in the pipeline's own ground rules and this campaign's design intent throughout, a party that proposes something else entirely — a negotiated term, a partial destruction, a bargain of its own — should be taken seriously and improvised around rather than steered back to the two prepared options. This branch exists because the party earned it by paying attention, not because the DM ran out of ideas.

[[it_that_kept_the_kingdom_stat]] holds the mechanical writeup for direct confrontation, which should be a last resort or a consequence of a botched attempt at destroy/renew rather than the default path — see that entry for how to keep even a fight here feeling alien and patient rather than a standard boss slog. This is the campaign's ending; whichever branch resolves, treat Port Calder's fate above as the epilogue, not this room's.

Layout & Hazards

Ambient Pressure. Simply being in this room under Its attention is a low-grade hazard even before any confrontation: a creature that spends more than 10 minutes here without resolving or attempting one of the three branches must succeed on a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw or gain one level of exhaustion, reflecting the sheer weight of sustained proximity rather than any hostile act. Waive this once the party commits to a branch — it's meant to discourage stalling, not punish deliberation.

Creature placement. [[it_that_kept_the_kingdom_stat]] itself, present but not manifest as a combatant unless the party attacks first, botches a destroy/renew attempt badly enough to provoke it (DM's call), or explicitly seeks a fight. If combat starts, run the Lair Actions from that entry (Whispered Command, Boundary Collapse, Look Away) on initiative count 20 every round — they're the encounter's real teeth, more than its Multiattack. No other creatures are placed here; this room is a one-on-one confrontation by design, not a swarm.
