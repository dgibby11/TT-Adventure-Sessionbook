The Suppliants' Colonnade

LOCATION · Deep Kingdom

The bottom of [[threshwater_stair]] opens onto a long colonnade, pillars still standing in even ranks despite three centuries underwater. Between them, statues kneel — hundreds, all worn to the same soft-edged blur, all facing the same direction, all bowed in the same unhurried genuflection. Not a battle line and not a mass grave. A congregation.

This was the old kingdom's pilgrimage route, walked on a fixed calendar by suppliants carrying offerings toward whatever waits at the colonnade's far end. The posture carved into every statue is reverence, not terror — people who came here believing the arrangement was working, not people driven to it by dread.

What the Party Might Notice

  • Every statue's base holds a shallow offering-niche, long since emptied — coin, grain-bowls, or small worked charms, judging by the wear pattern (Investigation DC 13).
  • The directional pull first felt at the stair is stronger here, strongest exactly where the statues face (no check needed).
  • A cluster of five statues near the colonnade's midpoint have been deliberately turned to face back the way the party came — chiseled around at the base, not fallen (Perception DC 15).

Inscriptions

Faded old-tongue script runs along the lower pillar band. Anyone who can read it (or has it translated) finds the same phrase repeated with only minor variation: a plea not for release, but for continuation — that the debt keep being paid, that the arrangement hold. Nothing here begs to be freed. Everything here begs for the deal to last.

DM Notes

This is the campaign's clearest early signal that "destroy the binding" is not the obviously heroic option — the old kingdom's own faithful wanted it to continue, not end. Feeds directly into [[why_the_kingdom_drowned_mystery]] and the party's eventual choice at the climactic dungeon (renew / destroy / third option). The five reversed statues are a hook, not a solved puzzle: reserve their meaning for [[mireths_reckoning]] (whoever turned them knew something, or disagreed with something, and left a physical record of it).
