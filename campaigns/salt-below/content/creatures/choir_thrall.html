Choir Thrall

CREATURE · Choir Thralls

A [[drowned_choir]] convert who pressed too far into "communion" — long vigils at [[chancel_deep]]'s altar-rail, breath held past any mortal limit, chasing whatever answered [[isolt_vane]]'s calling. What comes back isn't dead, exactly, and isn't fully present either: skin gone the grey-white of drowned wood, eyes fixed somewhere past the person in front of them, moving with the unhurried patience of someone who no longer needs to hurry.

Thralls don't preach and don't recruit. They guard — vigil sites, sealed passages, anything the Choir doesn't want disturbed — with a calm, wordless persistence that unsettles more than open hostility would. Convert leadership treats them as the faithful "gone deeper," a mercy rather than a horror; outsiders rarely agree once they've seen one up close.

What the Party Might Notice

  • A thrall breathes water as easily as air, and its chest doesn't rise or fall on any schedule a living diver would recognize (Perception DC 13).
  • It repeats a single phrase or hymn-fragment under its breath, always the same one, whether or not it's under threat.
  • Ordinary Choir converts treat a thrall with quiet, uneasy reverence rather than fear — most avoid meeting its eyes.

DM Notes

Mechanically, a reskinned Zombie (stat block above) — slow but relentless, resistant to the party simply outswimming it, water-breathing rather than land-bound. (No official "Drowned" stat block could be verified on D&D Beyond during authoring, so this uses the standard Zombie profile as the closest confirmed match rather than fabricating numbers for an unverified sourcebook entry.) Use sparingly and only as a guard posted somewhere the Choir genuinely wants protected ([[chancel_deep]], [[the_hollow_choir_yard]]) — never as generic cannon fodder, or it stops reading as tragedy and starts reading as a monster closet. [[sister_meck]] is aware of what thralldom actually costs a convert and privately opposes it; a captured or spared thrall recognized by name is a strong beat for her arc.
