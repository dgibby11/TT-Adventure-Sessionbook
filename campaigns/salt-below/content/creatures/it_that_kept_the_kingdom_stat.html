Traits

Legendary Resistance (3/Day). If it fails a saving throw, it can choose to succeed instead.

Amphibious. It can breathe air and water.

Mucous Cloud. While underwater, it is surrounded by a mucous cloud. A creature that touches it or hits it with a melee attack while within 5 feet of it must make a DC 18 Constitution saving throw. On a failure, the creature is diseased for 1d4 hours. Diseased creatures can't regain hit points except by magical means, and their skin secretes a slimy mucus.

Probing Telepathy. If a creature communicates telepathically with it, it learns the creature's greatest desires if the creature fails a DC 18 Intelligence saving throw.

Actions

Multiattack. It makes three tentacle attacks.

Tentacle. Melee Weapon Attack: +10 to hit, reach 15 ft. (extended by the boundary), one target. Hit: 14 (2d8+5) bludgeoning damage. If the target is a Humanoid, it must succeed on a DC 18 Constitution saving throw or begin to transform, over 1d4 hours, into a form suited to the drowned kingdom — its skin sloughing and eyes growing to fit the deep dark. The transformed creature is under its control. The transformation can be reversed only by a remove curse spell or similar magic.

Tail. Melee Weapon Attack: +10 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. Hit: 16 (3d6+5) bludgeoning damage.

Bind to the Deal (1/Day). It targets one creature it can see within 30 feet. The target must succeed on a DC 18 Wisdom saving throw or be charmed for 1 hour. The charmed target is under its control and can't take reactions, and it and the target can communicate telepathically with each other over any distance. Whenever the charmed target takes damage, it can repeat the saving throw, ending the effect on itself on a success.

Legendary Actions

It can take 3 legendary actions, choosing from the options below. Only one legendary action option can be used at a time and only at the end of another creature's turn. It regains spent legendary actions at the start of its turn.

Detect. It makes a Wisdom (Perception) check.

Tentacle Attack. It makes one tentacle attack.

Command (Costs 2 Actions). It uses Bind to the Deal, if that action isn't already expended for the day.

Lair Actions

On initiative count 20 (losing initiative ties), the containment site itself can take one lair action to cause one of the following effects; it can't use the same effect two rounds in a row.

Whispered Command. It forces its will on one creature it can see. That creature must succeed on a DC 18 Wisdom saving throw or become frightened until the end of its next turn and take 9 (2d8) psychic damage.

Boundary Collapse. The edge of containment briefly gives way in a 20-foot-radius sphere centered on a point it can see within 60 feet. Each creature in that area must make a DC 18 Constitution saving throw, taking 22 (4d10) psychic damage on a failed save, or half as much on a successful one.

Look Away. Any creature that has dealt damage to it with a melee attack since the last lair action must succeed on a DC 18 Wisdom saving throw or become stunned until the end of its next turn — the cost of having been truly seen in return.

DM Notes

Reskinned Aboleth base (link below), scaled up for a level 9-10 party: HP, AC, save DCs, and to-hit bumped roughly to CR 15 by the book's monster-creation guidelines, plus Legendary Resistance, the Legendary Actions block, and the Lair Actions block above, all new. The three Lair Actions are drawn directly from [[the_undoing]]'s environment per the original design brief — a save-forcing whispered command, an area psychic-damage boundary-collapse, and a punish-confrontation "look away" effect — and are meant to do most of the encounter's real work, with the Legendary Actions kept comparatively minor (a free Perception check, an extra tentacle, a rare charm) so the fight doesn't just become three extra attacks a round bolted onto an Aboleth. Aboleth's Enslave was renamed Bind to the Deal and Tentacle's disease clause was reflavored into the same slow transformation the campaign already uses for [[choir_thrall]]-adjacent corruption, rather than inventing a new mechanic.

Critically: direct combat should be the least likely path to actually resolving the finale. It is old, patient, and has never needed to win a fight to get what it wants — see [[the_undoing]]'s DM notes for how the destroy/renew/invent branches are meant to work around a confrontation rather than through one. If combat does happen, it should feel like arguing with weather: overwhelming, alien, and not actually the thing that ends the encounter. A full retreat or a bargain struck mid-fight are both valid, even encouraged, outs.
