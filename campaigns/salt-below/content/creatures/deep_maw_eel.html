Deep-Maw Eel

CREATURE · Mid-Depth Horrors

A genuine predator, and a straightforward one compared to most of what the Drowned Districts throw at a dive crew — which is exactly why it's dangerous. Long enough to coil twice through a flooded corridor, patient enough to wait out a slow-moving survey team, and drawn to the tunnel networks and stalled machinery pits where prey funnels through narrow, predictable routes.

It hunts [[underbell_cistern]]'s tidal tunnels and the stalled gearing of [[millhollow_undercroft]] alike, using the confined geometry to its advantage rather than any unnatural trick. Divers who work these routes regularly know to move in pairs and never linger at a chokepoint — the ones who don't tend to become the reason everyone else knows the rule.

What the Party Might Notice

  • Scrape marks along tunnel walls at roughly torso height, consistent with something large moving through repeatedly (Survival DC 13).
  • A sudden absence of smaller fish or scavengers in a stretch of tunnel that should have plenty.
  • [[drowned_choir]] way-markers through [[underbell_cistern]] route around one particular junction without explanation.

DM Notes

Reskin a Giant Eel or similar aquatic ambush predator — this is meant to be a real, winnable combat encounter, not an omen. It's the district's answer to the Shallows' wreck_crab: physical danger the party can prepare for and fight, a deliberate breather from the omen-heavy sites so dread doesn't flatten into a single register. The Choir's way-marker detour is a light hook — they know it's there and route around it rather than confront it, which says something about Choir priorities if a player notices.
