The Faithful Husk

CREATURE · Choir Thralls

What's left of a [[drowned_choir]] pilgrim who reached [[the_faithfuls_end]] and kept going past the point where faith should have stopped them. Where [[choir_thrall]] describes ordinary zealots bound to service in the Drowned Districts, a faithful husk went further and closer than any sanctioned rite prepares for, and came back changed rather than devoted.

It still moves like a pilgrim mid-hymn — the same reverent, unhurried motion — which makes the moment it turns hostile worse, not better.

What the Party Might Notice

  • It hums fragments of hymns from [[the_drowned_hymnal]], slightly wrong, as if the melody is being remembered rather than sung (Perception DC 13).
  • Its devotional posture and gear are intact and clearly cared-for, in contrast to the violence it's capable of (Investigation DC 14).
  • Unlike a standard choir thrall, it shows no fear response and does not flee even when clearly outmatched (no check needed, observable in combat).

Actions

Multiattack. The faithful husk makes two Devotional Blade attacks.

Devotional Blade. Melee Weapon Attack: +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. Hit: 6 (1d8+2) slashing damage.

Drowning Grasp. Melee Weapon Attack: +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one creature. Hit: 11 (1d6+2 plus 3d6) necrotic damage, and the target's hit point maximum is reduced by an amount equal to the necrotic damage taken. The target dies if this reduces its hit point maximum to 0. A humanoid slain this way rises as a [[choir_thrall]] in its service in 24 hours, unless the body is destroyed or receives a proper hymn-rite.

DM Notes

Reskin as a stronger variant of [[choir_thrall]] — comparable base creature, higher CR appropriate to a level 9-10 encounter, no morale/flee behavior. Drowning Grasp's create-a-thrall clause (reskinned Wight Life Drain) is a deliberate mechanical echo of [[choir_thrall]]'s own origin — a party that loses someone to this attack and doesn't intervene is watching the exact process that made the husk in the first place. Play the tragedy angle from [[the_faithfuls_end]]'s DM notes through to the fight itself: this was a person, and the fight should read as sad rather than triumphant. Not every faithful husk needs to be fought — a party that recognizes what happened and chooses to leave them be, or attempts something merciful, should find that's a legitimate option too.
