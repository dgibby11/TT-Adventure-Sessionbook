Diving the Salt Below — A DM Reference

REFERENCE · Procedures

Every dive site in this campaign — reef wreck, drowned district, or capital ruin — quietly assumes the party can survive long enough underwater to reach it, work it, and get back. Nothing else in the campaign states how that actually works session to session. This entity is that missing procedure: what a routine licensed dive looks like, what the two pieces of standard gear cover between them, and what a DM should do when one of them fails mid-dive. It does not require retrofitting any existing location — establish it once here, at the gear level, and it applies everywhere.

The Two-Gear Baseline

Two pieces of gear, sold as a standard pair at [[dive_outfitters_row]] and functionally mandatory for any licensed dive, solve two separate problems and fail in two separate ways on purpose — better DM-usable tension than one gear check standing in for "can this diver survive."

  • [[pressure_ward_token]] — solves crushing depth pressure. Fails silently: a fake or lapsed token gives no warning at all before it stops working. The danger is not noticing something is wrong until it's too late.
  • [[brinelung_mask]] — solves breathing underwater. Fails loudly: a spent or fouled reservoir hisses and gurgles for roughly ten minutes before it actually runs dry. The danger is not respecting a warning that's plainly there.

A diver wearing both, in good repair, can work indefinitely at any depth this campaign features. A diver missing either one is one bad roll away from a genuine emergency, which is the point — gear checks before a dive should feel like real prep, not paperwork.

Dive Duration & Depth Budget

Don't track air or pressure by the minute at the table — it's busywork that doesn't serve the campaign's boomtown-to-dread tone. Instead, treat every dive as having a rough, table-scalable budget the DM can spend down narratively:

  • The Shallows (levels 1-4): a full working day per Brinelung charge is normal; the region's danger is what's down there, not the clock. Use the clock only as flavor ("your reservoir's due for a refill by the time you surface").
  • The Drowned Districts (levels 4-7): start treating the return trip as a real decision. A crew that pushes past a single Brinelung charge without a planned refill point is choosing to gamble on the ten-minute warning rather than avoid it.
  • The Deep Kingdom (levels 7-10): the budget becomes a genuine pressure tool. No licensed refill points exist this deep — a party diving past [[the_sealed_approach]] is committing to whatever charge they're carrying in. This is a deliberate, tellable stake going into the finale, not a new tracked resource.

Never let the clock alone kill a character. It exists to force decisions (turn back, press on, split the party to relay gear) — the ten-minute Brinelung warning is meant to always be enough time to act on if the table is paying attention.

Descent & Prep

A routine licensed dive starts at [[concordat_licensing_house]] (manifest filed, site permit stamped) and gears up at [[dive_outfitters_row]] immediately after — both tokens checked, both charges topped off, cheap fakes and fouled reservoirs the two things a careful crew tests before ever entering the water. Gray-market or [[freedivers]]-brokered dives skip the licensing step but never skip the gear check; going down without both pieces working is the kind of risk only a desperate or reckless crew takes.

The Return Trip

Treat the trip back as the mirror of the descent, not an afterthought — this is where a dive that went fine can still go wrong. A crew returning past its planned refill point, carrying salvage that slows them down, or fleeing something they shouldn't have disturbed should feel the Brinelung's ten-minute warning as a real countdown, not a rules footnote. This is the natural place to spend a "gear failure" complication if a scene needs one.

When Gear Fails Mid-Dive

  • Pressure-Ward Token fails (silent): the wearer takes crushing damage at the DM's discretion (scale to depth and party level) starting the round after it lapses, with no warning beforehand. This should be rare and dramatic — a fake token bought from the wrong stall, or one that's simply run its course unnoticed.
  • Brinelung Mask fails (loud): the ten-minute hiss gives the party a real window to surface, reach a refill point, or share a working mask. If the charge actually runs out, the wearer begins suffocating under standard drowning rules. This should read as a consequence of a choice the table made (pushed too far, ignored the warning), not a surprise.
  • Both failure modes are meant to be survivable with attention and bad luck to actually cost a life — use them to raise tension, not as instant-death traps.
