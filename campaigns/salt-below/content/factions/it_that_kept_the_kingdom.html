It That Kept the Kingdom

FACTION · The Deep Presence

Not a political faction — a presence. Three centuries ago, something was bound beneath the old kingdom rather than destroyed, and the kingdom itself was the price of the binding: it drowned so that whatever this is could be kept, not so that it could be killed. It has agents, wants, and something like a plan for the surface, but almost none of that should ever be visible directly. Port Calder should feel its weight the way a coastal town feels a coming storm — through pressure, omens, and things that wash up wrong — long before anything resembling a confrontation is possible.

It is patient in a way nothing mortal is patient. Centuries of near-total containment have taught it that force fails and erosion works: a rung bell out of rhythm, a salvage crew that comes back changed, a cultist who believes she's freeing something wronged. It does not need to break the deal that keeps it down. It only needs someone on the surface to break it for it.

What Reaches the Surface

  • The Drowned Choir — see [[drowned_choir]]. Its most effective agents are the ones who don't know they're agents; the Choir's sincerity is the leverage.
  • [[the_drowned_bell]] — cast as part of the original binding-working; ringing it correctly can be felt below, and something down there has recently started testing it.
  • "Still-breathing" salvage — the occasional relic or find hauled up from deep sites that isn't inert the way it should be. Best used as rare, escalating incidents rather than a running mechanic.

In Play

Should almost never speak, appear, or act directly before the Deep Kingdom chapters (levels 7-10). Its presence is a tone, not a scene: dread that thickens with depth, never a villain monologue. When it must act on the surface, it should act through proxies who have their own real motives — the Choir's faith, the Concordat's greed, a salvage crew's desperation — so the party is never fighting "it," only the damage it causes secondhand. Direct contact belongs to the climactic dungeon (see [[why_the_kingdom_drowned_mystery]] and [[the_breaking_deal_mystery]] for how the party's final choice about it should resolve).

DM Notes

See [[why_the_kingdom_drowned_mystery]] and [[the_breaking_deal_mystery]] for the full truth and how to seed it. In short: the kingdom's own rulers struck the binding as a last resort when they could not destroy what they'd found beneath their own capital, and the "drowning" was the mechanism of containment, not a byproduct of it. The deal is degrading — not because it is inherently unstable, but because the accumulating weight of salvage (relics, the bell, the Choir's rituals, the Concordat's dives) is slowly reintroducing exactly the conditions that let it act. Every faction in Port Calder is unknowingly contributing to the same countdown from a different, self-interested direction.
