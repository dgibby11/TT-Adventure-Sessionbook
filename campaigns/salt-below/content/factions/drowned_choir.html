The Drowned Choir

FACTION · Cults & Deep Powers

A dockside faith that reveres the sunken kingdom as a lost paradise rather than a warning — and believes, with real conviction, that it can be raised again. To outsiders the Choir looks like any of Port Calder's stranger dockside sects: candle-processions to [[the_drowned_bell]], old-tongue chants half the city can't translate, converts who talk a little too fondly about what's under the water. What makes the Choir different is that they're not entirely wrong.

The old kingdom didn't simply sink by accident, and something was bound rather than destroyed when it went under. The Choir has pieced together fragments of that truth across generations of half-remembered ritual and salvaged old-kingdom text — they just believe the right response is to undo the binding, not maintain it. Whether "raising the kingdom" and "freeing what's kept beneath it" are the same act is a question the Choir itself doesn't fully understand.

Notable Members

  • [[isolt_vane]] — the Choirmother; leads by conviction, not force.
  • [[deacon_rill]] — recruits at the docks, believes without a shadow of doubt.
  • [[sister_meck]] — newer to real doubt than to the faith itself.

In Play

Should read as unsettling rather than villainous on first contact — a faction whose goals sometimes run parallel to the party's (both want to understand what's actually down there) and sometimes directly against them (the Choir wants to loosen what the party may need to keep bound). Avoid making them simple to dismiss as "the bad guys"; the best Choir scenes leave the party genuinely unsure whether a given member should be opposed, ignored, or listened to.

DM Notes

The Choir's core belief — that the kingdom drowned as an imprisonment rather than a disaster — is the closest anyone in Port Calder has gotten to the truth about [[it_that_kept_the_kingdom]] (see [[why_the_kingdom_drowned_mystery]]), which is exactly why they should feel more right the deeper the party goes, not less. Their fatal error is treating containment as cruelty rather than necessity. Individual members can be written across a wide sincerity spectrum — Isolt genuinely believes she's liberating something wronged; Rill wants the certainty faith gives him more than he understands what he's asking for; Meck is the party's most likely wedge into the Choir, being the one member closest to realizing the "kingdom" and "the thing underneath it" might not want the same outcome.
