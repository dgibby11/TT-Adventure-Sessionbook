Why Did the Kingdom Really Drown?

MYSTERY · Central Mystery

Every Port Calder schoolchild knows the old kingdom sank three centuries ago. Fewer ask why an entire capital would go under all at once, on a coastline that hasn't moved since. Fewer still have ever gotten close enough to an answer to regret asking.

Clues

  • [[the_sunken_rostrum]]: A weathered inscription references a final address given from the rostrum — a sovereign's decree, not a plea for rescue (History DC 14).
  • [[the_leaning_archive]]: A surviving civic decree references sealing "the lower wards" on the sovereign's own order, days before the water came — an evacuation order, not an accident (History DC 15).
  • [[the_chancellors_rest]]: The chancellor's sealed private papers implicate the crown's own choices in what happened, not just misfortune — a strong player-found-lore site best run as a handout or DM-read excerpt.
  • [[mireths_reckoning]]: The writing along the far wall shifts tone partway through — early passages read as dutiful reports, later ones as something closer to a private confession (Investigation DC 14 to place the shift precisely).
  • [[drowned_chancellery]]: The archive seals bear a chancellor's formal seal-mark, matching the one on [[the_chancellors_rest]]'s door exactly — the two sites corroborate each other (Investigation DC 15).
  • [[the_last_accord]]: States outright, in formal old-tongue legal phrasing, that the kingdom sank itself deliberately as the price of containment — the definitive confirmation, once the party can reach and translate it (Investigation or Religion DC 14).

The Truth

The kingdom's own rulers drowned it on purpose. Something was uncovered beneath the old capital — [[it_that_kept_the_kingdom]] — that could not be destroyed by any means the kingdom's mages and priests could bring to bear. Containment was the only option that worked, and the working they found required scale: the whole city, sunk and sealed, became the vessel of the binding rather than just its site. The "drowning" was the casting of the spell, not a disaster that interrupted one.

Almost nothing of this survives as clean historical record — the kingdom's collapse erased most of the people who understood what they were doing, on purpose, as part of the working itself. What's left is fragments: old-tongue inscriptions like the ones on [[the_drowned_bell]], half-understood ritual passed down through [[drowned_choir]] tradition, and salvage that behaves like it remembers being part of something larger.

Seeding This

Never state this outright to players even via NPCs — it should be assembled the way real mysteries are, from inscriptions, salvage, and Choir testimony that individually read as legend and only add up in the aggregate. [[isolt_vane]] has pieced together more of this than she fully understands the implications of; she's a good pressure valve for partial reveals in the mid-game. Reserve full confirmation for the Deep Kingdom chapters.
