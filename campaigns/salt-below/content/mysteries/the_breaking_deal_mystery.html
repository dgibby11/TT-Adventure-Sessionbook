How Much Longer Does the Deal Hold?

MYSTERY · Central Mystery

Whatever kept the old kingdom's binding intact for three centuries is not holding as well as it used to. Something rang [[the_drowned_bell]] out of rhythm with nobody on duty. Salvage crews are hauling up finds that don't behave like inert relics should. The question isn't whether the deal breaks — it's how much time Port Calder has before someone breaks it for good, and whether that someone already has.

The Truth

The binding was never meant to survive centuries of active salvage on top of it. Every dive, every relic hauled to the surface, every [[drowned_choir]] ritual performed at [[the_drowned_bell]] chips at the same containment, each for its own self-interested reason: the [[salvage_concordat]] wants the boom to keep booming and has been quietly burying "irregular" dive reports rather than investigating them; the Choir wants the kingdom "freed" and reads every tremor as encouragement; ordinary greed sends crews past sites they've been warned off. [[it_that_kept_the_kingdom]] does not need to force the deal open — the surface is doing it by degrees, and it is patient enough to let that happen.

There is no fixed doomsday clock — this should scale to the pace of the table. Treat depth as the real clock instead: shallow-dive incidents (levels 1-4) are cosmetic and deniable, mid-depth Drowned Districts incidents (levels 4-7) are undeniable but still survivable, and by the Deep Kingdom (levels 7-10) the deal is close enough to failing that the party's choice at the climactic dungeon — destroy the binding, renew it, or find a third option — determines whether it fails on their watch or not.

Seeding This

Escalate incidents gradually and let multiple factions' self-interest visibly contribute rather than pinning it on one villain. The [[harbormasters_post|harbormaster]]'s private tide logs and the Concordat's suppressed reports are both good sources for the party to notice the pattern before any NPC says it outright.
